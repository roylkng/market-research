# NEWGEN: a durable workflow opportunity whose cash return still needs a better entry

**Decision at the original NSE 20 November 2026 EQ close of ₹413.55: WATCH_PRICE_AND_COLLECTIONS.** This completes a priced review. It is not an unconditional purchase recommendation and does not alter any earlier company decision or experiment.

The attractive feature is ownership of software embedded in regulated customer workflows. The immediate constraint is the combination of slow total growth, rising same-season debtor days and a valuation of about **28.7 times actual trailing cash after all capital expenditure and leases**, before a separate treasury credit. A lower share quote does not establish a cheaper business. The current quote is about 18.5 times trailing reported profit per legal issued share, which contains treasury income and accounting effects.

The five-year funded base scenario produces **8.3% dividend-inclusive annual return**, with a downside of **−7.1%** and an upside of **18.9%**. These are conditional scenarios, not probabilities, target prices or measured investment skill. The price route to a more attractive case is approximately **₹325–350**, provided the operating thesis has not deteriorated. At ₹350, the same base assumptions produce about 12.0%. That case-specific comparison is not a universal return hurdle.

## What the business could own over the next decade

Newgen sells a workflow, document and communication platform, with industry-specific applications in banking, insurance, government and other enterprises. The long-term need is the auditable execution of complex work across old systems, documents, people and increasingly AI agents. A customer may replace an AI model frequently while retaining the workflow definitions, integrations, security controls and operating records around it. That is the defensible part of the opportunity. It is an inference from the product architecture and customer use cases, not proof that every existing customer will renew. [S2, S3]

The latest quarter gives useful evidence of continuity. Annuity streams reached about ₹268cr and grew about 15%, while total revenue grew only 5.4% to ₹422.24cr. SaaS grew about 36%, but SaaS was only about 15% of quarterly revenue. Support and annual maintenance remain substantial parts of recurring revenue. Calling the whole company a 36% SaaS grower would misstate the economics. Nine new enterprise customers and ten AI deal bookings in the first half are adoption signals. They do not establish the size, margin or cash yield of AI revenue. [S2, S3]

The positive counter-case deserves preservation. A transition from upfront licenses to subscription revenue can temporarily depress reported growth while improving lifetime customer economics. US and EMEA quarterly revenue grew about 17% and 19%, respectively. AI add-ons could increase what customers buy from the same platform, and a product business can reuse engineering across clients. Management said AI components can increase deal sizes, but it did not isolate realized AI revenue or gross profit. The favorable scenario below tests this outcome explicitly. It is not silently built into the base case. [S2, S3]

The competing explanation is less favorable. Implementation delays and slower bank decisions can reduce license conversion. AI can make implementation and support cheaper for competitors as well as Newgen. Customers may demand lower prices, fewer paid seats or more functionality at the old price. The current call explains domain workflows and auditability but does not provide a quantified answer on who retains productivity savings. Switching friction supports customer retention. It does not automatically establish pricing power.

The cash residual is an observed diagnostic. It is neither all immediately distributable nor a claim of permanent normalized cash. It includes the year's actual tax and collection timing. It excludes portfolio turnover, employee option exercise proceeds and asset-sale proceeds. Trailing CFO is 78.5% of PAT. The residual is about **₹14.41 per opening fully diluted share** in the model.

Three adjustments materially change the interpretation of profit.

1. FY26 recorded **₹30.06cr labour-code expense and ₹13.37cr legal expense**. The legal amount follows a final adverse Qatar court judgment involving a bank implementation, with refund of fees and legal costs. It is not an optional provision to dismiss as irrelevant. The model separates recurring earning capacity from these past charges while retaining all September provisions as a treasury reserve. It does not add back their cash cost to the historical residual. [S1, PDF pages 310–314]
2. FY26 consolidated share-based compensation was a **₹4.53cr credit**, compared with a **₹25.39cr expense** in FY25. This approximately ₹29.93cr change improved the year-on-year cost comparison. RSU disclosures identify forfeitures including non-achievement of targets. A recurring credit is not assumed. The model adds back SBC once to calculate cash EBIT, which means subtracting a historical credit. It then charges ownership through fully diluted opening shares and future dilution. It does not also deduct a second full SBC cash cost. [S1, PDF pages 277, 309 and the share-plan annexures]
3. TTM other income was **₹72.11cr**. The forecast strips this from operating earnings and models treasury income separately. Valuing the operating business on profit that includes treasury earnings and then adding all the treasury would overstate value. [S1, S4]

### Seasonality does not explain away the deterioration

Net trade receivables rose from ₹556.68cr in March 2025 to ₹708.24cr in March 2026, against only 5.9% annual revenue growth. They declined to ₹634.95cr by September, but the issuer's same-season debtor-day comparison is **145 days versus 124 days** a year earlier. March's 164 days falling to 145 is not evidence that the problem is repaired. Management attributes part of the delay to Middle East collections. The scenario model retains a working-capital burden and does not assume a return to the old 120-day pattern. [S1, S2, S3]

The FY26 annual report's receivable allowance is material. Trade receivables are generally contracted at 15–90 days, and older buckets grew. The report also includes the Qatar delivery dispute described above. Customer dependence is broader than one large client, since no single FY26 customer exceeded 10% of revenue, but geography, banking exposure and implementation execution remain meaningful concentrations. [S1, PDF pages 298–300 and 338]

### Software capitalization and treasury ownership

FY26 intangible additions were ₹2.60cr and the September carrying value of other intangibles is only **₹2.43cr**. The issuer says it usually invests about 10% of revenue in R&D, but that is a management description rather than a separately audited cash R&D schedule. The small intangible asset balance and the explicit capex deduction give no basis to claim that a large volume of product spending is being hidden in capitalized software. R&D remains inside forecast operating costs. [S1, PDF pages 76 and 296, S4]

The NSE iXBRL maps ₹75.03cr to other intangibles. The signed balance sheet separates **₹72.60cr right-of-use assets and ₹2.43cr other intangibles**. The signed original controls the analysis. A machine field would have falsely suggested a large software acquisition or capitalization jump. This correction is retained in the source manifest. [S4, S5]

The September current cash, bank balances and investments total ₹1,182.09cr. The base model excludes all ₹109.93cr of noncurrent financial assets rather than presuming those deposits are freely available. FY26 notes identify margin deposits and other restrictions. It also reserves all September provisions of ₹104.07cr, unclaimed dividends, a ₹30cr investment and realization haircut and ₹163.19cr operating liquidity. **Opening available treasury in the model is about ₹884.70cr**, not the entire headline pool. Its 3% net yield and retained balance are counted once. [S1, S4]

This is a valuation reserve, not a forecast special dividend. The portfolio includes some equity and hybrid mutual funds, so book value is not the same as risk-free cash. Leases are paid in future cash flows, so the opening lease liability is not deducted again. The October proposed merger concerns a wholly owned subsidiary and explicitly creates neither new shares nor cash consideration. No acquisition benefit or additional acquisition spending is invented. [S1, S4]

## Ownership and management evidence

The May allotment and September shareholding statement reconcile to **142,510,002 legal issued shares**. Employee trusts held 997,187 shares. The shareholding statement reports 144,763,743 fully diluted shares, which is the model's opening denominator. Annual dilution is a separate 0.5% base assumption for future awards. Existing share claims are counted once, and exercise proceeds are not credited. The 2024 bonus is already reflected in these current counts and the ₹10 face value. No new split or bonus is inferred from the nominal share price. [S6, S7]

Execution continuity is a real current issue. Tarun Nandwani, formerly COO, was appointed CEO from 1 August 2026 to 31 January 2028 following Virender Jeet's resignation. The company also appointed a chief growth officer and designated T. S. Varadarajan vice chairman. The 23 October filing says the chairman and managing director remains unavailable for regular duties and that operational continuity arrangements are in place. The internal succession is evidence of team depth. Its commercial outcome has not yet been established. No inference is made about anyone's medical condition. [S8, S9, S10]

Promoter control was about 53.45% of legal shares in September and the declaration records no promoter pledges or other encumbrances. That supports alignment and financing stability but is not a complete integrity verdict. FY26 key management compensation and stock-related amounts are disclosed. They are already reflected through costs and ownership, not deducted again as an invented governance charge. The final customer legal loss, weak cash conversion and target-linked RSU forfeitures provide more useful management tests than awards or biographies. [S1, S7]

## Five-year funded cash, not just an EPS extrapolation

Every scenario funds revenue growth through operating working capital, all capex and an operating liquidity buffer. All cash lease payments remain deducted. Treasury income is outside the operating exit multiple. Dividends reduce retained cash. Any cash shortfall would consume opening available treasury and appear as an external funding need if that pool were exhausted. None of these three scenarios requires external funding.

## What changes the decision

**Price route:** around ₹325–350, the unchanged base case becomes a conditional purchase candidate for an investor able to bear this business risk. Recheck that debtor days and credit-loss charges have not materially worsened, no new large delivery loss has emerged and share claims remain close to the modeled path. This route does not require perfect AI attribution or a return to the old DSO. It accepts the ordinary uncertainties already priced into the scenario.

**Operating-evidence route near ₹413.55:** demonstrate company revenue growth moving toward the low to mid teens, stable cash operating margins, and higher cash per diluted share. A concrete re-underwriting trigger is about ₹260cr of trailing cash after all capex and lease cash, with same-season DSO moving toward 130–135 days and no offsetting rise in unbilled assets. Evidence that larger AI deals produce paid gross profit would help explain the mechanism. A better quarter alone is insufficient to annualize a new run rate.

**Adverse route:** continued same-season DSO above 145, new material collection losses, support revenue pressure, or dilution materially above the assumed path lowers value even if the share quote falls. A cheap-looking multiple should not override that change.

## Historical lesson and limits

FY26 provides a useful observed adverse pattern: recurring revenue growth and AI investment coexisted with only 5.9% total revenue growth, rising receivables and a favorable SBC expense comparison. That is a warning against inferring a compounding stock from a compounding product category. The competing explanation is an orderly subscription transition plus temporary collection delays. The early discriminator is cash per share with same-season debtor and unbilled balances, not an award count or a large contract's face value. This is a retrospective mechanism check, not a blind historical buy simulation, and no historical investment return is invented.
