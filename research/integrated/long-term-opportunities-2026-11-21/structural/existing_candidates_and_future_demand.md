# Long-term demand and the existing investment candidates

Review date: 21 November 2026, India time. Official price observation: 20 November NSE EQ close. This is a new structural assessment. It preserves the dated 20 November funded valuation models and does not silently rerun them at new quotes.

## Decision

Add a 5–10 year structural lens to the existing price and cash work. A durable opportunity needs demand, a defensible share of customer spending, good incremental returns on capital and growing cash per diluted share. A large market or a low historical cash multiple cannot establish all four.

The new deep cases are CAMS, APAR and Vijaya. Their separate notes distinguish historical facts, issuer claims, analyst inference and reverse-valuation assumptions. Water reuse and AI-enabled workflows are secondary opportunity areas. They have credible demand mechanisms but do not yet produce a new fully funded stock valuation in this increment.

## 1. CMS: rising currency stock is not the same as rising ATM service activity

**Facts.** RBI's FY2025–26 annual economic review reports currency in circulation growth of 11.4%, versus 5.8% a year earlier. Its September 2026 payment-system table separately reports ATM cash-withdrawal volume of 4,006.59 lakh, versus 4,395.48 lakh in September 2025, and value of ₹220,378cr, versus ₹230,952cr. The year-over-year changes are about −8.85% in volume and −4.58% in value. The annual currency-stock comparison and the monthly ATM-flow comparison cover different periods and measures. They are not contradictory and one is not a reconciliation of the other. [S1, S2]

**Inference.** Cash can remain economically important while parts of the delivery network face lower activity, consolidation or pricing pressure. Currency growth is insufficient evidence for a decade of CMS revenue growth. A shrinking market is also not an automatic rejection when outsourcing, share gains, service mix, route density or pricing improve company economics.

**Investment implication.** Retain CMS as a concrete cash-normalization and adaptation candidate. Do not relabel it a secular compounder without evidence from collected service margins and reinvestment returns. The prior ₹200cr versus ₹250cr recurring investment sensitivities remain useful. The next evidence should connect contract pricing, cash collections, ATM and non-ATM gross profit, receivable aging and recurring cash investment. It need not wait for an arbitrary number of quarters. This structural finding does not mechanically overturn the 20 November current-price conditional judgment.

Official 20 November price ₹203.39. The preceding funded model used ₹204.60 on 19 November. No revised model return is claimed here.

## 2. AI: who keeps the productivity benefit?

**Facts and issuer claims.** In R Systems' 4 November call, filed on 10 November, management describes predominantly project-driven work and customer productivity pressure. Management says new project proposals use AI-enabled engineering and reusable agents to offer clients approximately twice, and sometimes more than twice, the productivity. These are management claims about delivery, not independently measured profitability or contracted recurring revenue. [S3]

**Inference.** A vendor can deliver more output with fewer hours while earning less on the same scope. Alternatively, cheaper and faster work can expand client demand and allow profitable fixed-price or outcome-based contracts. The correct investment question is the net effect on company revenue, gross profit and cash per share after talent costs, software investment, sales costs, acquisitions and equity compensation.

**Investment implication.** R Systems remains a priority conditional candidate under the prior funded five-year analysis. The structural case requires organic constant-currency growth, retained customer economics, revenue and gross profit relative to delivery headcount, and cash per diluted share. Acquisition growth and foreign-exchange margin gains are not substitutes for this evidence. Price alone can still improve prospective return at unchanged business assumptions.

eClerx's October call also reports demand for analytics and automation, alongside deal-win variability, customer concentration and utilization pressure. Analytics and automation revenue must not be relabeled entirely as generative-AI revenue. The full 20 November eClerx priced assessment is already complete and remains part of the retained comparison. [S4]

Official 20 November prices: R Systems ₹241.30 and eClerx ₹1,454.10. Their prior models used ₹242.50 and ₹1,468.80. These small quote changes alone do not resolve the operating questions.

## 3. eClerx original notice: a material clarification, not a new 18% expense

The original 17 November postal-ballot PDF was captured in this session, SHA-256 `ca226a6d78d87e323d4d91d32551d12977ca944fb43bc3b5d91c78f71c48a7fa`. The relevant explanatory statement was text-read and visually checked on physical PDF pages 12 and 13, printed pages 11 and 12. This closes the original-notice retrieval limitation in the 20 November note. [S5]

The proposal permits CEO aggregate remuneration of up to 18% of Section 198 profits in relevant option-exercise years during the remaining appointment through 24 May 2028. It also proposes increasing the overall managerial ceiling from 11% to 20%. This is a shareholder proposal, not an approved recurring expense. Voting closes 17 December 2026 and results are expected by 18 December.

The explanation identifies **1,180,500 already granted options**, adjusted for the bonus issue:

| Grant year | Options | Grant price | Stated vesting |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| FY2023–24 | 450,000 | ₹709.91 | May 2026, already vested |
| FY2024–25 | 450,000 | ₹1,151.23 | May 2027 |
| FY2025–26 | 150,000 | ₹1,234.73 | May 2028 |
| FY2026–27 | 130,500 | ₹1,549.21 | May 2029 |

The issuer states the options were granted at fair market value without a discount, approximately half have performance-based vesting, remuneration beyond the normal 5% limit would arise solely from option-exercise perquisites, and this ceiling proposal entails no additional company cash outflow. Those statements concern the proposal and the described grants. They do not mean that all employee equity compensation or trust purchases have no economic cost.

**Revised interpretation.** Do not deduct 18% of profit as a new annual cash charge or add this ceiling again on top of the same existing options. Reconcile the existing grants, performance conditions, trust-funded shares, exercise proceeds and share count once. The expanded ceiling alone is not evidence of misconduct. It narrows the prior uncertainty and should not become an indefinite veto. The existing current-price pass continues to rest mainly on valuation and supported growth, not a fictitious 18% cash drain. A future priced review should use these actual terms.

## 4. Water reuse: recurring need needs a paying customer

**Facts.** On 25 October 2026, the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs announced a Used Water Marketplace under AMRUT 2.0. It connects local bodies offering treated water with potential users and investors. The platform lists quantity and quality. This is a market-enabling policy action, not evidence that sales or payments have occurred. [S6]

The August 2026 Eleventh Report of the Standing Committee on Housing and Urban Affairs records an April 2026 government response that 11 states had notified wastewater-reuse policies. This report is the action-taken review concerning urban drinking water. It is **not** the separate sewerage/septage report. Figures from a search result summarizing the latter are deliberately excluded because this original does not verify them. [S7]

**Opportunity mechanism.** Water scarcity, industrial process requirements and discharge limits can make treatment, recycling and reliable operations recurring customer needs. The more attractive economic structures may combine contracted industrial offtake, funded construction, measured plant performance and paid operations over the asset life.

**Failure mechanism.** A project can be technically necessary and still be unattractive to shareholders if the payer delays cash, the company finances construction for too long, concession obligations absorb the apparent profit, or competitive bidding prices away the return.

**Existing candidate relevance.** WABAG remains the relevant existing research lead. Its prior contract and cancellation analysis in PR #619 remains separate. No new WABAG target price or reversal is made in this increment. Track funded orders, customer credit, cash milestones, operating collections, guarantees and incremental capital returns. A policy announcement or order book is not enough.

## 5. How the older candidates fit the longer horizon

| Candidate | Long-term role to investigate | Evidence that would support the case | What would weaken it |
|---|---|---|---|
| CMS | Cash-services adaptation and cash normalization | Collected margins and recurring cash investment supporting per-share distributions | Lower activity plus adverse pricing, rising receivables or repeated customer funding |
| R Systems | AI-enabled engineering and workflow transformation | Organic growth, better client economics and cash/share after equity and acquisition costs | Productivity benefits passed to clients without enough new demand |

| Epigral | Funded materials reinvestment | New capacity earns acceptable cash returns through a normal cycle | Industry supply growth, weak spreads or debt/dilution outrunning earnings |

These are analyst classifications. The older company case files hold the underlying detailed evidence. This table does not claim a new full audit of every issuer or a same-date revaluation of the seven companies.

## Confidence and horizon

The additions improve the definition of what must happen. They do not establish investment prediction accuracy. The retained historical replay remains a retrospective reconstruction, and the frozen selected-cohort advantage remains unproven. Neither a longer story nor additional extraction checks count as a successful backtest.

Use a nested horizon: the next available disclosure tests the immediate economic mechanism, the following 1–3 years test repeatability and reinvestment, and the 5–10 year thesis tests whether competition and capital allocation allow that advantage to persist. These are observation horizons, not compulsory waiting periods before a recommendation.
