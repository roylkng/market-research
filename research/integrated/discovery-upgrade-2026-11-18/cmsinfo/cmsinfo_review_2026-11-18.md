# CMS Info Systems: cash conversion must support the valuation

Decision date: 18 November 2026. Reference price: official NSE EQ close ₹205.35 on 18 November. Consolidated figures below are in ₹ crore. Research disposition: **WATCH_EVIDENCE**, with a specific cash-spend and collection bridge required. This is a preliminary original-document review, not a complete capital-ready company underwriting.

## Decision

CMS is a credible fresh research candidate at about 10.9 times trailing reported profit. The move toward fixed-fee services and technology deserves attention. The purchase thesis at this price depends on cash conversion recovering after investment, rather than on the headline earnings multiple alone. That recovery is plausible but has not been adequately reconciled.

The strongest counterargument to waiting is already visible: trailing cash after selected investment, lease and interest payments is positive ₹206.73cr, despite a negative latest half. It would be wrong to annualize the negative half and call the business permanently cash-consuming. It would also be wrong to count management's lower capex definition as if it replaced the cash actually spent.

At the reference price, last reported shares imply ₹3,286.89cr market value. A five-year illustrative valuation using a 12-times final-year owner-cash multiple requires initial recurring owner cash of approximately **₹256cr growing 5% annually for a 12% return**, or **₹227cr growing 8%**. Those are assumptions and reverse calculations, not approved return hurdles or forecasts. The current ₹206.73cr trailing cash measure is not yet certified sustainable owner cash. If a defensible cash bridge supports those levels without fresh equity, debt or omitted acquisition funding, this case can reopen at the same price. A price fall is not the only route to a positive decision.

### The capex issue is specific

Management guided to FY2027 capex of ₹100–125cr and described H1 net capex of about ₹50cr. The signed H1 cash-flow statement shows ₹149.442cr cash purchases of PPE/intangible assets. In the 14 November call, management attributed about ₹50cr to an acquisition-related payment and ₹50–54cr to CWIP moving into capitalized assets. The transcript names Securens both as an asset purchase and as the separate prior-year share acquisition. [S1, S3, S4]

A transfer from construction in progress into completed assets is not, by itself, a reduction in cash paid. The response therefore does not yet reconcile reported cash spending with the lower recurring-capex figure. The annual report separately identifies Securens as a share acquisition, with ₹48.556cr total purchase consideration including non-compete and contingent amounts. The FY2026 cash-flow statement separately reports ₹46.266cr acquisition cash. This is a disclosure/classification question, not a finding of misconduct. Do not silently substitute FSS for Securens or remove ₹100cr from cash investment based on a guessed transcription error. [S2, S4]

The next decisive item is a schedule connecting cash paid, acquired assets/businesses, capital creditors, CWIP opening/additions/transfers and recurring maintenance/growth expenditure. Until then, the statutory cash line remains the valuation input, with alternative normalization shown separately.

### Collections and treasury also matter

September receivables were ₹970.851cr versus ₹892.201cr in March. Receivables absorbed ₹100.962cr of H1 cash. Management describes H2 collections as seasonally stronger and reported year-on-year improvement in DSO. Those are issuer claims, not confirmation that older balances will be collected. [S1, S4]

H1 bad-debt/receivable provisions and write-offs were ₹22.312cr and ATM shortage/claims provisions were ₹22.369cr. These costs are part of the business economics. They are not an automatic add-back to normalized profit. Equally, do not subtract them a second time from reported operating cash without reconciling the gross working-capital and provision movements. [S1]

The presentation's ₹473cr net-cash figure has a broader definition than immediately available bank cash. Cash equivalents, current investments, noncurrent investments and other bank balances sum to ₹341.323cr in the statutory balance sheet, before distinguishing restrictions. Loans and other financial assets cannot automatically be called unrestricted cash. No separate ₹473cr cash addition is used in the reverse valuation. Lease liabilities of ₹267.405cr remain relevant, but deducting their full balance again after charging lease payments would require a consistent enterprise-to-equity model. [S1, S3]

## Reopening and invalidation

**Reopen at the same price** if original evidence supports recurring owner cash near ₹256cr with approximately 5% funded growth, under the illustrative 12%/12-times scenario, and the source bridge removes the double-counting risk. Also examine the more conservative/no-growth scenarios in the calculation file. These are research conditions, not automatic order rules.

**Weaken or close the thesis** if recurring cash investment remains materially above the low-capex narrative without commensurate contract returns, older receivables fail to convert, cash shortage/claims costs remain structurally high, or growth needs further capital that the owner-cash forecast omitted.

The 18 November transcript was filed after the closing-price reference. Its underlying call occurred on 14 November and an earlier audio recording may have been available. The case does not assume all transcript information was new to the market on 18 November.
