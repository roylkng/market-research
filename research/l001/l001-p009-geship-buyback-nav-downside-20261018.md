## Economic asymmetry, deliberately cross-dated

Using the June 30 **reported** consolidated NAV and the October 15 close, at the old basic share count (no legal buyback adjustment):
- Historical basic capitalization **~₹21,188 crore**.
- **21.31% discount** to the dated issuer-reported NAV per share. Equivalently, NAV is **27.08% above** the historical share price, which is not a forecast price appreciation.
- June 30 consolidated net cash **~₹564 per historical basic share**. The non-cash residual of the issuer's NAV is **~₹1,322/share**, including fleet/offshore assets and other liabilities.
- 27,08,660 repurchased through Oct 15 is around **1.90% of old basic issued shares**. The separate authorization maximum ₹900cr is around **4.25% of old historical basic market cap**. Repurchased vs extinguished share counts are not interchangeable.

**Do not infer an assured share-price floor at ₹1,530.** That is the maximum company buyback price, not an obligation to buy every offered share. A buyback at a discount to the company's asset NAV may be accretive per remaining share, but consumes balance-sheet cash, changes the denominator, and requires exact cancellation and dividend timing.

The stress ignores inter-period net-cash change, later share cancellation, FX exposure, capital commitments, tax, minority claims and the company's valuation methodology. It is a downside sensitivity only, not a real forecast or investable margin of safety.

### Cyclical return-on-assets risk: highest-ever Q1 is not normalized profit

On the August 4 FY27 Q1 call, CFO said consolidated quarterly profit **₹1,309 crore** and a marked-up consolidated NAV just under ₹1,900. Management specifically attributed tanker market strength to oil trade diversions around the Strait of Hormuz, unusually high vessel day rates and asset-price increases. It also described very large **new tanker order books**: around **27% for crude tankers**, **35% for VLGCs** and **20–21% for product tankers**. Only approximately **25–26%** of its shipping capacity was on time charter, leaving much spot-market freight exposure. These are management-reported numbers from the reproduced call, not audited cycle-normalized projections.

There is a **real tension**:
- Positive: substantial cash, dividends, 2026 open-market repurchases below stale NAV, disciplined fleet replacement (rather than expanding at the top) may create shareholder value.
- Negative: Q1 profits benefited from an exceptional shipping/freight environment, order-book growth risks future vessel oversupply, and the NAV can shrink materially if vessel/rate markets normalize.

A high return on reported capital in Q1 is not reliable evidence of sustainable 1–3-year cash generation.
