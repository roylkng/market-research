## Specific findings that changed the decisions

### PIX: operating quality with a price condition

The business sells mechanical wear products across several industries and export markets. Reported profit is flattered by investment valuation movements. Removing all other income and restoring an investment loss placed in FY26 expenses gives TTM core EBIT of **₹168.35 crore**, about **26.6% of ₹632.77 crore sales**. The funded base includes **₹273.96 crore capital spending and ₹109.98 crore working capital** across five years, plus liquidity and scheduled debt repayment. It does not extrapolate the unusually low latest capital spend.

High-cost related-party debt alongside investments is a capital-allocation concern. Disclosed pay and rent already reduce operating earnings and are not deducted a second time. The base exit 16× NOPAT is about 24.1× year-five funded cash, so the terminal valuation still depends on durable growth and capital productivity.

### POWERGRID: financing the expansion is feasible, but return matters

The issuer's FY28 funding description supports an 80:20 debt/internal-resource mix. The scenario model closes capitalized and cash interest, capex, commissioning, dividends and debt. Base gross debt/book equity peaks near **1.67×**, while the current borrowing authorization would need to increase by model year four. Base ten-year equity return is **7.3% at ₹269**, or **9.9% at ₹220**.

New regulated cost-plus assets and competitively bid projects have different economics. The new TBCB return assumptions are explicit first-year accounting returns on reference equity, not verified lifetime project IRRs. The base is a financing sensitivity, not a portfolio of committed loan and concession contracts. A higher-maintenance-spending stress reduces the base five-/ten-year returns to **5.6% / 6.8%**.

### NEWGEN: recurring growth is useful, cash collection must keep pace

The platform supports customer workflow, records and control needs. Faster SaaS growth applies to only part of revenue. Same-season net debtor days rose from **124 to 145**. TTM cash after all reported capex and lease cash is **₹208.55 crore**. A prior share-compensation credit and a final customer legal loss prevent a clean headline-profit extrapolation.

The signed balance sheet resolves an iXBRL mapping problem: the alleged ₹75.03 crore software/intangible balance actually combines **₹72.60 crore right-of-use assets and ₹2.43 crore other intangibles**. Product development remains an operating cost in the forecast. The model funds working capital and counts existing diluted shares plus future awards once.

### NUCLEUS: cash is meaningful, but the base already needs recovery

The issuer's ₹942.1 crore treasury includes investments and deposits. The model credits about **₹767.23 crore** after provisions, dated restrictions, a haircut and operating liquidity. TTM cash after capex and leases is **−₹35.05 crore**, or **−₹43.51 crore** after employee-trust share purchases.

The working case requires 6% growth and an EBIT-margin path of 8%, 9%, 10%, 11% and 11.5%, while paying a 1.5%-of-sales cash award budget. The latest H1 cash EBIT margin before share awards was only 4.1%, so even the first-year 8% assumption requires recovery. Those are recovery assumptions, not observed achievements. Management's explanation of delayed collections is testable. A treasury-rich company can remain a poor investment if the operating franchise consumes the cushion.

### MSTC: the earlier review missed older accounting evidence

The FY26 annual report already contained CAG observations on ECL policy and **₹14.71 crore** of old-remittance liability writebacks. Management disputed the interpretations, the statutory auditor agreed with management's replies, and a formal FY27 ECL method was promised. The complete case includes both positions. This is a dated correction to our coverage, not a new event or an allegation of fraud.

September gross bank balances of **₹2,271.73 crore** reduce to a cash-only residual of **₹432.48 crore** after recognized financial liabilities, borrowing and trade payables, before other obligations and buffers. The base credits only ₹250 crore own treasury. It separately assumes ₹30 crore after-tax float income, which requires about ₹729 crore average eligible float under the modeled yield/tax. Customer principal is never treated as equity cash.

## Introspection that improves the research

**A cautious error can be as consequential as an optimistic one.** Negative cash after productive investment can exclude a future winner if financing and later returns are ignored. Conversely, treating low interim capex or a temporary creditor balance as recurring owner cash can produce a false bargain. These cases explicitly fund investment and show its return requirements.

**The retained-cash treatment has been checked rather than generalized.** In the earlier MSTC table, the undistributed half had no explicit roll-forward. Under the identical old 10% growth and 15× terminal assumptions, crediting it once increases the illustrative return from **2.91% to 5.41%**, assuming no additional uses. The new funded model has its own assumptions and gives 5.1%. The same targeted check found **no retention omission in eClerx or CAMS**. Their old cases remain unchanged. See [the reproduction](legacy-cash-audit/README.md).

**Original disclosure review must precede a confident cash claim.** MSTC's older CAG comments and NEWGEN's machine-field mapping show why semantic review of annual/interim notes matters. A successful download, a clean calculation or a bigger source count does not establish economic truth.

**No purchase is manufactured to relieve the frustration of waiting.** The current prices still require stronger economics or acceptance of modest working returns. The useful output is a set of priced, falsifiable decisions, with an explicit favorable case that can overturn the cautious verdict. This batch does not establish an unconditional new buy or demonstrated investment edge.
