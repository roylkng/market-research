# KRSNAA Diagnostics: growth is real, but collections and price do not yet justify a fresh purchase

Case reviewed on 10 November 2026. Reference: the official NSE close of **INR 493.60 on 9 November 2026**, not an intraday quote or a post-research execution price.

## Decision

**WAIT. No fresh purchase at the dated reference price under the conservative underwriting case.** This is a price-sensitive research judgment, not a sell instruction for an existing holding or a prediction that the next price move is down.

KRSNAA has a plausible expansion and utilization story. The difficulty is that its reported valuation includes a noncash investment gain, substantial expansion still consumes financing, and government collections are behind revenue recognition. Stronger execution can justify the offered price. It is not established by the received results.

The original funding question is now answered: **the expenditure was not funded by operating cash alone**. Deposit liquidation, borrowing and the upfront warrant receipt supported it. That explains the cash bridge, but does not establish attractive returns on the new capital.

## 1. Valuation starts with recurring per-share earnings, not the reported P/E

Using consolidated additive profit flows, FY26 + H1FY27 minus H1FY26 gives **INR 98.104 crore** of trailing owner-attributable profit. The audited FY26 investment note identifies a gain from remeasuring the Apulki investment after loss of significant influence: **INR 25.952 crore before deferred tax and INR 22.241 crore after deferred tax**. The net figure is disclosed, so a generic 25% tax haircut is not needed. [S1, PDF page 217 / printed 229. S2 and S3.]

| Earnings basis | Owner profit | Reference EPS | P/E at INR 493.60 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Reported trailing | INR 98.10 crore | INR 30.24 | 16.32x |
| Remove only the identified after-tax gain | INR 75.86 crore | INR 23.39 | 21.11x |
| Same profit, full exercise of reference options and warrants | INR 75.86 crore | INR 21.63 | 22.82x |

Reference issued shares are **32,438,672**, explicitly stated in the audited capital note, not inferred from rounded paid-up capital. The full-exercise sensitivity adds 1,005,607 outstanding March ESOP options and 1,621,000 October warrants, producing **35,065,279 shares**, an 8.10% increase. It is not a certified current diluted-EPS denominator. Weighted-average shares used in accounting EPS differ from end-period issued shares. [S1, PDF pages 221, 230 and 233. S4.]

Removing this one gain does not make the remaining profit fully normalized owner earnings. Ordinary depreciation, finance costs, receivable impairment, other income, maintenance requirements and future funding still matter. The gain's removal also does not imply the Apulki investment itself is worthless. Its unquoted carrying value is INR 57.125 crore, about INR 17.61 per issued reference share, but it is not freely available cash. No separate Apulki valuation or surplus-cash bonus is added in the base earnings model. [S1, PDF page 217.]

### Dilution is not free money, but it is not a pure loss either

Full exercise would also bring cash. The remaining warrant contribution is **INR 68.81145 crore**, and the March option counts at their stated exercise prices imply another **INR 32.1529451 crore**. These are conditional proceeds, not balances received.

As an explicitly hypothetical offset, using all INR 100.9644 crore to reduce debt costing 8%, with a 25% tax effect, would save INR 6.0579 crore annually and bring full-exercise EPS back to approximately **INR 23.36**. This is not a forecast or proof that the funds will be used that way. It demonstrates why dilution must be evaluated with the use of proceeds rather than presented as an automatic 8% economic loss.

## 2. Revenue is growing faster than shareholder profit

H1 FY27 operating revenue increased **26.95% to INR 506.501 crore**. Operating EBITDA after CSR and ESOP increased approximately **16.78% to INR 129.10 crore**, but its margin declined from **27.71% to 25.49%**. Owner profit declined **7.48% to INR 41.138 crore**. Finance costs increased from INR 14.492 crore to **INR 30.286 crore**, or **108.98%**. [S2, PDF page 6, and the matched XML periods.]

The benign interpretation is that Rajasthan and other new sites carry staff, equipment and depreciation before full utilization. Management expects operating leverage as volumes mature. The alternative is that low-priced PPP growth keeps consuming working capital while interest, channel-partner fees and depreciation absorb much of the benefit. Both interpretations remain plausible.

The November call says the Rajasthan project has potential for approximately INR 250 crore annual revenue, with the fuller contribution expected in the next financial year. It does not establish that run rate as already achieved. Retail contributed about 9% of Q2 revenue. Management's September operating break-even comment excludes marketing costs, while full break-even remains an expectation. Do not value the entire company as if it were already a mature cash-pay retail laboratory network. [S5, PDF pages 7-9 and 15.]

## 3. Where expansion funding actually came from

All figures below are H1 FY27 consolidated cash-statement amounts, in INR crore. [S2, PDF page 8.]

| Cash bridge | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Operating cash flow | +78.796 |
| Cash purchases of property, equipment and intangibles | -250.263 |
| Asset-sale proceeds | +4.610 |
| Net liquidation of bank deposits | +139.180 |
| Interest received | +20.304 |
| **Net investing cash flow** | **-86.169** |
| Warrant subscription money received | +22.937 |
| Net short-term borrowing proceeds | +71.313 |
| Net long-term repayment | -2.476 |
| Lease payments as presented | -8.137 |
| Interest paid | -29.085 |
| **Net financing cash flow** | **+54.552** |
| **Increase in cash: operating + investing + financing** | **+47.179** |

Operating cash minus gross asset purchases is **negative INR 171.467 crore** for the half-year and **negative INR 195.931 crore** on the reported trailing-flow basis. This is a funding diagnostic, not normalized maintenance free cash flow. Purchases include expansion and may settle earlier capital-creditor balances. Negative cash after expansion does not by itself prove that the investments destroy value.

### Collections and supplier funding are the immediate test

Receivables rose from **INR 295.506 crore in March to INR 474.556 crore in September**. Trade payables rose from INR 63.202 crore to **INR 231.741 crore**. The cash statement records a receivable outflow of INR 181.569 crore and a payable contribution of INR 149.492 crore. After all working-capital movements, their net effect is a cash outflow of INR 46.816 crore. [S2, PDF pages 7-8.]

Holding everything else fixed, eliminating the positive payable movement would turn the reported INR 78.796 crore CFO into **negative INR 70.696 crore**. This is an explanatory sensitivity, not a forecast that suppliers will suddenly demand all their money. Management says the payable increase contains trade, project and capital items without giving a complete split on the call. It should not be treated as permanently costless financing. [S5, PDF page 14.]

Management reports visibility on **approximately INR 200 crore of collections during Q3**. This is a material near-term catalyst, not cash already received. The best evidence will be actual collection receipts, ending receivables and whether supplier balances improve at the same time. A large gross receipt does not automatically reduce net receivables by the same amount when new billing continues. [S5, PDF pages 5, 9 and 10.]

## 4. Financing is available, but the new announcements must not be counted twice

Borrowings excluding leases increased from **INR 490.505 crore to INR 559.341 crore**. Adding leases gives INR 534.317 crore and INR 596.432 crore respectively. The XML borrowing totals include lease amounts in these filings, while the PDF shows separate rows. Adding the PDF leases again to the XML total would double-count them. [S2, PDF page 7, S6 page 11, and exact XML instant contexts.]

Current cash plus other current bank balances declined from INR 360.717 crore to INR 191.618 crore. This is not a complete unrestricted-liquidity measure. Noncurrent financial assets include additional deposits, and the audited March note identifies **INR 66.681 crore of bank deposits pledged for facilities and guarantees**. A fully reconciled September free-cash/net-debt figure is not claimed. [S1, PDF pages 218 and 220. S2, PDF page 7.]

The audited note describes **INR 430 crore face value of ADB debentures**, a **7.79% coupon**, a seven-year tenor and thirteen equal principal installments, with principal and interest payable half-yearly. The approximate principal per scheduled installment is INR 33.08 crore. This is an amortizing facility, not a seven-year principal holiday. The note does not by itself verify every subsequent repayment. The long-duration financing is a genuine positive relative to dependence solely on short-term bank lines. [S1, PDF page 223.]

### Warrants: only the remaining 75% is potential new money

The October issue is 1,621,000 warrants at INR 566 each, with **INR 22.93715 crore already received** and the balance payable on conversion within 18 months. The H1 cash statement already records INR 22.937 crore at its disclosed rounding, although formal allotment followed in October. The exact receipt date is not established here, but the money must not be added again as an entirely post-September funding source. [S4 and S2, PDF page 8.]

The unpaid amount is **INR 424.50 per warrant**, not another INR 566. Therefore, the reference share price being below the full issue price does not, by itself, imply that conversion is economically irrational. The original upfront payment is already sunk. Equally, promoter participation at INR 566 does not establish a price floor or guarantee the remaining cash.

### Equipment monetization is approved, not proven operating cash

The September board approved sales of identified unencumbered diagnostic equipment for **up to INR 120 crore**, with terms to be finalized. The call describes a sale-and-leaseback strategy. Neither an approval ceiling nor an asset sale establishes recurring cash generation. Any executed transaction must be assessed together with lease obligations, taxes, proceeds and continuing access to the machines. No INR 120 crore cash receipt is assumed in this valuation. [S7 and S5, PDF page 13.]

## 5. Read the contract, not just its title

The Delhi disclosure title refers to a 15-year project. The operative terms specify **ten years from commercial operation, extendable by up to five years**. KRSNAA must finance, establish, operate and maintain the facilities. The agreement is still to be executed, and the commercial-operation deadline is conditional on site handover. Consideration cannot be fixed upfront because revenue depends on test volumes and applicable prices. [S8, both pages.]

Cash-based PPP can improve collections. It does not remove investment risk, guarantee utilization or create fifteen years of contracted profit on the award date.

## 6. Five-year valuation and a falsifiable decision

The business is capital-intensive and primarily PPP rather than predominantly retail. The 12x-20x exit-multiple range is an analyst stress range reflecting that distinction and the possible improvement in cash quality. It is **not** claimed as a statistically estimated peer fair-value range. Growth assumptions are deliberately visible. No probabilities are assigned.

All per-share paths use explicit starting EPS assumptions that allow for funding/dilution risk. A starting EPS of INR 22 is close to the identified-gain-removed, full-exercise sensitivity. It is not reported interim EPS annualized.

| Case | Starting EPS | Annual EPS growth | Exit P/E | Year-five price | Annual price return at INR 493.60 | Entry for 15% hurdle |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Stress | 18 | 5% | 12x | 276 | -11.0% | 137 |
| Conservative base | 22 | 15% | 16x | 708 | 7.5% | 352 |
| Stronger execution | 22 | 18% | 18x | 906 | 12.9% | 450 |
| Bull | 25 | 20% | 20x | 1,244 | 20.3% | 619 |

Dividends, investor taxes, costs and slippage are excluded from the displayed price returns. An illustrative flat INR 2 annual dividend adds about INR 6.70 to each 15%-hurdle entry calculation, making the base threshold **about INR 359** and stronger-execution threshold **about INR 457**. That dividend path is not a forecast.

At INR 493.60 and INR 22 starting EPS, meeting a 15% price-return hurdle requires approximately **23.0% EPS growth with a 16x exit**, or **20.2% growth with an 18x exit**, for five years. If conditional exercise cash actually reduces debt at the assumed terms, the improved EPS sensitivity makes the stronger case more competitive. That is a reason to preserve the upside thesis, not to count unreceived funding as fact.

**Reconsider around INR 350-360 under the conservative assumptions. A level around INR 450-460 requires stronger evidence on collections and repeatable post-financing earnings.** These are conditional research thresholds, not automatic buy orders or certified intrinsic values. Good operating evidence could justify a higher price. A cheaper price with worsening collections may still be unattractive.

The stress case implies about 44.1% terminal-price downside over five years. It is not a maximum possible loss, a short-term price target or a stop-loss instruction.

## 7. What would change the conclusion

1. Q3 collection evidence and ending receivables confirm improvement without merely shifting the funding burden to suppliers.
2. New-site utilization improves operating margins and per-share profit after financing and maintenance costs. Retail profitability includes marketing costs.
3. Debt repayments, restricted cash, vendor credit, warrant conversion and any asset sale/leaseback are reconciled together.
4. A dated entry price or a materially stronger recurring-earnings case creates a return margin under reasonable downside assumptions.

Tax disputes remain an additional risk. The September notice reports an aggregate INR 62.6917435 crore in appellate orders and separately references a revised amount for one assessment year. The H1 auditor highlights the continuing matter. The aggregate is neither an adjudicated final liability nor a reconciled maximum exposure. No arbitrary full-tax-loss probability or duplicate subtraction is embedded in the valuation. [S9 and S2, PDF pages 3 and 9.]
