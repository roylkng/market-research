# PIX Transmissions: a funded industrial compounder with a price condition

**Decision date: 21 November 2026. Reference close: ₹1,698.70 on 20 November 2026.**

**Decision: WATCH at the reference price. Conditional BUY research price at or below ₹1,450, assuming the operating thesis and balance sheet survive the intervening evidence.** This is a stock-specific research decision, not a trade, sizing instruction, or a decision to keep an entire portfolio in cash.

The attractive business is a diversified mechanical consumables supplier with operating cash generation, accumulated financial assets and a funded route to growth. At the reference price, the conservative base case produces **7.8% annualized cash-flow IRR**, the downside **−6.4%**, and the favorable case **16.1%**. At ₹1,450, those become approximately **11.3%, −3.3%, and 19.9%**. A lower price does not prevent a loss.

The current-price verdict can change without a price decline. Sustaining about **10% sales growth and the current 26.6% normalized EBIT margin** produces approximately **11.1%** under the base funding and valuation treatment. That is the explicit favorable counter-case to a cautious verdict.

## 1. What was discovered and why it matters

The original four-company lane was PIX Transmissions, Control Print, Roto Pumps and Tega Industries. All four have current primary financial filings in the source manifest. PIX was selected because it combines profitable mechanical wear products, a global distribution footprint and financial capacity with an interpretable valuation. This is a selected four-name comparison, not a full-market screen. See `SCREENING_LOG.md`.

The mechanism is straightforward. Industrial equipment must transmit mechanical power. Belts wear, require replacement and can be upgraded to improve reliability, energy use and maintenance cost. A failed inexpensive component can interrupt a much more valuable machine. Availability, fit, specification and reliable service therefore matter alongside selling price. That creates an opening for a competent supplier with a broad range and distribution coverage. It does not prove that any particular supplier has pricing power or guaranteed recurring revenue.

PIX reports sales into automotive OEM and replacement, agriculture, machinery, mining, cement, steel and textiles. It offers V-belts, timing belts, Poly-V belts and special construction belts. FY26 product sales were ₹573.31 crore, of which ₹324.31 crore, about 56.6%, were outside India. This gives several demand pools but also meaningful export, currency and global industrial-cycle exposure. No quantified aftermarket share or independently verified customer-retention rate was obtained. [S2, printed pp35–37 and 211]

### The coming years

| Long-term opportunity | How the supplier earns | What can prevent shareholder gains |
|---|---|---|
| More installed industrial machinery and agricultural mechanization | Initial fitment followed by service and replacement demand | Equipment cycles, inventory destocking and competition can offset installed-base growth |
| Buyers value efficiency and less downtime | Better materials and synchronous designs can win product upgrades | Premium competitors offer the same benefit and can win the profitable niches |
| Wider export and distributor reach | More sales through an established product catalogue and manufacturing base | FX, shipping, local channel economics and foreign demand can weaken returns |
| Conversion of some chain drives to advanced belts | Additional applications beyond conventional replacement | The new application may be won by Gates or another specialist, with no benefit to PIX |

**Competition is active.** Gates announced its Poly Chain Carbon ONE industrial product on 4 November 2026, explicitly targeting chain-to-belt conversion. Optibelt markets engineered heavy-industry solutions with field support and reduced maintenance. Those are competitor claims about their products, not independently measured PIX disadvantages. They do establish that the innovation opportunity is contested. [S12, S13]

Better belt life also reduces replacement frequency. The investment case therefore needs growth in installed equipment, customer wins, product mix or value per installation. Assuming every machine consumes the same annual quantity indefinitely would overstate the opportunity. Gates' public maintenance manual says well-designed drives can operate for several years and distinguishes conventional maintenance needs from its premium service-free products. [S11]

## 3. Cash belongs to shareholders, but realization matters

At September 2026, the balance sheet shows cash equivalents of ₹106.53 crore, FVTPL investments of ₹262.42 crore and debt of ₹18.72 crore. That is **₹350.23 crore reported net cash before other obligations**. Other bank balances of ₹7.76 crore are excluded from usable cash because availability is not resolved. [S1]

FY26 investment notes identify ₹194.32 crore mutual funds and ₹5.25 crore equity instruments, all quoted. The available notes do not establish the mutual funds' complete underlying risk composition. Treating the entire portfolio as risk-free cash would be unjustified. [S2 note8]

The model therefore reserves existing provisions of ₹54.58 crore, a 6% revenue operating cash buffer in the base case and a 15% investment-portfolio haircut for valuation/tax uncertainty. It begins with ₹237.04 crore excess financial assets before scheduled debt settlement. New operating costs are treated as cash costs, without adding back future provisions. Existing debt is repaid from cash in model year two, covering the disclosed March 2028 related-party loan maturity and conservatively retiring other residual borrowing as well.

**Capital allocation is mixed.** FY26 disclosed director/related-party borrowing was ₹15.27 crore. The unsecured loan terms were 11–11.5% interest, repayable March 2028. This coexisted with substantial parent-company investments and cash, so the entire issue cannot be explained by cash held in overseas subsidiaries. The contract's prepayment terms were not resolved. This is a question about capital efficiency and owner economics, not a finding of improper conduct. [S2 notes17 and 38]

FY26 KMP salary/remuneration was ₹15.59 crore, with further related-party salary, rent and interest transactions. Rent to the related enterprise was ₹2.08 crore. The latest H1 related-party disclosure continues to show interest and rent payments and records some loan repayment. Those payments are expenses in the reported earnings used here, not extra deductions invented by the model. What remains uncertain is whether the compensation and retention policy gives minority shareholders good value for a growing treasury. [S2 note35, S10]

The annual report shows 62.06% promoter/group ownership as of March 2026. The controlling family occupies multiple executive roles, including the CFO position. This creates economic alignment and concentrated control. An unmodified audit and disclosed transactions are useful evidence. They do not establish superior governance, capital-allocation skill or workplace culture. The latest September ownership file was linked by the issuer but its direct download returned 403, so the original March percentage is the accepted ownership fact in this packet.

## 4. Five-year owner-cash valuation

The forecast uses five successive twelve-month model periods from the September 2026 TTM base. These are analyst scenarios, not company guidance. Price is the 20 November close. Future trading price, tax to the outside investor, fees and an executable fill are not assumed.

Owner cash is calculated from operating profit after tax, less after-tax debt interest, plus depreciation, less **maintenance spending, growth spending, incremental working capital and additional operating cash reserves**. Retained cash earns an explicitly assumed after-tax return. Dividends leave the company. Retained cash is carried forward and recognized once at exit. Debt repayment is funded explicitly and debt is zero at exit.

| Assumption | Downside | Base | Favorable |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue growth | 0%, 2%, then 3% | 8% per year | 12% per year |
| EBIT margin | 21%, then 20% | 24.5% | 27% |
| Tax on operating earnings | 26% | 26% | 26% |
| Maintenance capex / revenue | 4.0% | 3.5% | 3.5% |
| Growth capex / incremental revenue | 60% | 45% | 45% |
| Incremental working-capital ratio | 38% | 34% | 34% |
| Initial portfolio recognition | 70% | 85% | 95% |
| After-tax treasury return | 2% | 4% | 5% |
| Dividend policy assumption | Higher of ₹6/share or 10% funded cash | Higher of ₹9/share or 10% funded cash | Higher of ₹9/share or 20% funded cash |
| Terminal operating value / NOPAT | 12× | 16× | 18× |
| Terminal excess-cash recognition | 70% | 85% | 100% |

The 34% base working-capital ratio is anchored near the observed 32.6% operating working capital / TTM revenue and includes an initial catch-up. Depreciation is 3.7% of revenue. Maintenance plus growth capex exceeds depreciation and funds the asset base needed for growth. The 45% incremental capital ratio roughly maintains the relationship between net PP&E and sales. Neither maintenance spending nor physical capacity utilization is separately disclosed, so these remain valuation assumptions with explicit sensitivities.

The valuation is highly dependent on the price paid for future operating earnings, not just on current cash. No scenario probabilities are assigned. An attractive upside scenario does not establish the probability of achieving it.

The base terminal **16× NOPAT equals about 24.1× year-five funded owner cash**, because cash must continue funding working capital and productive assets. It is not a 16× multiple of cash distributable after growth. This terminal valuation still requires durable growth and capital productivity. With the rest of the base case unchanged, terminal NOPAT multiples of 12×, 14×, 16×, 18× and 20× produce current-price returns of approximately **3.0%, 5.5%, 7.8%, 9.9% and 11.8%**. A favorable rerating is therefore another possible route to returns, but it is not assumed in the base operating upgrade test.

## 5. How the cautious thesis can be wrong

The base model may understate durable margins, future growth or the productive use of already-installed capacity. These are tested rather than hidden in a broad uncertainty label.

| Change from base, other assumptions unchanged | IRR at current reference | Implication |
|---|---:|---|
| Full credit to initial portfolio and terminal cash | 8.8% | Treasury discounts alone do not explain the entire cautious verdict |
| Preserve the current 26.6% core margin | 9.6% | Margin durability matters substantially |
| 10% sales growth plus current core margin | 11.1% | A credible operating route to a current-price BUY exists |
| Growth capex only 25% of incremental revenue | 8.1% | Better spare capacity helps, but does not by itself transform expected returns |

To support that operating route, the next evidence should show product revenue growth beyond a weak comparison period, stable core margins after removing treasury movements, working capital within the funded range and incremental assets earning satisfactory operating returns. These are specific economic questions. An additional document, test count or model feature is not progress unless it changes one of them.

FY22 and FY23 also reported separate lease payments of approximately ₹1.98 crore and ₹0.83 crore, respectively. The table is before those payments. FY23 cash generation also benefited from working-capital and provision movements. It is not all a permanent operating improvement. [S8, S9, S2]

The FY23 chairman's letter stated that the expansion had been completed and capacity had come online. The subsequent cash generation demonstrates why a rule rejecting every year of negative cash after growth spending would be too crude. The correct questions are whether the growth investment is funded and whether later operating earnings and cash justify the cost.

There is also adverse evidence. Revenue was about ₹486.27 crore in FY23, ₹493.06 crore in FY24 and ₹589.45 crore as originally reported in FY25. FY26 revenue then fell to ₹582.31 crore, compared with the reclassified FY25 comparator of ₹593.41 crore. The latest annual report moved ₹3.96 crore miscellaneous recoveries into FY25 operating revenue, so historical growth calculations must not mix presentations silently. Installed capacity did not produce smooth annual growth. [S8, S9, S2 notes25–26]

That history supports a plausible industrial compounder, not a guaranteed secular growth stock. It justifies using a sustainable-margin case and funding growth explicitly, while allowing a higher-growth case to succeed if evidence supports it.
