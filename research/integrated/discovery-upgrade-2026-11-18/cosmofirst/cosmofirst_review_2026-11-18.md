# COSMOFIRST: current-price, cash and capex-cycle review

As of 18 November 2026. Bounded preliminary owner valuation, with independent model review. All financial figures in ₹ crore unless indicated.

## Decision

**WATCH_PRICE / PASS at ₹824.90.** Cosmo First is an improving operating business emerging from a large investment cycle. It deserves more attention than a mechanical rejection of a duty refund. However, a flat operating-profit case produces only approximately **6.85% annual shareholder IRR** at the current price, while leverage and supplier funding make the downside substantial. A conditional **₹650–700 research entry range** is defensible. This is an analyst valuation range, not an automatic buy instruction.

The current price can work well if specialty growth, new-capacity utilization and deleveraging progress together. That is the upside scenario, not established normalized earning power. This review closes today's preliminary price decision. It does not claim full competitive, governance or cycle coverage.

## The mechanical screen overstated H1 cash

The issuer's machine-readable filing puts **₹10.38 crore under interest paid** and **₹74.14 crore under dividends paid**. The signed PDF shows the reverse. Both can pass a total-cash reconciliation because the financing outflows still add to the same number. This is a semantic source error, not merely rounding. [S2, S6]

**Correct H1FY27 cash after gross capex, borrowing interest and all lease payments:**

₹256.42 − ₹90.40 − ₹74.14 − ₹6.01 − ₹4.27 = **₹81.60 crore**.

The initial screen's ₹145.36 crore residual was overstated by **₹63.76 crore**. Dividend paid was ₹10.38 crore. These residuals are before ordinary debt-principal repayments and dividends. They are not cash distributed to shareholders. Both original consolidated cash-flow pages were rendered locally and visually checked. [S2 p8, S3 p12]

## Why the forward case is materially better

Management says more than ₹1,250 crore of strategic capex was invested in FY24–26, major capacities are commissioned, and film utilization reached around 90%. The November call guides FY27 cash capex of **₹100–110 crore**, versus ₹90.40 crore already spent in H1, and tentatively **₹50–70 crore for FY28**. Management targets net-debt reduction of ₹300–500 crore over two years. These are management plans, not guaranteed cash flow. [S4 pp4,7,11, S5 pp5–7]

The operating evidence is broader than the duty refund. Q2 sales volume grew 15%, specialty sales grew 20%, BOPP base-film spreads improved year over year, and rigid packaging became profitable. H1 specialty-chemical EBIT increased to **₹37.39 crore**, from **₹23.96 crore**. [S2 p6, S4, S5]

The offsets remain real. Consumer and petcare H1 EBIT losses totaled **₹37.11 crore**, and the petcare loss increased from ₹21.94 crore to ₹29.73 crore. They are recurring business costs in this valuation. They are not added back as optional growth investment. Management is considering a transaction to fund Zigly's next stage, but an uncompleted transaction is not cash. [S2 p6, S5]

Industry additions over the next two years could affect base-film spreads. Management describes a roughly 60% specialty/semi-specialty mix, not a wholly insulated specialty business. Q2 base-film margin of around ₹29/kg, compared with ₹22/kg a year earlier, should not simply be capitalized forever. The working case stays flat and the downside reduces annual operating EBITDA after leases to ₹400 crore. [S5 pp4–6]

## Normalize the profits, not the entire business away

H1 reported EBITDA, defined here as PBT plus finance cost plus depreciation, is **₹324.04 crore**. It includes a provisional U.S. duty-refund benefit of **₹24.81 crore before tax**, or **₹18.23 crore after tax**. The November call also identifies about **₹14 crore one-time tax credit** on tax-regime transition. Reported PAT of ₹152.32 crore is therefore not an ordinary half-year earnings base. [S2 p8, S5 p4]

For the forward operating model, exclude **all ₹44.69 crore other income**, not merely the refund. This also removes investment returns from operating earnings because financial investments are already valued in net debt. That gives **₹279.35 crore H1 operating EBITDA**, or ₹558.70 crore annualized. Subtract the annualized ₹20.56 crore lease cash burden, giving **₹538.14 crore**. The working assumption is rounded to **₹540 crore annual EBITDA after lease cash**. The ₹0.15 crore joint-venture loss is retained and immaterial to the rounded model. [S2]

This is a run-rate starting point, not proof of through-cycle earnings. Inventory gains/losses are not selectively adjusted in one direction. The H1 specialty improvement and higher commodity spreads can coexist.

A stricter earnings reference gives H1 PBT excluding all other income of ₹124.23 crore. At an illustrative 25% tax charge, this is ₹93.17 crore H1 profit, or roughly ₹70.32 annual earnings per modeled diluted share. The current price is around 11.7 times that reference, rather than the much lower multiple implied by annualizing reported, tax-assisted PAT.

FY26's ₹7.20 crore Netherlands unauthorized-transfer loss provision relates to a previously reported 2023 incident, net of expected insurance/legal recovery. It is not projected as an annual manufacturing cost. It remains a controls-history issue. The original results and call were examined, but a complete control-remediation review was not performed. [S3 p12]

## Cash conversion still needs discipline

H1 CFO includes **₹178.90 crore additional trade payables** and **₹87.14 crore other financial liabilities**, against inventory and receivable absorption of **₹302.13 crore**. The gross working-capital movements matter even though the net effect was small. A portion of customer refunds or supplier funding can make reported cash temporarily stronger. [S2 p8]

The U.S. subsidiary received **USD6.97 million**, while **USD3.31 million was provisionally appropriated**, consisting of USD2.61 million other income and USD0.70 million inventory credit. The balance remains subject to customer-refund settlement. The model includes an approximate **₹35 crore one-time cash reserve** for the unappropriated USD3.66 million. It is a conservative modeling reserve, not a verified separate September debt balance. [S2 p8]

There is also a recurring incentive issue. H1 **₹36.68 crore sales-tax incentive income** is removed from CFO as unrealized, while it remains within operating revenue and EBITDA. It is not removed by excluding other income. The working annual ₹45 crore cash absorption is explicitly split into **₹30 crore incentive-receivable build** and **₹15 crore other working capital**. On an annualized ₹73.36 crore incentive-accrual assumption, this requires about ₹43.36 crore cash collection each year. That is a forecast assumption, not verified receipts.

March incentive receivables increased from **₹101.52 crore to ₹129.17 crore**, versus FY26 incentive income of ₹57.20 crore. If there were no other changes, that implies around ₹29.55 crore collections. This is an inference from balances, not a directly reported cash-receipt figure. If the annualized incentives were not collected at all, the incremental annual drag beyond the already reserved ₹30 crore would be **₹43.36 crore**. The working value at a 15% discount rate falls from ₹662 to **₹630**. No full ₹73.36 crore is deducted on top of the existing reserve. [S1 printed pp264,270,276–277, S2]

## Debt: neither cash-only nor every investment at par

September gross borrowing is **₹1,508.67 crore**, cash ₹20.78 crore and current investments ₹423.52 crore. The screen's cash-only net borrowing is ₹1,487.89 crore. Netting investments at par gives ₹1,064.37 crore before other bank balances, close to management's roughly ₹1,057 crore net debt. [S2, S4]

But the detailed March investment portfolio includes **₹74.78 crore AIFs**, **₹59.95 crore unquoted equity**, **₹135.98 crore perpetual/corporate bonds**, and other funds and market securities. The accounting label “current” does not make it all cash or fully available to meet loan maturities. The September security-level mix was not verified. [S1 printed p265]

The model applies an explicit **25% haircut to September current investments**, excludes the ₹7.16 crore other-bank balance, and obtains **₹1,170.25 crore adjusted financial net debt**. This is a sensitivity assumption, not a finding of impairment. Add the ₹35 crore temporary refund reserve and **₹30.41 crore March capital-goods payable reserve**, giving **₹1,235.66 crore model opening financial net debt**.

The capital-payable reserve is deliberately conservative: its September balance was not separately verified, and it may already have been paid or be included in future capex budgets. It is not presented as newly discovered September borrowing. Removing both temporary reserves lifts the working 15%-discounted value only to **₹678**. Removing those reserves and also using the entire investment portfolio at par lifts it to **₹705**. The current-price conclusion therefore does not depend entirely on these conservative adjustments.

Lease liabilities of **₹101.61 crore** are excluded from this net-debt measure because the operating EBITDA used for valuation is already reduced by recurring lease cash. Adding lease debt again without changing the EBITDA basis would mix valuation conventions.

### Supplier funding is the material additional funding sensitivity

The annual report discloses **₹649.59 crore supplier financing within trade payables**, up from ₹538.05 crore. Banks pay suppliers and Cosmo settles later. Terms are **90–180 days**, versus up to 90 days for comparable ordinary payables. [S1 printed p318, note53]

The working model assumes continued ordinary access to these arrangements. The downside includes a separate **₹150 crore one-time runoff**. It is charged once, not both as extra debt and working-capital expense. Adding the full ₹649.59 crore to debt while also funding its settlement through working capital would double count.

March term-debt current maturities of ₹253.25 crore exceed the working model's initial retained free cash. Thus the model relies on some combination of existing investments, normal working-capital renewals and refinancing. Net-debt reduction does not mean every contractual loan is repaid solely from the first year's cash. Capital commitments of ₹57.62 crore and uncalled investment commitments of ₹57.89 crore were also disclosed at March. They are not all expensed as losses because settlement may create assets, but they remain competing uses of liquidity. [S1 printed pp274–275,284–285,318]

## Owner-return scenarios at ₹824.90

The official NSE close is **₹824.90**, with **63,755 shares traded** and **₹5.3005 crore turnover** that day. Annual issued shares are **26,249,727**, giving market capitalization of **₹2,165.34 crore**. The model uses a conservative rounded **2.65 crore diluted shares**, versus roughly 2.6348 crore implied by H1 diluted EPS. That is a modeled denominator, not a claim of an exact current fully diluted count. [S1, S2, S7]

All following numbers are analyst assumptions. Free cash is retained to repay debt. Only ₹10 crore annual dividends are separately distributed. Retained cash is therefore counted through lower terminal debt, not again as annual shareholder distributions. No negative-tax benefit repeats in the forecast. Stock compensation remains an economic expense because the EBITDA comes from the P&L without adding it back.

The upside is economically possible: better mix, operating growth and capex falling after commissioning can drive substantial deleveraging. The working case deliberately does not assume that growth. The downside illustrates how ordinary cyclical profit compression can be magnified by leverage. These are not assigned probabilities or statistically calibrated forecasts.

No 15% hurdle is attributed to the user. The discount rates are scenario choices.

The current ₹824.90 price is above the working value even at a 10% required return. A ₹650–700 entry band would compensate better for this combination of cyclicality, leverage, treasury complexity and funding dependence, provided the operating thesis remains intact.

Sensitivity is explicit. One additional terminal EBITDA turn raises the working 15%-discounted value to roughly **₹796**. Increasing annual capex by ₹35 crore for all three years lowers it to **₹636**. Management's ₹50–70 crore capex outlook is not treated as a permanent maintenance-capex floor.

## What materially improves this case

The valuable next evidence is not another broad checklist. It is two quarters showing durable specialty-led earnings after the refund and inventory effects, real collections of incentive receivables, and debt reduction after ordinary supplier/customer/capex payments. Current portfolio liquidity and supplier-finance rollover terms matter more than a headline low P/E. If operating EBITDA after leases grows convincingly toward the upside path, ₹824.90 may become attractive without a price decline. If it stays near the working run rate, the price should provide the compensation.

This case therefore produces a substantive, current-priced decision and a specific alternative entry range. It also exposes a source-field failure that the wider discovery process must correct. It does not validate the remaining 196-company screen by implication.
