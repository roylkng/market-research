# FDC: business valuation closure, 19 November 2026

## Decision

**WATCH_PRICE, with product-quality evidence monitored. No purchase case at the verified ₹373.90 reference under the working assumptions.** This is a completed, explicitly bounded business judgment for a five-year horizon. It adds an operating-business review to the earlier `PASS_NEW_TENDER_ENTRY` judgment. It does not replace, soften or re-date that earlier tender conclusion.

**Identity and price:** FDC / EQ / INE258B01022. The original NSE final 18 November 2026 bhavcopy records close ₹373.90, volume 65,602 shares and turnover ₹2.44182729 crore. This is a reference close, not an executable fill or 19 November final price. The original CSV and ZIP were independently rehashed and the symbol, series, ISIN and date matched. [S10]

The business is considerably more resilient financially than a large, debt-funded construction project. It has established domestic formulations, recurring demand, zero reported borrowings at September and substantial financial investments. Yet the relevant purchase question is what the outside shareholder receives after continuing capex, working capital, product-quality costs and capital allocation. The working model produces **4.01% annualized shareholder return** from this price, with dividends and retained treasury treated consistently. The upside can reach **14.94%**, but needs 12% revenue growth, 18.5% operating EBITDA margin and a 22-times terminal multiple of core owner cash. That is a scenario, not an expected outcome.

The strongest counter-thesis is credible: stronger domestic brands, recovery in exports, falling capex after current capacity work and effective resolution of recalls could produce substantially more cash than the working case. The current evidence supports keeping the business on the list. It does not establish that full combination. The completed decision is therefore price-dependent rather than a blanket rejection of pharma or of a company with historical recalls.

## What improved versus the previous research

The 17 November note correctly closed the tender eligibility question but did not value the operating business. This review retrieves original annual and H1 filings, reconciles cash across comparable periods, removes treasury and one-time income from core earnings, models the buyback's cash and share effects, identifies actual recall disclosures and incorporates subsequent tax-refund orders separately. The conclusion now has an economic reason and reversible triggers. It is not simply another request for evidence.

The model also avoids a selection bias toward exceptional situations. FDC's recurring domestic pharmaceutical operations are evaluated on the same owner-cash principles as the more capital-intensive candidates, while using the appropriate financial structure. Strong liquidity improves survivability. It does not by itself create a high return at every price.

## Operating business and seasonality

The issuer's 27 October operating release reports H1 domestic formulations revenue of approximately ₹1,036 crore, 84% of consolidated sales, international formulations ₹148 crore and APIs ₹52 crore. Domestic growth was 7.8% in H1, with a much stronger second quarter. International formulations grew 47.6% from a smaller base. The cited IQVIA growth/ranking data are management-reported references, not a separately licensed dataset independently reproduced here. [S4]

The annual audited geographical disclosure shows FY26 India revenue ₹1,805.54 crore, USA ₹98.41 crore and other countries ₹266.98 crore. No single customer accounted for 10% of group revenue. The US market therefore matters to execution and quality credibility, but it does not represent the majority of group revenue. Product-specific brand contribution and margin concentration are not disclosed at the level needed to value Electral, Zifi and Enerzal separately. [S2, printed pp264–267]

**Do not double H1 profits.** H1 FY27 PAT was ₹181.55 crore, but the June quarter produced ₹132.49 crore and September ₹49.06 crore. Quarterly operating EBITDA excluding other income was approximately ₹143.00 crore in June and ₹64.29 crore in September. The H1 aggregate is ₹209.61 crore. The note on quarterly fair-value gain/loss presentation explains why some quarterly expense/income subtotals are not simply additive. H1 FY26 had also produced ₹149.72 crore of FY26's ₹281.42 crore annual PAT. A high first quarter is not a reliable annual run rate. TTM construction uses FY26 minus H1 FY26 plus H1 FY27. [S1, physical pp10–13]

Demand for treatments and rehydration recurs, while sales seasonality, therapeutic mix, price controls, generic substitution and quality execution affect what FDC earns. Recurring patient need does not automatically confer pricing power. This review gives no additional brand premium on top of both elevated growth and margins.

### Treasury versus operating earnings

FY26 other income was ₹113.52 crore. Its components included interest ₹52.38 crore, investment-sale gains ₹7.50 crore, fair-value gains ₹11.79 crore, PPE disposal gains ₹24.26 crore and other non-operating/foreign-exchange items. H1 FY27 interest, investment-sale and fair-value gains alone total ₹55.96 crore, against total other income ₹63.28 crore. Those are not pharmaceutical operating margins. The FY26 ₹20.79 crore exceptional employee-benefit obligation is separately identified. A prior-period leave-policy provision reversal also affects comparability. Normalizing the exceptional charge does not determine how much remains unpaid or prove that similar employee costs will never recur. The annual cash flow starts after that expense, and the reported employee-provision changes do not justify assuming the whole charge remains payable. The model therefore does not deduct the entire charge a second time in its base case. A conditional additional ₹20.79 crore employee-settlement stress, paid in year one and separate from the claims buffer, lowers working return to 3.93%. This stress is not a claim that ₹20.79 crore is currently unpaid. [S1–S2, printed pp252–253,261]

The model removes **all other income** from core EBITDA. It then models a separate after-tax return on credited treasury balances. No treasury income is capitalized in the operating multiple and then counted again through the same asset value. Historical CFO already excludes investing-classified interest receipts but includes taxes that can relate to non-operating income. The historical residual above is therefore a cash bridge, not a perfectly tax-allocated core earnings measure.

September investments were ₹629.62 crore non-current and ₹574.85 crore current, plus ₹36.21 crore cash. Borrowings were zero and lease liabilities ₹12.82 crore. The latest detailed annual investment notes identify bonds at amortized cost, mutual funds and a small InvIT exposure, including a ₹10 crore existing investment impairment allowance. It would be wrong to equate every investment with immediately realizable cash at par or to subtract that already-booked impairment again. [S1–S2, printed pp237–239]

## Buyback and subsequent cash claims

The signed letter fixes 162,810,084 current shares and 160,410,084 after full acceptance. Buying back 2,400,000 at ₹500 spends ₹120 crore, with transaction costs additional. The offer opens 19 November and closes 26 November. Record date was 13 November. Promoters intend to participate. Shares bought after the record date do not acquire fresh tender entitlement. This review gives a new buyer no tender premium, entitlement or guaranteed acceptance. [S3]

Full completion is a **conditional model state**, not a factual completion claim. The model subtracts the ₹120 crore and a clearly labeled ₹2 crore cost reserve before using the smaller denominator. The no-buyback comparison uses the original shares and retains the cash. Working return is 4.12% without the transaction versus 4.01% with it. The modest difference illustrates why the tender premium should not be presented as free value for continuing shareholders.

Three later original exchange filings disclose favorable tax orders totaling **₹72.13 crore**: ₹37.22 crore on 31 October, ₹14.20 crore on 4 November and ₹20.71 crore on 5 November. They say the interest portion will be recognized as income. They do not disclose the principal/interest split or verify receipt of all cash. [S7–S9]

The model excludes the entire September ₹67.89 crore income-tax asset balance and all other-bank balances. It gives the separately identified refunds a 75% credit, ₹54.10 crore, to allow for timing, tax on interest and uncertainty. This is an assumption, not a measured collection probability. It does not simultaneously add the tax asset and refund cash, nor turn a refund into recurring drug earnings.

## Product quality, governance and management

### Evidence that prevents an unqualified quality premium

The annual BRSR, printed p95, reports four voluntary recall instances in FY26: Ziram stability-assay results, adverse-event reports after Vitcofol-FCM administration, and two Timolol container issues. It reports zero forced recalls. The same section gives no useful detailed corrective-action explanation, while the management discussion says root causes of prior US recalls are being addressed. This is a disclosure and execution limitation. It is not evidence that all medicines or facilities are defective. [S2]

The original FDA enforcement API query for the recalling firm FDC Limited returned **11 historical Class II recall product records**, initiated in 2024–2025. Ten were marked `Ongoing`, one `Terminated`, at retrieval on 19 November. The latest initiation returned was 5 July 2025. The reported reasons concern container/nozzle defects. These are the status labels of the retrieved records. They do not establish present defective inventory, a company-wide import ban, new 2026 recalls or an exhaustive inspection audit. A California Board of Pharmacy notice independently identifies FDC's ciprofloxacin batch recall and the same container mechanism. [S5–S6]

The annual discussion says FY26 US profit share fell from approximately ₹37.30 crore to ₹23.30 crore partly because earlier batch recalls continued to affect finances, despite higher US sales. That makes the issue economically relevant. The working case does not award a premium margin or multiple for flawless quality. The downside captures weaker margins and extra spending. No numerical personal-integrity penalty is inferred from a product recall. [S2, printed p51]

## Reopen conditions and highest-value next step

**Price route:** Reopen business entry analysis around ₹285–311 if the operating evidence is intact. That is the current model's 10–8% reference range. At ₹261 the same stream corresponds to 12%. These are moving analytical references, not a permanent fair value, instruction to buy or an invented 15% hurdle. A lower price does not excuse deteriorating quality or uncontrolled capex.

**Evidence route at the same price:** Reopen when comparable full-year cash supports substantially better recurring earnings, such as the 10% sensitivity's approximately ₹380 crore year-five core cash with a defensible path, without relying on treasury gains, tax refunds or automatic multiple expansion. An alternative is well-supported lower continuing capex that releases cash while preserving the business. Stronger operating data can update the model before price falls.

**Quality route:** New issuer/regulator evidence resolving the recurring container issue, explaining Ziram and Vitcofol corrective actions and showing limited recurring recall costs can narrow the margin range. A historic FDA status changing to terminated alone is not proof that the whole quality system is fixed. Conversely, a material new recall or regulatory restriction should reopen the downside immediately.

**Dated checkpoints:** On or after 4 December 2026 check actual buyback completion, extinguished shares and spending. At the next December-quarter release, targeted by 15 February 2027 as a research checkpoint rather than an announced board date, separate tax-refund interest from operations and check seasonality. After FY27 reporting, targeted by 31 May 2027 as a review checkpoint, rebuild annual cash after capex. No new automation is created here.

**Highest-value next evidence task:** Obtain a project-by-project capex/completion and quality-remediation cash bridge from the next original annual/interim disclosures, then compare it with actual post-capex cash. That single task tests whether the current heavy spending earns higher returns and falls away, and whether quality costs are contained. It is more decisive than another headline P/E comparison.

## Material ambiguities retained

1. September investments have aggregate balances, not a refreshed security-by-security liquidity/maturity map. Portfolio haircuts are assumptions.
2. Maintenance versus growth capex and the complete remaining September commitment schedule are not disclosed at decision-grade granularity.
3. Tax-refund orders are confirmed by original issuer filings, not original tax-authority decisions. Cash receipt and interest split remain unverified.
4. Historical recall status labels do not quantify current exposure or establish completed corrective actions. Costs and affected product revenue remain incomplete.
5. Brand-level contribution, price elasticity and customer preference lack independently reproduced evidence.
6. Current encumbrance detail and a comprehensive regulatory/litigation search are not certified. No zero-issue claim is made.
7. Five-year governance, capital-allocation and terminal-value outcomes remain scenarios. No named portfolio suitability or trade authority is inferred.
8. Broad-market opportunity cost is deliberately an independent allocation question, not a reason to force a stock purchase or to remain out of equities.

## Independent arithmetic challenge and correction

The independent review confirmed the H1 figures and found no double counting of core income/treasury, dividends/retained cash or tender cash/shares. It identified the need to deduct the observed current tax liability explicitly and distinguish the employee-obligation normalization from actual settlement. This final version makes that deduction and adds the conditional employee-settlement sensitivity. The preliminary working return of 4.08% becomes 4.01%. The WATCH_PRICE decision is unchanged. No earlier published investment judgment is rewritten.
