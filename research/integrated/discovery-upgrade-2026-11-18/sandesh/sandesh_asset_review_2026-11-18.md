# Sandesh: asset discount review, 18 November 2026

## Decision

**PASS at ₹987.15. Retain as a focused asset-value candidate, with a ₹750–850 research re-entry band, not an automatic buy order.** This is a better candidate than its deteriorating media earnings alone suggest. The balance-sheet discount is real, but it is not all liquid cash and a minority shareholder has no demonstrated route to receive the discount. On an explicit, reasonably favourable retained-capital model, a persistent discount produces about 8% annual return. The 15.5% case requires discount narrowing. That is insufficient support for a high-confidence BUY today.

This is a bounded first valuation, not a complete operating-company audit. The exact issues affecting the decision are identified below. No adverse inference of fraud or misconduct is made from disclosure inconsistencies.

## Valuation without pretending everything is cash

First use only the September current-investment bucket, cash and all liabilities. This does not require assuming which noncurrent securities were held in September:

**₹874.0195 + ₹10.8322 − ₹90.0851 = ₹794.7666 crore, or ₹1,049.97 per share.**

A 10% haircut to that current portfolio gives **₹934.50 per share**. There is no compelling margin over the ₹987.15 stock price from this most directly identified asset pool alone. This is not a full valuation because it excludes the noncurrent securities, properties and profitable media business. [S2, model]

A fuller financial-asset sensitivity uses September total investments and provisionally subtracts the **March Applewoods carrying value**. This explicitly assumes that the Applewoods holding and value did not change. September's named portfolio was not disclosed in the reviewed H1 result, so these are sensitivity values rather than verified liquidation proceeds.

| Haircut to all investments excluding Applewoods | Net financial value after all balance-sheet liabilities | Value per share |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0% | 970.7050 | ₹1,282.40 |
| 10% | 865.7092 | ₹1,143.69 |
| 20% | 760.7134 | ₹1,004.98 |
| 30% | 655.7176 | ₹866.27 |

Cash is included. Other bank balances, Applewoods, properties, media franchise and other operating assets are excluded. All reported liabilities, including deferred tax, are deducted. No separate blanket liquidation tax is then deducted, as that could double count recorded deferred tax. Actual tax basis and realization costs could change the result. A generic portfolio haircut is a sensitivity input because the September scheme and security mix has not been verified, not a precise estimate of loss on bonds or funds.

The media business also has value. FY26 reported media EBIT before exceptional items was **₹69.6157 crore** and after exceptional items **₹67.5863 crore**. H1FY27 media EBIT is **₹29.4903 crore**, down **24.16%** from **₹38.8839 crore**, on revenue down about **9.38%** to **₹136.7822 crore**. [S2 p14, S3 printed p202]

For a separate owner-return test, assume **₹35 crore sustainable annual media cash after tax and reinvestment**, and a **₹250 crore media value**. These are analyst assumptions, not extracted owner earnings. They are below the simple annualized H1 after-tax EBIT and acknowledge operating decline, corporate costs and reinvestment. We do not add media plant, receivables, inventory or property value again. Commodity trading gets zero franchise value. Deducting all corporate liabilities while adding a net operating franchise value is intentionally conservative.

That makes illustrative opening adjusted NAV **₹1,220.705 crore**, and the stock price **61.21% of that value**, a **38.79% discount**. It is possible to regard that as attractive. The next question is what makes the discount produce an acceptable return.

## A discount is not a promised return

Illustrative three-year model: non-Applewoods financial investments earn **6% annually after corporate tax**, the media business contributes **₹35 crore annual owner cash**, total dividends remain **₹3.7847 crore per year**, retained cash is reinvested, and media terminal value stays ₹250 crore. Applewoods and every other separately valued asset remain zero. The future portfolio return is an assumption, not a performance forecast. The full formula and yearly path are in the JSON file.

| Terminal discount to this adjusted NAV | Ending wealth per share, including three dividends | Three-year annualized return |
|---|---:|---:|
| 50% | ₹1,019.46 | 1.08% |
| Same 38.79% discount as today | ₹1,244.70 | **8.03%** |
| Narrows to 25% | ₹1,521.69 | **15.52%** |

This is distinct from the financial-asset haircut table. One estimates asset-value sensitivity. The other models the stock return under retained capital and an explicit market discount. We do not add the two discounts invisibly. The 15% outcome requires a market repricing. A perpetual discount alone does not prevent a good investment, but here plausible retained-capital compounding at the existing discount is only moderate.

The proposed ₹750–850 research band is close to, or below, the 30% financial-asset stress value before any media franchise value. It is a price at which to reopen the exact capital-allocation question, not a claim that a lower share price automatically improves future return if the discount stays permanent.

## Capital allocation and minority access

Promoters held **74.81%** at March 2026. FY26 annual dividend was proposed at **₹5 per share**, and the H1FY27 cash-flow statement shows **₹3.7847 crore dividends actually paid**, consistent with that amount. The yield at today's price is about **0.51%**. This establishes current cash payout without relying on a secondary AGM headline. [S3 printed pp71 and181, S2 p13]

The two promoter managing directors' FY26 remuneration was **₹5.175 crore each**, total **₹10.35 crore**, including commissions, against the ₹3.7847 crore aggregate shareholder dividend. Compensation is already an operating cost, so it is not deducted a second time in the owner-return model. The comparison matters because it illustrates the current priorities for shareholder cash access. It is not itself a finding of improper remuneration. [S3, printed p60]

This is not a new cash hoard generated entirely by H1 exceptional profitability. Investments were already **₹1,136.3869 crore** in March 2025, then **₹1,003.0872 crore** in March 2026. The case therefore needs evidence about how existing capital is deployed and returned, not just discovery of a low price/book ratio. A verified historical share-price discount series was not completed in this bounded review, so no precise historical discount claim is made. [S3, printed p174]

## Commodity use and the cash-flow inconsistency

H1FY27 commodity trading revenue was **₹467.3269 crore** with EBIT **₹6.7480 crore**, a margin of about **1.44%**. Segment assets fell from **₹266.1490 crore** at March to **₹37.6790 crore** in September. The latter number was verified visually, correcting an OCR reading of ₹31.619 crore. Thus, headline sales growth largely comes from a new low-margin activity. [S2 p14]

The annual governance report says the company had not traded in the commodity market, while its financial statements identify a commodity-trading segment and commodity transaction profit, and discuss derivatives used for hedging. This might reflect imprecise differentiation between physical trading and commodity-market activity. It needs explanation and is not sufficient to allege misconduct. [S3 printed p72, pp182,194,202]

A more concrete issue is the signed H1 cash flow. The original consolidated page, visually checked, reports:

- **CFO ₹354.5338 crore**.
- **A positive ₹64.6529 crore fair-value adjustment**.
- **Inventory release ₹181.1732 crore**.
- **Net cash purchases of investments ₹370.0507 crore**.

The P&L note says the parent had an H1 **FVTPL gain ₹64.6194 crore**, with a small subsidiary difference. Yet the investment roll-forward exactly fits a *loss*:

**₹1,003.0872 + ₹370.0507 − ₹64.6529 = ₹1,308.4850 crore.**

If the gain sign is correct, an illustrative repair would lower both CFO and reported investment purchases by **2 × ₹64.6529 = ₹129.3058 crore**, leaving the net change in cash unchanged. This would make CFO **₹225.2280 crore**, and only **₹44.0548 crore after removing the inventory release**, before any other working-capital normalization. **This is a reconciliation hypothesis, not an official restatement.** [S2 pp11–13]

Therefore the filed high CFO must not be used as evidence of recurring owner cash. The asset case can still be investigated independently, since the balance-sheet amounts and net cash reconcile, but the cash-flow presentation requires a source correction or explanation before high confidence is warranted.

## What can change the decision

The material remaining work is narrow: obtain September's named investments and restrictions, reconcile the signed cash-flow gain/purchase treatment, and identify a credible allocation/payout mechanism that can lift owner returns without simply assuming discount convergence. A large buyback, consistent high payout, demonstrably superior capital returns, or a substantially lower price would change this analysis. A positive earnings headline alone would not.

The current screen has therefore produced a real asset-discount candidate and a priced rejection, rather than a generic permanent research queue. It has not established a BUY.
