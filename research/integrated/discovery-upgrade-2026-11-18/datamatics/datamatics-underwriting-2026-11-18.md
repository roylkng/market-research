# Datamatics: completed underwriting and a price-dependent candidate

**Decision date: 18 November 2026. Research disposition: WATCH_PRICE, with a conditional entry reference around ₹600.** Horizon: five years. The official NSE close was **₹703.65**, for DATAMATICS EQ, ISIN INE365B01017. The latest reconciled share count is 59,106,389, giving a ₹4,159 crore market capitalization. This is a reference close, not an executable order. [S1, S2]

The acquisition and cash questions are substantially resolved. This is now an economically supportable candidate, rather than a company suspended indefinitely for unavailable evidence. At the current price the base range implies approximately **9.0–10.45% annualized economic return** before investor taxes and trading costs. That can be acceptable for an investor satisfied with a roughly 9–10% modeled return. I prefer a lower entry for this single company because the same valuation depends on sustained organic cash growth, margins and a terminal valuation. The user has **not** imposed a universal 15% return requirement. The table below exposes the choice rather than silently imposing one.

The strongest counter-thesis to WATCH_PRICE is straightforward: H1 earnings and cash generation are strong, TNQ has produced attractive cash relative to its overall price, and the market could reward continued delivery before ₹600 becomes available. The cost of waiting is missing that return. Conversely, the evidence does not justify treating current margins, AI pricing power or every future acquisition as permanently successful.

## The three decision-changing findings

### 1. TNQ was expensive at the margin, but the overall acquisition has earned real cash

The audited FY26 report gives **₹348.12 crore** for the initial 80%. The signed NSE filing dated 31 July 2026 gives **₹206.80 crore** for the remaining 20%, completed that day. Total purchase consideration is therefore approximately **₹554.92 crore**, before transaction fees and financing costs. The annual report separately records ₹5.15 crore of FY25 acquisition expenses, so identified consideration plus that expense is approximately ₹560.07 crore before financing. The cash-return percentages below use purchase consideration, and would be slightly lower on all-in cost. This resolves the confusing forward-liability language in the annual report. It does not support adding the liability and its remeasurement twice. [S3, S4]

The original TNQ subsidiary accounts, read from rendered pages 14 and 15, provide this bridge. Values below convert the source's INR lakhs to INR crore. [S5]

The last two measures represent **18.0% and 14.1% of total purchase consideration**. They are useful cash-return proxies, not acquisition IRRs, audited ROIC, or proof that one strong year repeats. The corresponding 20% share of the working-capital-neutral proxy is ₹15.69 crore, only **7.6% of the ₹206.80 crore final payment**. Management bought a valuable business, but paid a much richer marginal price for full ownership.

TNQ revenue grew about 14.0% over FY25. Its FY26 PAT margin was 25.1%, versus 13.4% in FY25. The sharp improvement requires monitoring. A working-capital release and much lower other expenses both assisted the reported results. No FY26 dividend was paid by TNQ, so “earned cash” is not the same as cash already remitted to Datamatics shareholders. [S5]

**No extra 20% profit uplift is added after July.** The FY26 consolidation schedule already includes TNQ's full ₹84.91 crore PAT, with the identified noncontrolling interests elsewhere in the group. The remaining stake also had a recorded forward liability. The legal move from 80% to 100% cannot be assumed to create a new 20% earnings step-up in this model. [S3, S4]

### 2. The cash decline reflects acquisition deployment, not an operating cash collapse

H1FY27 selected operating equity cash was **₹127.99 crore**, calculated as ₹190.41 crore operating cash flow less ₹37.70 crore capex, ₹18.04 crore lease principal and ₹6.68 crore interest paid. Acquisition/subsidiary/minority payments were ₹210.30 crore, including the independently confirmed ₹206.80 crore TNQ completion. The remaining ₹3.50 crore is not assigned to a particular deal without further evidence. Net cash and investments therefore fell despite positive operating cash generation. [S4, S6, S7]

To reduce half-year seasonality, the following bridge uses FY26 plus H1FY27 minus H1FY26. It is a research definition of available owner cash, not a reported GAAP subtotal. [S6]

The tax reserve compares **current tax ₹88.37 crore** with cash tax ₹63.52 crore. Using total P&L tax would incorrectly allow the ₹12.07 crore net deferred-tax credit to reduce this cash normalization. This review corrected that preliminary mistake. The reserve is conservative timing normalization, not a claim that all ₹24.85 crore is immediately payable.

The minority reserve uses TTM noncontrolling profit as a provisional attribution proxy. Subsidiary-specific minority cash is not separately disclosed in the half-year cash statement, so ₹271.16 crore is not an audited parent-distributable amount. The observed minority earnings share is small. A further ₹10 crore annual cash haircut lowers the base valuation at a 12% required return by about ₹24.5 a share.

Interest and dividend receipts appear under investing cash flow, outside CFO. Their TTM total is ₹21.56 crore. The model includes only ₹16.17 crore after an illustrative 25% tax haircut. Some treasury tax may already be in the total current-tax reserve, making this addition conservative. The operating-only base case omits all treasury income and uses ₹255 crore. The ₹270 crore working case retains a conservative treasury contribution, subject to financing changes described below. **No separate net cash, investment value or enterprise-value-to-equity adjustment is then added.** Cash interest is already deducted, so debt is not subtracted a second time.

TTM working-capital movement contributes only ₹2.94 crore to group cash generation. The group cash result is consequently not explained mainly by releasing working capital. H1 cash is nevertheless seasonal, and the first-half figures should not simply be doubled into a forecast.

### 3. The operating improvement is real, but AI durability remains a commercial question

H1FY27 revenue was ₹1,060.55 crore, up 10.7%. EBITDA was ₹216.6 crore at a 20.4% margin. Profit attributable to the parent's shareholders was **₹145.76 crore**, distinct from group PAT ₹145.94 crore. TTM attributable PAT is ₹226.35 crore, or ₹38.30 a share, giving a reported trailing P/E of 18.37. Exceptional acquisition accounting makes that P/E incomplete, but it is not a reason to capitalize every adjusted charge as free cash. [S6, S7]

Operations currently leads, rather than Technology alone. Q2 Operations revenue grew 15.6%, Technology 8.8%, and Experiences 0.8%. FY26 Technology revenue had fallen 4.5% and Experiences 8%, with clients moving work to captive centers. This is an observed counterexample to the claim that all digitization automatically benefits outsourced suppliers. [S7, S8]

Management does not separately report AI revenue because services, software and human involvement are bundled. Its statement that roughly 55% of opportunities are heavily AI driven is an opportunity-pipeline assertion, not 55% of revenue. Continuing annual R&D of roughly ₹40–50 crore is expensed and remains a cost in this model. Automation can improve margins, while buyers can also demand price reductions or internalize work. These effects have not been separated quantitatively. [S7]

## Owner economics and reverse valuation

All cases below are analyst assumptions. They use five years of annual cash available to common owners, valued as distributed or accumulated at par, followed by a terminal multiple of year-five owner cash. They are **not forecasts of actual dividends**. No cash is counted both as annual distributions and again as terminal excess cash. If management retains cash and spends it poorly, its value must be reduced.

Organic growth must be financed by operating expenses, capex and working capital already deducted in the cash assumptions. The company's ₹3,000 crore ambition includes possible acquisitions, so its full proposed 15% revenue growth is not imported into the base model. A new material acquisition would require deducting its purchase price and rebuilding the case. Treasury income is not assumed to compound independently of the rest of the business.

| Scenario | Business assumptions, not forecasts | Starting annual owner cash | Annual cash growth | Terminal owner-cash multiple |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Conservative | Revenue grows roughly 0–3%, EBITDA margin compresses toward 17–18%, customer savings accrue partly to buyers | ₹220 crore | 0% | 10× |
| Base range | Organic revenue grows roughly 7–9%, margins remain around 19–20.5%, necessary reinvestment is funded within the cash estimate. Lower bound excludes treasury income. | ₹255–270 crore | 8% | 12× |
| Upside | Organic growth near 10–12%, margins around 21–22% persist, products and account expansion convert into cash | ₹300 crore | 12% | 15× |

The cash assumptions, rather than a separate detailed revenue-and-cost forecast, determine the calculations. The operating descriptions show what makes each cash path plausible. No probabilities or calibrated win rates are assigned. The upside deliberately requires several favorable developments together.

| Present value per share | 10% required return | 12% required return | 15% required return |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Conservative | ₹372 | ₹345 | ₹310 |
| Base, operating cash only | **₹677** | **₹625** | **₹558** |
| Base, with treasury contribution | **₹716** | **₹662** | **₹590** |
| Upside | ₹1,101 | ₹1,015 | ₹902 |

These are scenario present values, not one-year price targets or a claimed liquidation floor. The base terminal component is about 69% of value at a 12% discount rate. Reducing the base terminal multiple from 12× to 10× lowers value from ₹662 to ₹586. Reducing annual cash growth from 8% to 4% lowers it to ₹562. This sensitivity is the reason for restraint, not a claim that the company must fail.

At today's ₹703.65, keeping starting cash ₹270 crore and terminal multiple 12× requires annual cash growth of approximately **7.6%, 9.5%, or 12.4%** to earn 10%, 12%, or 15% respectively. Alternatively, at 8% growth, starting cash must be approximately **₹265, ₹287, or ₹322 crore**. The current price therefore already capitalizes meaningful continuing improvement.

**Around ₹600 is a conditional research entry reference, not “fair value.”** The base cash range implies approximately 13.1–14.6% annualized economic return at that price, about 4–9% below the corresponding 12%-discounted values. An investor requiring 15% under these same assumptions would instead derive approximately ₹558–590. Its purpose is to show the tradeoff between price and required return. It is neither a standing buy order nor protection against a weaker business. At ₹703.65 the case can become a research buy for an investor comfortable with approximately 9.0–10.45% in the base range, but the conservative case offers little protection against permanent business deterioration.

Investor-level taxes, brokerage, spread and market impact are excluded because actual user circumstances and position size are unknown. The observed NSE session traded only 17,573 shares, about ₹1.24 crore. That is an execution consideration, not a rejection gate without a proposed position size. [S1]

## Funding, ownership and integrity

September net cash and investments less borrowings reconcile to ₹556.87 crore. This includes ₹199.80 crore of noncurrent investments. Current investments plus cash less all borrowings are ₹357.07 crore, before leases. Total lease liabilities are ₹66.40 crore. Other bank balances of ₹15.63 crore are not assumed freely distributable. Goodwill of ₹611.32 crore is approximately 36.6% of parent-attributable equity. The headline ₹557 crore is consequently not a guaranteed cash floor. [S6]

The annual report discloses a ₹150 crore TNQ acquisition term loan with ₹12.5 crore quarterly principal installments beginning 20 March 2026. The conservative valuation does not add existing financial assets to value. Those assets provide capacity to meet principal amortization, subject to actual liquidity and operating needs. The operating-only ₹255 crore case reserves those existing assets for principal repayment and omits their income, while still deducting current interest expense. It adds no uplift for interest savings. The ₹270 crore working case is conditional on sufficient interest savings or residual treasury income preserving the modeled net financing contribution. It does not treat the same cash as both freely spent on debt and permanently earning interest. No new leverage funds growth. Restricted cash, asset monetization and maturity matching remain less precisely verified than the reported balance totals. [S3]

The reconciled share count was unchanged during the September quarter. The annual report says the earlier performance stock option plan failed its performance conditions and closed, reversing ₹5.59 crore of provision. That reversal is not recurring economic earning power. Cash-settled phantom compensation remains relevant and is reserved in the cash bridge. Unmodified audits and review opinions provide useful assurance, but are not a forensic certificate. The secretarial report identifies a five-day delay in the prescribed interval for a risk committee meeting. Family control and related-party remuneration warrant continuing minority-owner scrutiny. No verified adverse integrity finding in the inspected sources establishes a purchase veto. [S2, S3, S6]

## What changes the decision

**Price route:** approximately ₹600 with substantially intact cash, balance-sheet and customer evidence supports upgrading to a research buy candidate for policy review. A price fall caused by a damaged business requires recalculation rather than automatic buying.

**Evidence route at today's price:** sustained attributable owner cash near ₹287 crore with 8% organic growth supports a 12% modeled return. Near ₹322 crore supports 15%, all else equal. These are sensitivities, not new mandatory company guidance gates. Better capital-allocation evidence or more durable client economics can also support a lower required return.

**Invalidation route:** sustained compression toward the conservative cash case, a material customer loss, new acquisition spending without adequate returns, or a substantive integrity problem lowers the value. Exact quarterly threshold rules would create false precision from a seasonal business and are not imposed here.

**Highest-value next evidence:** at the next annual cash-flow release, reconcile recurring parent-owner cash after current tax, compensation and capex with client retention and any new acquisition payments. The release date has not been assumed. This is monitoring an already completed judgment, not postponing the judgment until perfect information appears.
