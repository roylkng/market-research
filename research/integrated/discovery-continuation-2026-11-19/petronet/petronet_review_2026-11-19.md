# Petronet LNG: cheap reported earnings, a much larger capital-allocation decision

**Research date:** 19 November 2026, India time. **Disposition: WATCH_CAPITAL_ALLOCATION.** This is a bounded original-source funding review, not completed company underwriting or a stock price target. The prior 18 November work only identified the large capex programme. This continuation checks the signed cash statements, actual loan facility, vessel dispute, dividend entitlement and the return required on remaining project investment.

**Verified reference:** NSE PETRONET, ISIN INE347G01014, EQ, 18 November close **₹325.50**. The official original ZIP was hash-verified again. That session traded 23,44,791 shares, ₹76.65 crore. This is one day's liquidity, not an executable current fill. The 19 November final archive returned HTTP 404 at the bounded retrieval attempt. The FY26 audited count is 1,500,000,088 shares, with unchanged rounded September capital, implying ₹48,825 crore equity value. A fresh November capital audit is not claimed.

## Decision and strongest counterargument

The consolidated mechanical trailing P/E is **10.54x**, using ₹4,633.92 crore trailing PAT. That is a legitimate reason to investigate. Established LNG infrastructure, contracted customers, a substantial financial-asset balance and a signed bank facility make this more credible than an unfunded project announcement.

The purchase case still depends on two unresolved economic quantities: sustainable regasification and commercial cash under normal vessel operations, and returns on the new petrochemical investment. Current low P/E cannot resolve either. The counterargument is meaningful: if existing regasification economics remain durable and new projects earn satisfactory returns, the price could already compensate for much of the risk. This note does not prove otherwise. It also does not silently convert the expenditure programme into a permanent cash burn or value all new assets at zero.

The corresponding consolidated H1 proxy is ₹1,253.32 crore and the trailing proxy is ₹1,858.63 crore. These exclude separate investment disposals, treasury receipts, equity distributions and future committed capex. They are **not normalized distributable owner cash**. The originals' H1 consolidated cash page and annual funding page were visually inspected after downloading the bytes.

Three normalizations matter before extrapolating:

1. **Vessel suspension.** Parent lease cash fell from ₹348.50 crore to ₹38.64 crore between the two half years. Owners of dedicated vessels claim **₹625.42 crore plus applicable taxes** following suspended operations. Arbitration is pending. The company disputes the claims and has not provided for them. The audit review draws attention to this without modifying its conclusion. Neither a certain loss nor a cost-free permanent saving is established.
2. **Commercial earnings.** Management combines trading and inventory effects in a **₹658 crore Q2 operational/commercial efficiency gain**. This is approximately 39.7% of parent Q2 PBT. It is not necessarily all nonrecurring, but its durability cannot be assumed from the label. Management no longer gives the two components separately.
3. **Tax and use-or-pay collections.** H1 current tax is ₹851 crore versus ₹643.41 crore paid. The ₹207.59 crore timing gap is not a deferred-tax credit. Use-or-pay receivables remain ₹408.23 crore gross, ₹177.51 crore net of provision. Waivers and provision reversals must be reconciled to current revenue and receipts. They are not automatically recurring cash growth.

## Funding is real, but the cash is not all surplus

At September, parent cash equivalents, current investments and other bank balances total **₹12,389.63 crore**. Other bank balances are not all freely distributable. The March consolidated note identifies ₹29.49 crore unclaimed dividends, ₹59.90 crore unspent CSR cash and ₹112 crore deposits under lien. Those March amounts are examples, not an invented September restriction schedule. Parent September lease liabilities total ₹2,113.06 crore.

The FY26 audited note verifies **₹12,000 crore secured rupee facilities**, ₹6,000 crore each from SBI and Bank of Baroda. They were undrawn at March. September statements contain no borrowing line. A loan commitment provides funding capacity, while a draw creates debt and interest. It cannot be added to equity value as cash.

The approved petrochemical budget is **₹20,685 crore**, with management reporting approximately ₹3,300 crore spent through September. The arithmetic remaining budget is therefore approximately **₹17,385 crore**, before evaluating cost revisions or exact payment obligations. Management's aggregate company capex guidance is around ₹9,000 crore in FY27 and a similar amount in FY28. It described H1 capex of ₹1,626 crore, whereas the parent's signed PPE/intangible cash is ₹1,686.03 crore. This ₹60.03 crore definition/timing difference remains explicit. The implied ₹7,374 crore H2 budget uses management's basis, not a fully reconciled statutory payment schedule.

Other published projects include the ₹2,013 crore third jetty, ₹6,354.80 crore Gopalpur terminal and a newly signed **50:50 CBG JV** with total estimated project expenditure of ₹1,200 crore. Do not add these full budgets to the aggregate guidance without an overlap and already-spent bridge. Equally, do not omit them when estimating future distributable cash. Fifty percent ownership of the CBG JV does not establish that ₹600 crore of immediate equity funding is due.

The November **₹7 dividend has a 6 November record date**. A fresh 19 November buyer has no entitlement to that payment. It represents approximately ₹1,050 crore of company cash outflow. The current-price purchase model must not claim both the September cash and that dividend as a new buyer's free benefit.

## What the remaining petrochemical investment must earn

We can quantify a useful requirement without inventing a plant forecast. Assume the remaining ₹17,385 crore is paid 40%, 45% and 15% at the end of years 1, 2 and 3. Assume 25 years of level project cash beginning in year 4, with no terminal salvage. These timing proportions are analyst assumptions. The 25-year useful-life reference comes from the November call.

| Required project discount rate | Annual after-tax project cash required |
|---|---:|
| 10% | ₹2,162 crore |
| 12% | ₹2,562 crore |
| 15% | ₹3,218 crore |
| 12%, plus 20% cost overrun and one-year delay | ₹3,443 crore |

Cash here means **after operating costs, tax, sustaining investment and working capital, before financing**. It is not EBITDA, revenue or an equity return forecast. This is a remaining-investment sensitivity, not the IRR of the entire project from inception. Spending already incurred is not charged again. The model does not also deduct financing interest, and it does not assert that the published project budget excludes capitalized interest.

Management declined to quantify the new plant's unit operating cost on the November call, describing that discussion as premature. The highest-value next task is a defensible propane-to-propylene/polypropylene spread and utilization model, including integration savings, maintenance, tax, working capital and financing. Until then, a stock price target or probability of outperformance would create precision unsupported by the work completed.

**Reopen at the same price** if normal-vessel recurring cash and the funded project case support it. A lower price can improve the prospective return, but cannot replace a material project-profitability assessment. The strongest falsifier of caution would be satisfactory unit margins and contracted profitable demand with budget discipline. The strongest adverse signal is rising project cost or borrowing accompanied by deteriorating core cash.
