# NUCLEUS: the treasury is substantial, but the operating recovery is not yet earned

**Decision at the original NSE 20 November 2026 EQ close of ₹611.45: WATCH_RECOVERY_AND_PRICE.** The cash lead was tested through original annual and interim statements and a funded five-year sensitivity. It was not parked on a vague list. This is a compact cash and recovery review, with narrower leadership and competitive diligence than the accompanying NEWGEN case.

The headline temptation is straightforward. Roughly 2.633 crore paid-up shares imply equity value of about ₹1,610cr. Management reports ₹942.1cr of treasury. That makes the operating franchise appear inexpensive. The contradiction is that **H1 operating cash flow turned negative and operating profitability deteriorated sharply**, while reported earnings still include substantial treasury income and a favourable employee-benefit provision reversal. Buying cash plus a struggling business at a reasonable-looking P/E requires underwriting that deterioration, not ignoring it.

The completed base recovery sensitivity produces about **7.8% dividend-inclusive annual return** at the current price. The downside is approximately **−11.3%**, and the favorable recovery case approximately **23.7%**. A **₹475–500 reconsideration range** offers a more attractive payoff under the same base scenario, but is conditional on treasury protection and evidence of collection and margin stabilization. These are case-specific scenarios, not probabilities or a common hurdle imposed on every company.

## Cash ownership, not a headline subtraction

The September signed consolidated balance sheet and the 2 November earnings call reconcile as follows. Figures are ₹crore. [N1, N2]

| Difference excluded from issuer treasury measure | **22.13** |
| Issuer's broad cash/treasury measure | **942.10** |

The ₹942.1cr figure is therefore not accounting cash equivalents and is not an assumed immediate distribution. The annual original describes long-term bank deposits, bond and debt-fund investments, some strategic equity and security deposits. Its dated restricted balances are small, including bank-account and deposit liens plus unclaimed dividends. The interim filing does not separately disclose every September component. The model excludes the ₹22.13cr difference instead of assigning it a convenient liquid value. [N1, N2, N3]

In the base valuation, the ₹942.1cr pool is reduced by all ₹64.97cr of current and noncurrent provisions, a ₹0.99cr dated lien/unclaimed-dividend proxy, ₹0.62cr other noncurrent liabilities, a ₹20cr investment/realization haircut and ₹88.29cr operating liquidity. **Only about ₹767.23cr enters as opening available treasury.** The downside uses a larger liquidity reserve and haircut.

Customer advance billing and deferred revenue are part of current liabilities, not free owner money. They enter the working-capital bridge. The base scenario permits negative operating working capital of only 5% of revenue, compared with approximately 7.5% negative at the starting date, so that transition consumes cash. The downside removes this funding advantage. Future service delivery remains an operating cost. This avoids subtracting all customer obligations from cash and then charging the same obligation twice through future cash flow.

The asset pool cushions execution risk. It does not create a liquidation floor or compel management to release the cash. In a business with weaker cash generation, large treasury holdings can also support prolonged low-return expenditure.

The source is the signed current cash statement, with its comparative columns visually inspected. The annual original corroborates FY26. These are mechanical reported-period bridges, not a claim that a negative TTM residual is a perpetual loss. [N1, N3]

H1 EBITDA declined to about ₹23cr from ₹56.8cr, and Q2 EBITDA margin was only 6.8%. Revenue grew 6.6% in Q2, but H1 growth was only 1.6%. Management attributes cost pressure partly to AI talent, the changed employee-benefit basis and new RSUs. These may be investments, but investment spending earns a growth assumption only when it translates into customer revenue and cash. [N1, N2]

The H1 reported PBT contains a **₹9.82cr exceptional credit** after a remuneration-structure revision reduced the previously recognized gratuity and leave liabilities. FY26 had recorded ₹21.95cr of related exceptional expense. The model removes the net exceptional effect and all other income from operating profit, then adds back noncash SBC. TTM cash EBIT before SBC is approximately **₹76.93cr**, or **8.7% of revenue**, compared with reported PAT of ₹100.37cr. The same calculation for the latest half year is only **₹18.01cr, or 4.1% of revenue**. The base model's 8% first-year cash EBIT margin already assumes recovery from that recent level. There is no reason to capitalize treasury interest as operating franchise earnings and also add the treasury at book value. [N1]

Receivables rose from ₹121.12cr in March to ₹220.37cr in September. The call reports 80 DSO versus 63 in June. Management says some annual maintenance invoices were raised late and collected within the following 10–15 days. That is a specific and testable explanation. The signed September statements cannot confirm the subsequent receipt, and this review does not pretend they do. Increased deferred billings explain part of the balance-sheet shape, but they do not by themselves establish cash recovery. [N1, N2]

## Equity claims and funded compensation

The annual report gives **26,325,306 subscribed paid-up shares**, with 2,800 additional forfeited shares pending reissue. A FY25 buyback extinguished 448,018 shares. The original September shareholding statement confirms that same paid-up share count, including **114,187 employee-trust shares**. The model uses the full count rather than the smaller weighted EPS denominator. Promoters hold 73.6%, and the declaration records no promoter encumbrance. This concentration supports alignment but also leaves capital-allocation control with a small group. [N1, N3, N5]

The current RSU scheme is primarily funded through secondary-market purchases, although fresh issuance is permitted. H1 actually spent ₹8.46cr buying shares into the trust. The October 30 original grants a further 4,481 RSUs, after 259,060 grants disclosed for the first half. The scheme pool is one million shares. The model therefore deducts an explicit annual cash award budget of 1.5% of revenue in the base case, after adding back noncash SBC. It imposes no second dilution charge on top. If awards are instead satisfied through new shares, the model must replace the relevant cash budget with dilution, counting the cost once. No tax shield or option exercise proceeds are credited. [N1, N3, N4]

The signed balance sheet shows no bank borrowing. Lease liabilities total ₹7.18cr, and future lease cash is deducted in every scenario. Software intangibles are ₹2.41cr plus ₹0.05cr under development. This does not support a claim that large current product costs are being deferred into intangible assets. All forecast capex remains funded. [N1]

## The long-term positive case and its limit

Nucleus owns lending and transaction-banking software used in regulated institutions. The customer need for servicing loans, collections, compliance and a stable operating history persists through technology cycles. The installed base can create renewal and upgrade opportunities, and a bank cannot casually replace a production loan-processing platform. The latest disclosed order book was ₹1,317cr, with about 90% classified as product business. These are support for continued relevance, not a backlog-to-profit conversion guarantee. [N2]

The competing explanation is that a mature platform must keep spending to retain relevance, while stronger rivals and banks' in-house capabilities limit price realization. AI capability may be a cost of staying competitive rather than a separate revenue stream. The current CFO explicitly said the company embeds AI in its software and does not identify AI as a separate revenue stream. Five new logos in the half year and recent implementations are useful, but the reported margin and cash trend remains adverse. [N2]

Customer concentration is meaningful. The top five clients contributed about 27% of quarterly revenue, and India about 57%. Those figures are broader than a single-client dependency, while still leaving the company exposed to a small set of banks' timing and purchasing decisions. The review does not claim independent customer interviews, renewal-cohort statistics, technical benchmarking or a complete current leadership-integrity assessment. Those limits do not create the current-price decision. The funded economics do.

| Cash EBIT margin path | 6% throughout | 8%, 9%, 10%, 11%, 11.5% | 10%, 12%, 14%, 15%, 16% |
| Annual cash share-award budget / revenue | 2.0% | 1.5% | 1.25% |

| Exit funded-cash multiple | 10× | 15× | 18× |
| Five-year exit price | ₹315.80 | ₹805.56 | ₹1,564.20 |
| Cumulative modeled dividends / share | ₹23.70 | ₹73.68 | ₹140.12 |
| Dividend-inclusive annual IRR | **−11.3%** | **7.8%** | **23.7%** |

The base is already a recovery case. It does not extend the latest weak quarterly margin for five years, and it does not restore the earlier peak margin. Its year-one funded operating cash is only about ₹14cr after working capital and cash award funding. By year five that reaches approximately ₹77cr, or ₹29.36 a share. Available treasury grows to about ₹961cr after dividends. No scenario needs external funding, but the downside consumes a material portion of the cushion during the initial working-capital adjustment.

Treasury earns only the specified net yield. Interest enters distributable cash and retention once. The terminal operating multiple excludes treasury income. The terminal equity value adds only the remaining treasury after all modeled costs and dividends. The model does not add ₹942.1cr to earnings that already include the earnings on ₹942.1cr.

At ₹500, the same base case offers approximately 12.4% annual return. Around ₹475, it offers a higher return without needing the favorable case. These prices are reconsideration conditions, not orders or forecasts of where the market will trade.

## Specific decision conditions

**At approximately ₹475–500:** consider a conditional value position only while the protected treasury remains close to the modeled pool, the recent receivable expansion reverses without rising credit losses, and margins stabilize. A practical operating checkpoint is quarterly EBITDA returning above roughly 10%, supported by actual collections rather than only a provision reversal. Do not reduce the entry price and simultaneously assume the adverse business change has no effect on value.

**At ₹611.45:** a stronger economic case needs evidence that the company can restore cash EBIT into the low teens, fund employee awards and product development, and convert at least about ₹70–80cr annually to operating cash after capex, leases and awards. A repurchase or higher distribution could improve realization of the existing asset value, but none is assumed unless announced and funded.

**Adverse evidence:** persistent receivables around ₹220cr or higher without matching sales growth, repeated explanations of imminent collection without cash arrival, further underlying margin decline, or using treasury for expensive acquisitions would invalidate the protected-cash interpretation. The existing assets do not excuse deterioration.
