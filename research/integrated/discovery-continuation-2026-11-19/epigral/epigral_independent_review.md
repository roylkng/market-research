# Epigral independent accounting and valuation review

Reviewed on 19 November 2026. Reference price ₹944.30, official NSE close on 18 November. All company amounts are INR crore. This is a separate review of the primary researcher's work, not a new company recommendation or a return forecast.

## Conclusion

**I agree with WATCH_PRICE. I found no direct double count in the capex, debt, dividends, lease or retained-cash mechanics.** An independently implemented calculation reproduces the original downside, working and upside annualized returns, peak net debt and terminal share values exactly.

The consequential limitation is the narrow valuation cushion and the assumptions behind the operating and funding path. The working present value at a 12% return preference is ₹946.59, only ₹2.29 or 0.24% above ₹944.30. That does not establish mispricing. It also does not justify rejecting the company categorically. The evidence supports a finished price-sensitive judgment with a same-price route through profitable ramp-up.

Two clarifications were sent to the author and accepted for incorporation. First, the core and downstream revenue convention must distinguish internal feedstock transfers from consolidated external sales. Second, the model demonstrates potential financeability, not verified committed financing. These are material interpretation corrections. They do not require mechanically deleting upstream profit or subtracting debt principal twice.

## Gross funding is larger than the net-debt increase

The annual report specifies quarterly term instalments of 14.58 and 14.24. Their combined full-year schedule is 115.28 while both loans continue amortizing. On those published terms, the first two model periods imply the following approximate financing requirements. [R2, physical pages 244–246, printed pages 269–271]

| Working model period | Net-debt change | Existing term principal | Gross incremental and replacement funding |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| H2 FY27 | +177.61 | 57.64 | **235.25** |
| FY28 | −40.42 | 115.28 | **74.86** |

This calculation assumes an unchanged minimal cash balance, no asset sales and no equity issuance. It is not a verified drawdown schedule. Timing within the half-year can require more liquidity than the model's period-end peak.

The June credit opinion described substantial unused facilities. The audited March report identifies a 600 working-capital sanction. The October rating list includes an 82 proposed term facility that is not allocated to a lender. These records are evidence of financing capacity, but rated limits, working-capital facilities and proposed term funding are not interchangeable with committed unrestricted cash for the new project. The July call described a 60% debt and 40% internal funding intention for the 600 program. That is management's plan, not an executed lending agreement. [R2, R4, R5]

It would be wrong to deduct the 57.64 and 115.28 again from terminal equity. The net-debt bridge already captures cash generation and the resulting borrowing balance. The needed correction is to expose the gross financing and refinancing assumption, then monitor actual access and covenants. The author's subsequent wording changes implement that distinction.

## Captive feedstock requires an explicit revenue convention

The November call says approximately half of expanded ECH output will be used internally when epoxy production ramps. The July call describes 700–800 of chlorotoluene and MPP turnover as a combined figure. Therefore, adding a fully utilized upstream sales figure and a downstream sales figure is not automatically a consolidated external revenue bridge. [R3, R4]

This **does not prove the model double counts EBITDA**. Upstream manufacturing profit can remain economically earned through the final product. If downstream margin is measured after buying feedstock at a market-equivalent transfer price, upstream economic profit plus incremental downstream profit is a coherent convention. It is the summed revenue that needs internal-transfer elimination. Deleting all upstream margin would then be an incorrect correction.

The author accepted a convention of gross upstream economic turnover plus downstream external sales, with downstream margin after market-equivalent feedstock cost. The displayed aggregate is consequently a pre-elimination modeling proxy, not independently verified consolidated revenue. Neither the issuer's whole-company 5,000–5,100 ambition nor the model's lower aggregate by itself validates product-level volume, price or transfer assumptions. Working capital must be treated as a separate conservative cash assumption until external revenue and balance movements are observable.

The independent 25% lower downstream-sales case below is a business stress. It is not an estimate of captive eliminations and does not mechanically remove upstream production profit.

Margin persistence is more consequential than the modeled interest convention. Using opening debt understates interest while borrowing rises but overstates it while debt falls. On the whole working path, a linear average-debt variant raises value modestly. I found no optimistic-value problem concealed in that approximation.

The fixed working-capital outlays in the lower downstream-sales stress are intentionally unchanged. This is conservative and avoids pretending that a reduction in revenue must immediately release all inventory and receivables. The higher-capex path is a stress, not a claim that book depreciation equals economic maintenance.

## Current tax, derivatives and cash restrictions

The 13.98 current-tax reserve equals current H1 tax expense 57.33 less cash tax 43.35. It is a valid timing diagnostic, but it is not the entire September current tax liability of 25.30. Charging that full liability instead adds 11.32 to initial cash uses and reduces working PV to 944.87. This stress gives no credit for noncurrent tax assets. It must not be described as the uniquely correct tax forecast, because payment timing and offset rights are not established. [R1]

The 25 derivative allowance is also an estimate. The annual report discloses March derivative liabilities of 15.40 noncurrent plus 12.31 current. September discloses 9.24 noncurrent but does not separately break out the current derivative portion from other financial liabilities. The model should retain the allowance's status as an analyst reserve. It does not prove the remaining currency exposure is settled. Nor should a noncash prior fair-value charge be deducted again from both normalized earnings and future cash without a bridge. [R1, R2]

The October tax demand of 28.11 is separately charged in full. Treating a disputed demand conservatively in a valuation does not establish that the company will lose the dispute. The remaining renewable OCD commitment is an additional financial investment, not free liquidity. [R1, R6]

Management's lower net-debt measure differs from the model's cash-only borrowing measure. The March annual report explicitly identifies 50.10 of bank margin money securing guarantees and borrowing. No immediate spare-cash credit is taken. September reclassification is not fully disclosed, so I agree with retaining the conservative treatment rather than labeling all other financial assets liquid. [R1, R2]

## Valuation clock and the 7× multiple

The reference is 18 November 2026, the first model endpoint is 31 March 2027 and the final endpoint is 31 March 2032. The discount intervals are 0.3641 and 5.3662 years. Starting from September balances while estimating all of H2 is a valid roll-forward convention if October to November activity remains explicitly estimated. It is not realized post-purchase cash. The original model is clear on this point and pays no first-period dividend.

The 7× terminal post-lease EBITDA multiple has no independently established historical floor in this work. Its economic plausibility can be assessed without pretending it is a forecast. Working terminal EBITDA of 875.5 gives enterprise value 6,128.5. Terminal cash after assumed tax, capex and working-capital use is approximately 574.3, a 9.37% yield on that enterprise value before dividends. Replacing the 95 capex allowance with 235, equal to the modeled non-ROU depreciation, lowers that cash yield to 7.09%. The latter is an intentionally demanding replacement-capital stress, not an estimate of actual maintenance requirements.

The original sensitivity already shows 9.47% annualized return at 6× and 6.59% at 5×, retaining the original operations and full cash recognition. That is a more useful disclosure than declaring 7× intrinsically cheap. Terminal retained cash of 1,236.31 is counted once, but its future availability and allocation remain assumptions. Small dividends explain why approximately 98.17% of working present value sits at the terminal date.
