# Grid investment: durable need, different shareholder economics

Assessment date: **21 November 2026**. Financial amounts are **₹ crore** unless stated. This is a completed bounded APAR structural assessment and reverse valuation, with two explicitly narrower comparators. It is research, not a capital-allocation instruction. Prior research and frozen experiments are unchanged.

## 1. Where the opportunity comes from

Electricity must be delivered reliably at the time and location of use. More generation, geographically dispersed renewables, industrial demand and higher peak loads require transmission, transformation, controls and distribution. Land and right-of-way constraints also make increasing the capacity of existing corridors valuable. These are separate spending needs, not a single renewable-generation forecast.

The Ministry of Power's **30 July 2026 parliamentary answer** provides useful physical anchors. The target columns below are policy plans. They are neither funded orders nor forecasts of company revenue. [GRID_PLAN]

| Physical measure | Observed baseline | Planned endpoint | Implied capacity CAGR, approximate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Transmission lines | 5.09 lakh circuit km, June 2026 | 6.48 lakh, 2032 | 4.1% over six years |
| Transmission transformation capacity | 1,478 GVA, June 2026 | 2,345 GVA, 2032 | 8.0% over six years |
| Inter-regional transfer capacity | 120 GW, June 2026 | 168 GW, 2032 | 5.8% over six years |
| Distribution transformers | 895 GVA, March 2026 | 1,631 GVA, 2035 | 6.9% over nine years |

The stronger growth in transformation capacity than line length is consistent with a need for more power throughput and network density. It does not prove which supplier wins. Replacement, reconductoring and product upgrades can add to physical capacity growth. Metal inflation can inflate reported sales without increasing installed equipment or economic value. The official plan therefore supports a long runway, but not automatic 20% to 30% sales or earnings growth for every supplier.

### Three ways to capture the spending

| Business | Mechanism that can retain value | What competes the value away | Current conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| **APARINDS** | Qualified conductors and fluids, installation capability, specialized cable approvals and export distribution | Other qualified bidders, technology licensing, metal/freight exposure, customer credit and funding needs | Structural case supported, five-year price demanding |
| **GVT&D** | High-voltage systems, HVDC/GIS engineering, complex execution and a global manufacturing network | Capacity additions, contract pricing, lower-margin mix, execution risk and parent-related terms | Strong business cycle, high expectations. Bounded comparator |
| **POWERGRID** | Construct, commission, finance and operate long-lived transmission assets reliably | TBCB tariffs, construction delays, cost overruns, funding cost and depreciation of the existing asset base | More moderate valuation, requires a project-return/funding case |

These businesses are not interchangeable. Equipment suppliers earn conversion margins and execution profits. Asset owners earn returns over a long asset life. A transmission project announcement should not be counted as equally valuable to all three.

## 2. APAR: what is defensible and what is not

APAR's conductor capability is more than an issuer slogan. POWERGRID's published award for the Talcher–Meramundali HTLS reconductoring package identifies APAR as the lowest bidder on **2 May 2025**, with a **₹115.60 crore contract**. It also identifies **three qualified bidders**, including K Ramachandra Rao Transmission & Project and Sterlite Power. This proves a real award and competing qualified supply. It does not prove completion or monopoly pricing. [PG_AWARD]

Technology partner CTC Global documented APAR reconductoring work in **March 2018**, well before the present market enthusiasm. It later described the Bihar projects awarded in August 2022 and completed in 2023, involving approximately 800 circuit km and demanding crossings. A commercial partner is corroboration of capability, not an independent assurance of project profitability. [CTC_2018, CTC_BIHAR]

AssetCool's **25 August 2026** announcement corroborates a commercial license for photonic conductor coatings and a multi-year development relationship. It supports a new product option. It does not establish exclusivity, a protected royalty stream for APAR, customer adoption or realized incremental returns. APAR depends partly on externally owned technology. [ASSETCOOL]

The best economic hypothesis is that customers pay for a qualified, reliably installed solution that increases power transfer without obtaining a new corridor. APAR could combine product and execution expertise, rather than compete solely on aluminium conversion. Falsification would be declining premium mix or unit margins, lost repeat qualifications, rising warranty costs, or larger volumes that require disproportionate working capital without earning adequate returns.

H1 working-capital movements absorbed ₹446.89 crore in receivables and other assets, ₹900.88 crore in inventory and ₹770.50 crore through lower trade/other payables. That is a disclosed funding response, not automatically poor capital allocation. The test is subsequent returns per diluted share. [APAR_H1, APAR_QIP]

**Headline debt/equity understates funding dependence.** FY26 reported borrowings were ₹840.51 crore, but note 28 separately discloses **₹5,013.78 crore supplier finance within trade payables**, including ₹4,311.55 crore already paid to suppliers by banks. Associated terms are 30–180 days, against 0–90 days for suppliers outside the arrangement. Supplier finance was ₹4,079.83 crore a year earlier. This is disclosed financing, not an allegation of hidden debt. It must be represented consistently in working capital and financing, without both adding it to EV and charging the same balance twice. [APAR_AR printed pp393–394]

At September-end, reported cash was ₹1,171.49 crore, investments ₹274.86 crore, borrowings ₹888.35 crore and leases ₹125.38 crore. These balances do not establish surplus distributable cash. Some QIP proceeds remain committed and the September supplier-finance split is not separately presented in the abbreviated results. FY27 management capex plans total ₹1,500 crore, while H1 cash PPE spending was ₹505.44 crore. The next evidence must connect commissioning, throughput, cash release and return on the expanded equity base. [APAR_H1, APAR_DECK]

## 5. What the price requires

The model uses after-corporate-tax PAT, 20% payout and 0.3% annual share dilution. All are conditional assumptions. Dividends arrive at year-end. Returns exclude personal tax and trading costs. No probabilities are assigned. Starting profits are analytical normalizations, not guidance. The working ₹1,400 crore is close to reported trailing PAT, but its sustainability is not established by that similarity.

| Conditional case | Starting sustainable PAT | Annual PAT growth | Exit P/E | Five-year IRR | Ten-year IRR |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Profit-reset sensitivity | 976.93 | 8% | 20× | **−13.4%** | **−3.2%** |
| Lower-growth case | 1,200 | 8% | 20× | **−9.7%** | **−1.1%** |
| Working | 1,400 | 15% | 25× | **3.6%** | **9.3%** |
| Upside | 1,800 | 20% | 30× | **17.9%** | **19.1%** |

The lower-growth row preserves the original assumptions, but its ₹1,200 crore starting PAT is already above FY26's ₹976.93 crore. Calling it a comprehensive downside case understated profit-reset risk. The distinct reset sensitivity uses reported FY26 PAT as an observable stress anchor, followed by the same 8% growth and 20× exit. It does **not** establish that FY26 is the true sustainable normal or the worst possible outcome. The ₹1,400 crore working starting profit remains an assumed normalization. No scenario is assigned a probability.

This is a **bounded reverse valuation, not a fully funded forecast**. It lacks an annual supplier-finance, capex, receivable and debt roll-forward. A steady-state retention identity implies 18.75% ROE to fund 15% growth with 80% retention, or 25% ROE for 20% growth. Actual cash and return timing can differ substantially. Additional equity raises or lower returns on new plants reduce per-share results.

The same ₹1,400 crore starting PAT, payout and dilution imply the following profit growth requirements. There is no imposed universal 15% hurdle.

| Desired annual return | Required PAT growth, five years at 25× exit | Required PAT growth, ten years at 25× exit |
|---|---:|---:|
| 8% | 19.9% | 13.6% |
| 10% | 22.1% | 15.7% |
| 12% | 24.3% | 17.8% |
| 15% | 27.7% | 21.0% |

The longer runway changes the judgment materially. A business can compound respectably for ten years while still delivering weak five-year returns from an expensive purchase. The current price is not nonsensical, but requires substantial durability. A purchase case improves through a lower entry price **or evidence of a higher sustainable profit base, well-funded growth and durable margins**. An arbitrary permanent price target would become stale as those facts change.

## 6. Historical mechanisms, not a hindsight backtest

**Capital-cycle failure:** the government's July 2017 review identified 34 stressed thermal projects with about ₹1.77 lakh crore debt. The disclosed causes included missing fuel arrangements, absent PPAs, inadequate promoter funding and contractual/regulatory issues. The lesson is that national electricity need does not guarantee bankable demand and operating cash for a particular asset. This is a sector mechanism, not an assertion that APAR resembles a distressed thermal developer. [THERMAL_2017]

**Matched supplier weakening:** GE T&D's FY22 report records revenue of ₹3,065.95 crore, operating loss before finance of ₹141 crore and PAT loss of ₹49.62 crore, despite ongoing grid additions and rising electricity demand. It attributes the deterioration to delayed customer projects, lower fixed-cost recovery and commodity inflation on firm-price contracts. It also reports intense bidding competition. COVID and supply-chain disruption were substantial alternative explanations, so this is not proof that excess factory capacity alone caused the loss. [GVT_AR22]

**Contrasting capability:** APAR's documented 2018 and 2022–23 reconductoring execution supports a capability accumulated before the current boom. It does not establish the stock was cheap at those dates. No historical purchase decision, total-return outcome, win rate or unseen holdout has been manufactured here.

**Early signals for this cycle:** order intake below execution, longer approval/collection delays, falling qualification-adjusted unit margins, increasing supplier-finance dependence, capex ahead of utilized demand, or repeated equity raising without improving per-share cash. These are observable before a decade-long story is decisively disproved.

## 7. Bounded comparator conclusions

### POWERGRID

The reported FY26 EPS is ₹17.13. Adding H1 FY27 EPS ₹8.03 and subtracting H1 FY26 ₹7.74 gives approximately **₹17.42 trailing EPS**. At ₹269, that is **15.44× trailing earnings**, **2.34× September book value** and **3.35% on the ₹9 FY26 announced dividend rate**. The final ₹1.25 was recommended in the annual report. This review did not verify its subsequent bank payment. None is a forward dividend promise. [PG_FY26, PG_H1, PG_AR]

H1 consolidated PAT rose about 4%, while transformation capacity and projects are expanding. The gap between investment and earnings is exactly why capital expenditure cannot be treated as instant profit. September debt was ₹1,51,207 crore and CWIP ₹55,419 crore. Work in hand of ₹2.20 lakh crore includes this CWIP, so adding them together would double count. The deck's 89% TBCB mix is the decisive qualification to the “regulated utility” thesis. Winning more projects at weak tariffs, delays before commissioning or higher construction and funding costs can dilute new-project returns. [PG_H1]

**Judgment:** serious structural alternative at a much less demanding earnings valuation than APAR or GVT&D. The bounded review supports prioritizing a funded asset-by-asset/portfolio capitalization model. It does not yet establish a BUY. A stable current dividend, cash held aside, and diversified equity exposure have different return/risk profiles. An APAR pass is not an instruction to keep the entire portfolio in cash.

### GE Vernova T&D India

The current call reports H1 revenue around ₹3,860 crore, order intake ₹3,580 crore, and September backlog ₹21,440 crore against ₹21,460 crore in March. Q2 EBITDA margin of 27.1% is strong. Current cash and cash equivalents around ₹3,250 crore **include lending to the group cash pool**, so this amount must not be labeled unrestricted bank cash. The approximately ₹1,000 crore expansion plan is only partly spent. [GVT_CALL27]

At ₹4,235, the FY26 reported EPS of ₹48.16 implies about **87.9× FY26 earnings**. This is a historical-earnings comparison, not a full current diluted valuation or FY27 estimate. The annual report and current operating disclosure were reviewed, but a complete current capital-change sweep was not performed. Technical capability and backlog visibility support the business case. They do not remove contract economics, margin normalization or parent-related value-transfer questions. [GVT_AR26]

**Judgment:** no new purchase conclusion from the bounded comparator. Do not promote a high-voltage demand story directly into a buy at this price.

## 8. Milestones and falsifiers

These are analyst-defined observables, not management promises.

| Horizon from November 2026 | Evidence that should improve confidence | Evidence that changes or breaks the thesis |
|---|---|---|
| **One year** | New capacity commissioning, conductor volume recovery, premium mix, ordinary profit excluding tariff refunds, supplier-finance reconciliation and WC release | Revenue remains metal-led while volumes stagnate, oil spreads normalize without compensation, or cash funding rises faster than capacity utilization |
| **Three years** | Actual return on the FY26/FY27 capex and QIP, repeated utility qualification, cable/export customer retention, per-share earnings and cumulative cash growth | Book equity grows through issuance while EPS and cash per share fail to improve, or capacity is filled only by lower-margin orders |
| **Five years** | Repeat upgrades and export wins produce cash-backed earnings through a weaker commodity period, with financing and dividends reconciled | Temporary shortage margins accounted for most of the apparent improvement, or terminal earnings require another unusually favorable cycle |
| **Ten years** | A demonstrated institution can replace leadership, commercialize new products and compound per-share owner economics beyond the original grid buildout | Mature grid additions slow without replacement/service economics, technology is commoditized, or capital allocation absorbs the accumulated gains |

This is a precise reopen rule, not an excuse to defer the current price judgment.
