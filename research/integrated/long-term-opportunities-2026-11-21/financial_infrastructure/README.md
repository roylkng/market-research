# Financial infrastructure: a credible structural opportunity with a demanding entry price

Research date: 21 November 2026. Price date: official NSE close, 20 November 2026. All financial amounts are INR crore unless stated. This is a long-term company assessment and reverse valuation, not a portfolio allocation or trade instruction.

## Decision

**CAMS is the strongest researched structural candidate in this comparison. At ₹724.65 it has credible conditional buy potential, but the currently evidenced economics do not establish a comfortable valuation margin.** Its price requires successful productivity improvements, profitable diversification and continued savings inflows. Those outcomes are plausible. They are not already earned.

The crucial distinction is between a weak business and a temporarily investment-heavy cash statement. CAMS is not rejected because its reported trailing cash yield is low. September trailing cash after all technology capex and lease payments was ₹311.62cr. The same calculation before working-capital movements was ₹384.85cr. The ₹73.23cr difference is working capital, not an economic collapse. A favorable ₹400cr starting cash normalization is therefore bounded, roughly 4% above the latter diagnostic. It still needs future growth to fund its own working capital.

At ₹400cr starting cash, a ten-year illustration using 0.5% annual dilution, ₹650cr provisional opening treasury credit and a 25× terminal cash multiple requires 13.28% annual company cash growth to produce a 10% annual investor return. That is 12.72% cash growth per diluted share. It is plausible at the favorable end of current business evidence. It is not a fully funded forecast, nor a universal return hurdle.

## 1. Where the long-term opportunity actually comes from

AMFI records ₹16.29 lakh crore of mutual-fund AUM in October 2016 and ₹83.39 lakh crore in October 2026. The arithmetic is 5.12× and 17.74% annual growth. October 2026 gross SIP collections were ₹32,160cr, equity net inflows were ₹32,993.64cr and all-scheme net inflows were ₹173,828.98cr. The latter includes large debt flows and is not all household equity saving. There were 28.78cr folios. Folios and SIP accounts are not unique people. [S5, S6, S11]

The causal chain is:

**Household income and investible savings → distribution and repeat contributions → net fund inflows → AUM plus investment returns → fees at a negotiated yield → cash after service, compliance and technology costs.**

Every arrow can weaken. Asset appreciation is not new saving. Gross SIP collections are not net flows. Retail adoption is not automatically pricing power. The investment opportunity lies in operating platforms that can process larger balances and transaction volumes with a slower increase in total cost, while keeping clients and protecting the shareholder's claim.

The next decade can extend formalization through digital distribution, recurring payments, retirement and insurance records, and alternatives administration. The more concrete near-term beneficiaries are CAMS's existing RTA, payments and KRA operations. Account aggregation and insurance digitization remain smaller options. They receive no separate speculative option value in this calculation.

Passive investing illustrates the tension. AMFI reports passive AUM of ₹14.79 lakh crore in October. On the October 30 call CAMS management said debt fees around 2–2.25 basis points can be replaced in the mix by passive fees around 0.5 basis points. Management also described about 2% normal telescopic pricing erosion and about 3% total annual yield erosion in the current quarter. Greater adoption can coexist with less revenue per rupee serviced. [S5, S8]

## 3. CAMS: why the incumbent position may last

MF recordkeeping has embedded transaction histories, interfaces with asset managers and distributors, regulatory obligations and operating continuity requirements. Migration affects a critical business process. Scale spreads technology and compliance costs across more clients and balances. This supports client retention and an economic advantage, but does not eliminate negotiation pressure from large asset managers or competition from KFin. [S1, S3, S8]

CAMS's FY26 revenue mix was 74% asset-based MF fees, 12% other MF services and 14% non-MF. Its industry position therefore provides exposure to financial savings, with substantial sensitivity to market values and fee yield. The Q2 deck's 67% share and ₹58.4 lakh crore serviced assets refer to the company's quarterly asset measure. They should not be divided by AMFI's October closing AUM. [S1, S3]

The moats must be tested through retained mandates, realized yield and cost per unit. Management's claims that large-scale in-housing would be expensive are economically coherent, but they are not independent customer testimony. Likewise, a joint industry platform such as MFC Technologies can improve service and preserve relevance without necessarily creating a new high-margin revenue stream.

### Growth required versus growth evidenced

The following are explicit mechanism scenarios using the FY26 revenue weights. They are not revenue forecasts.

| Scenario | Serviced AUM growth | Fee-yield change | Other MF growth | Non-MF growth | Resulting revenue growth |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Weak market | 6% | −3% | 4% | 12% | 4.3% |
| Steady adoption | 12% | −3% | 8% | 18% | 9.9% |
| Strong adoption | 14% | −3% | 10% | 20% | 11.8% |
| Very strong | 17% | −2% | 12% | 25% | 15.8% |

Asset-linked revenue growth is `(1 + AUM growth) × (1 + yield change) − 1`, not AUM growth minus a percentage-point fee. AUM assumptions already include both net flows and market performance. No separate market appreciation is added again.

The price-implied 13.28% cash growth can exceed 10–12% revenue growth if the platform becomes more productive and today's investment program matures. Management reports headcount declining from about 8,450 at the start of FY27 to about 8,200 in Q2, with roughly 30% of the platform transformation completed. It also reports non-MF margins improving to about 18–19%, with a three-year aspiration of 25% or higher. These are early supporting indicators and issuer targets. They do not prove a ten-year cash-growth path. Management explicitly said old and new systems coexist, so AI is not immediately eliminating total costs. [S8]

The bridge deliberately retains all software spending. A physical-asset-light company can still require substantial digital investment. The zero-working-capital row helps assess timing, but it does not justify assuming that future expansion needs no working capital. [S1, S2]

Reported FY26 ROE was about 39%. FY22 to FY26 split-adjusted basic EPS rose from ₹11.75 to ₹19.23, about 13.1% annually across four elapsed years. Those figures establish historical profitability. They do not measure the return on the next rupee retained. The newer businesses provide mixed evidence. FY26 subsidiary results include losses of about ₹2.70cr at Fintuple, ₹5.00cr at Think Analytics India, ₹3.06cr at the account-aggregator subsidiary and ₹5.99cr at the insurance repository. Project-level returns on the platform transformation are not disclosed in the reviewed originals. [S1]

Capital allocation is partly disciplined and partly still being demonstrated. FY26 cash dividends were ₹304.53cr, about 75% of selected cash. Cash spent on PPE and software increased. H1 FY27 also used ₹18.68cr for subsidiary interests and ₹3.50cr for the joint venture. These are separate uses and are not silently treated as recurring free cash. KRA's ₹7cr acquisition of NSE's KRA business is a small adjacent transaction. The platform remains primarily funded internally, with only ₹0.24cr of September borrowing. [S1, S2]

September gross cash, current investments and other bank balances totaled ₹875.10cr. That is not all unrestricted excess cash. Client collection and unpaid dividend balances recorded in other current assets are excluded. The ₹3.25 dividend had a November 13 record date, so a November 20 buyer is not entitled to it. The reverse model uses only ₹650cr of opening treasury credit, allowing for this roughly ₹80.74cr commitment, operating liquidity and other claims. This ₹650cr is an analyst assumption, not an audited distributable amount. Future lease payments are deducted from cash, so lease debt is not also deducted in full from opening value.

CAMS split one ₹10 share into five ₹2 shares effective December 5, 2025. The March 2026 issued shares plus H1 employee allotments give 248,418,378 shares at September 30. That date is explicit. No unverified claim is made that it is the exact November 20 denominator. H1 had no new option grants and 433,880 issued employee shares. The model allows 0.5% dilution each year. It does not simultaneously subtract the same stock compensation as cash or credit future exercise proceeds. [S1, S2]

## 5. Price and what must happen

| Observed ₹311.62cr | 25× | 14.37% | 16.49% | 18.61% |

All annual cash in this reverse calculation must already be after required reinvestment, working capital, tax and leases. It is assumed distributed at year end. Opening treasury is credited once and distributions are not added again to terminal value. The terminal multiple applies to year-ten cash. The method answers what cash growth the price requires. It does not prove that growth can be funded.

|---|---:|---:|---:|

This is a genuine valuation range with economically different assumptions. ₹724.65 is defensible if productivity, fee discipline and adjacent businesses deliver strongly. It is not justified by adoption alone. A price near ₹550–600, with unchanged fundamentals, would reopen the decision under less demanding assumptions. It is not a standing buy order. Conversely, evidence of sustained cash productivity could improve the decision at the current price without waiting for that range.

## 6. Adverse history and permanent-loss mechanisms

AMFI's history records sluggish industry AUM during 2010–2013 following the financial crisis and the abolition of entry load. Growing long-term savings demand did not prevent a difficult multi-year adjustment in intermediary economics. This is an adverse-regime diagnostic, not a blind backtest. [S12]

CAMS itself demonstrates nonlinearity. FY23 PAT was ₹285.25cr versus ₹286.95cr in FY22. FY26 PAT rose only about 1.2% despite the multi-year structural story. The correct lesson is to underwrite weak years and pricing changes, not to project the decade's AUM CAGR directly into earnings. [S1]

Permanent loss can arise from an operational or cyber failure that damages client trust, regulator-driven pricing or capital obligations, large AMC repricing, technology costs that persist without productivity, or capital spent on unrelated expansion. A prolonged weak market can combine lower AUM, weaker transaction activity and margin pressure. A high entry multiple adds a separate source of loss even if the business survives.

For CDSL, the latest signed report discloses an adverse arbitral award involving alleged misuse of client securities by former DP Anugrah and alleged negligence by CDSL. The award is challenged in the Bombay High Court, management believes it has a good case, and no financial impact is booked. This is a pending claim, not a finding of misconduct by this research. It demonstrates that regulated infrastructure still has operational and legal exposure. [S9]

## 7. Management, controls and observables

Anuj Kumar and the management team have delivered a profitable core franchise and make the pricing and transformation risks reasonably explicit in the current call. FY26 MD cash remuneration was ₹7.26cr, plus ₹3.08cr of accounting stock-option expense. These disclosures allow monitoring, but are not proof of incentive quality or integrity. The annual audit includes an exception for backups at one subsidiary and two step-down subsidiaries beginning only on May 15, 2025. The report states no detected audit-trail tampering. The correct judgment is a bounded review with a compliance follow-up, not a declaration of flawless controls. [S1, S8]

There is a specific execution transition to monitor. Chief Platform Officer Ravi Kethana resigned on September 18, with departure stated for December 10, 2026 or another mutually agreed date. The filing says he is pursuing the next phase of his professional journey. This is not evidence of wrongdoing. It does increase the importance of succession and delivery evidence while the investment case depends on a multi-year platform transformation. A successor was not verified in this bounded review. [S13]

### Data quality notes

AMFI's evolution page describes ₹16.29 to ₹83.39 lakh crore as about sixfold. The reproducible arithmetic is 5.12×. Its five-year wording similarly rounds aggressively. The annual CAMS chart labels some CAGRs differently from a four-elapsed-year computation across FY22 to FY26. This report computes from endpoints. The AMFI monthly note also has inconsistent wording around its monthly AUM-versus-flow chart. No exact market-return decomposition is claimed from that chart. Raw closing AUM, AAUM and reported flows remain distinct. This prevents polished summary language from replacing arithmetic.
