# Accelya Solutions India: the dividend does not settle the cash question

**Decision: conditional PASS_CURRENT_PRICE, with income-alternative status, at ₹974.75, the official 19 November 2026 NSE close.** Research completed on 20 November 2026. This is a priced decision on one selected company, not a claim of whole-market coverage or an instruction to trade. [S8]

The useful question was whether an established software business returning cash could offer a stronger alternative to acquisition-dependent growth. Accelya qualifies for that comparison. It does not yet justify calling its historical **8.21% declared dividend yield** a sustainable yield for a new owner. The working case produces approximately **₹105.79cr recurring owner cash, ₹70.88 per share**, before exceptional cash funding and a liquidity reserve. The five-year modeled return is **5.92% at a 12× exit** and **8.58% at 14×**. Keeping the current normalized owner-cash multiple unchanged gives **8.26%**. These are conditional calculations without probabilities. [S1-S5, MODEL]

The listed business has useful airline revenue-accounting, assurance and cost-management products. Airline settlement complexity, legacy integration and the migration toward order accounting create an actual customer need. They also require continuous software work. The issuer describes long contracts and recurring services. It does not provide a usable contract-expiry ladder or independently verified retention rate in the reviewed material. The 4 May 2023 original disclosure of one Air India service agreement's nonrenewal, effective after December 2023, is a concrete historical reminder that integration does not make every contract permanent. It is not a new FY27 loss. [S1 printed74-81, S7]

Parent-wide FLX/One Order announcements cannot be treated as wholly owned listed-India economics. Consolidated Note33 records **₹152.68cr services rendered to related entities, 28.69% of revenue**, and ₹107.00cr services received. The BRSR's 41.44% related-party sales reconciles to the standalone boundary: ₹152.68cr plus ₹65.53cr services to its owned Americas subsidiary, divided by ₹526.57cr standalone sales. Consolidation eliminates the subsidiary transaction. The two percentages are therefore not contradictory or interchangeable. Management says the transactions are at arm's length, and the auditor describes testing cost-plus related-party revenue and transfer-pricing analysis. That supports an audited framework. It does not establish that every future allocation of group opportunities or fees will favor listed minority owners. [S1 notes33 and audit KAM, S4]

The concrete cash exposure is more informative than a generic governance label. Related-party billed and unbilled receivables reached **₹77.64cr versus₹33.17cr**, an increase of ₹44.46cr. They were **63.6% of consolidated trade receivables including unbilled amounts**. These balances are disclosed as unsecured and interest-free. The company recorded no related-party impairment. Total receivables increased from ₹91.08cr to ₹122.12cr, while balances less than six months overdue rose from ₹19.47cr to ₹55.01cr. These are collection and contracting questions, not proof of diversion. One customer exceeded 10% of revenue, but the disclosed text does not identify it. [S1 notes9,31-33, MODEL]

The 23 October AGM approved the relevant FY27 related-party transactions with promoter votes excluded from the interested resolutions. FY27 approval limits are capacity to transact, not revenue forecasts. The parent owns 74.66% and received ₹94.72cr of FY26 dividends. This creates a shared incentive for distributions while leaving minority holders dependent on the prices and terms charged within the wider group. [S1,S4]

## 3. Dividend timing, current shares and treasury

**The share denominator is 14,926,261.** Capital of ₹14.9269cr includes ₹0.0006cr originally paid on forfeited shares. Dividing that capital line by ₹10 would slightly overstate shares. The annual EPS note has no dilutive shares and October AGM voting confirms the exact denominator. The reviewed current source set establishes no new capital issuance. [S1 notes14,27, S4]

The ₹80 declared FY26 dividend comprises ₹45 interim and ₹35 final. FY26 cash dividends of ₹126.87cr instead represent the prior ₹40 final plus the current ₹45 interim. These are different periods. ## 4. Funded forward scenarios

All numbers here are assumptions informed by the originals, not forecasts or assigned likelihoods. Cash taxes are approximated at 26% of operating EBITDA plus rent income less financing lease cash and ₹13.23cr starting nonlease depreciation. Working capital includes receivables, contract assets and net contract-fulfilment funding. No acquisition or outside financing is assumed. [MODEL]

The downside combines client/parent-channel pressure, lower margin, stubborn spending and an additional ₹15cr collection loss. It is a joint economic stress, not a confidence interval. The working case grants the lease reset and a substantial working-capital normalization relative to FY26's ₹43.27cr absorption. Its ₹5cr annual funding is an analyst assumption, not a verified forecast. At 2% growth, maintaining approximately the existing receivable and contract-asset proportions requires materially less funding than repeating the FY26 increase each year. ₹5cr allows some additional collection friction. Ordinary contract-cost amortization is already included in the operating margin, so it would be wrong to require the entire FY26 net asset increase perpetually as maintenance. The upside needs sales and margin improvement alongside better cash conversion. The last reported quarter has not established that combination. [S1,S2, MODEL]

The first-year gratuity and EPFO funding totals ₹16.59cr in every scenario before the downside's extra collection loss. Every scenario remains funded under its stated cash path, with no new debt or equity. Distributions are modeled as 90% of cash above the rebuilt reserve, and retained surplus is credited once at exit. The reserve itself is not returned at exit. These are cash-allocation assumptions, not a forecast dividend schedule. Personal dividend taxes, transaction costs and fill prices are not modeled. [MODEL]

### Independent review changed the normalization

The first draft charged ₹15cr of recurring working-capital/contract funding every year. Independent review correctly challenged that against only about ₹11cr incremental annual sales. Repeating an implementation-heavy year can create a false negative just as ignoring cash costs can create a false positive. The central budget is now ₹5cr, with 0/5/15 sensitivities and joint favorable cash/multiple scenarios visible below. This correction raised the working return range from 3.5-6.2% to 5.9-8.6%. It did not create a guaranteed dividend or an automatic BUY.

The no-compression case is a credible income alternative. This conditional PASS means the current combination of price and risk is less compelling than the strongest opportunities in the active research queue. It does not mean the business is categorically uninvestable. The return range can be acceptable for an investor with different income needs or risk preferences, neither of which is established by this case alone. The decision is not a mechanical 15% cutoff. A current-price buyer can reasonably obtain a different result if owner cash and growth are stronger than these working assumptions. That must be an economic case, rather than treating a previous dividend as an earned coupon. [MODEL]

For comparison, the working 12× cash-flow path has present values **₹894, ₹824, ₹762 and ₹679 per share** at 8%,10%,12% and15%. These are rate sensitivities. They are neither simultaneous fair values nor automatic trading targets. [MODEL]

## 5. Ten lenses and precise reopening routes

| Lens | Status | Decision-relevant evidence or limit |
|---|---|---|
| Market and alternatives | MIXED | Airline volume and accounting modernization support demand. Listed-company growth remains modest. The case must compete with other researched businesses and diversified exposure |

| Capital allocation | MIXED | Dividends are real, but paid cash exceeded selected post-investment cash in both comparative years. Software, leases and gratuity have funding claims first [S1,S3] |
| Expectations and valuation | COMPLETE, conditional | Official price, current denominator, funded scenarios and multiple sensitivities. Historical dividend yield does not equal future owner yield [S8, MODEL] |
| Timing and portfolio fit | MIXED | No portfolio suitability or sizing conclusion. ₹1.57cr single-day turnover is not a reliable multi-day liquidity limit. Research-only [S8] |

**Price route:** reopen if price reaches roughly ₹825-895 while the working cash budget remains intact, corresponding to the 8-10% comparison-rate range for the working 12× case. A fall in price accompanied by a weaker business does not satisfy this route.

**Same-price route:** demonstrate a funded recurring cash path around ₹115-120cr and rising, attributable to the listed entity, with collections and parent charges consistent with that cash. The upside assumptions provide one explicit example. Existing evidence could settle the question if it reconciles the economics. No arbitrary two-quarter waiting rule is added.

**Deterioration route:** sustained operating margin near 31%, further parent receivable growth without cash recovery, or higher required software/lease funding pushes the current case further from purchase. The next useful evidence is the collected-cash and funding bridge, not another parent-wide product announcement.
