# Accelya independent review: normalization improved, conditional PASS survives

**Review date: 20 November 2026. Price: ₹974.75, official 19 November close.**

The objective was to challenge the cash economics and the rejection, including a reasonable terminal multiple. **The initial working case was unnecessarily conservative on recurring working-capital funding. The author accepted the correction. The revised conditional PASS remains defensible, but this is an income alternative with plausible 8–10% scenario returns, not a business proved to be uninvestable.** No universal return hurdle or scenario probability is imposed.

## 1. Material revision produced by the review

The original model charged ₹15cr of working-capital and net contract-fulfilment funding every year, growing 2%. Year two would consume ₹15.30cr against only ₹11.01cr incremental sales. It would consume ₹78.06cr over five years. FY26's ₹10.87cr increase in contract-fulfilment assets and difficult collections explain a near-term burden. They do not establish that the full asset build recurs indefinitely. Ordinary contract-cost amortization is already represented in operating costs. [S1 notes7,13, consolidated cash flow, MODEL]

The revised central assumption is **₹5cr annual funding**, also growing 2%. For context, June trade receivables, contract assets and contract-fulfilment assets less contract liabilities total approximately ₹155.02cr. A proportional 2% increase is approximately ₹3.10cr before other working-capital movements. ₹5cr therefore allows friction beyond this simple proportional calculation. It remains an assumption, since cash collection after June is not disclosed. ₹15cr is retained as a drag sensitivity. [S1 notes7,9,13,21, MODEL]

This correction raises first-year recurring owner cash from **₹95.79cr to ₹105.79cr** and the 12× working return from **3.54% to 5.92%**. The earlier result should not be presented as the final central case.

## 2. Independent return calculation

The separate calculator uses the retained input snapshots and does not import or execute the author's code. It solves IRR in log-discount space and independently reconstructs the cash reserve, distributions, terminal business and residual cash. It reproduces all three original and all three revised scenario IRRs to less than 1e-12 absolute error.

Holding the former 15.19× after improving normalized cash is **multiple expansion**. For example, it produces 10.03% with ₹5cr funding and ₹18cr leases, but that improved cash starts at only 13.75×. Labeling 15.19× an unchanged multiple would falsely improve the comparison.

The revised downside and upside remain **−18.11% and 13.31%**. They are joint scenarios, not confidence bounds. Zero incremental contract funding despite growing sales is favorable. The ₹12.24cr lease amount covers current contractual payments, not all future renewals. The 10.68% upper sensitivity is visible and should not be hidden, but neither assumption is established as a durable run rate.

## 3. Cash and obligation checks

**Dividend entitlement and treasury are handled correctly.** The ₹35 final dividend had record date 9 October and scheduled payment 17 November. A new buyer at the reviewed price is not entitled to it. June unencumbered treasury before operating needs was ₹59.04cr, against ₹52.24cr committed to that dividend. The ₹6.80cr residual is not free surplus after all operating, tax and employee needs. The model credits no opening surplus and rebuilds ₹20cr liquidity. This is conservative because July-November cash may have rebuilt it. If ₹20cr operating cash were already available, the revised 14× return becomes **8.88% versus 8.58%**. No current balance sheet establishes that amount. [S1 notes10–12, S2, S3, MODEL]

**Historical payout was larger than selected cash in both years.** Cash after full PPE, software and financing-classified lease payments was ₹113.55cr in FY25 and ₹44.45cr in FY26, covering 84.5% and 35.0% of cash dividends paid. The weak FY26 number is not used as perpetual earnings. Conversely, an ₹80 historical declared dividend does not become a sustainable contractual yield. [S1 cash statements, MODEL]

**Leases are not double charged.** The ₹10.78cr short-term lease expense is already in operating expenses. Forward financing lease cash is a separate deduction. The old ₹15.70cr liability is not deducted again after funding future lease cash. Lease terminations and ₹15.16cr extension-option present value make both a lower recurring budget and renewal risk credible. [S1 note30]

**Gratuity funding is included once.** FY27 expected contribution of ₹17.4126cr less ordinary gratuity expense of ₹3.5458cr gives ₹13.8668cr additional first-year funding. The ordinary expense is already represented in the margin. Actual July-November contributions are unknown. The EPFO principal of ₹2.7250894cr is separately reserved once, with interest and damages unresolved rather than assumed zero. [S1 note29, S5]

## 4. Related-party scope and the strongest remaining risk

The 41.44% BRSR number is reconciled. Standalone related-party service revenue is ₹218.2142cr, including ₹65.5315cr rendered to the owned Americas subsidiary. Dividing by ₹526.5691cr standalone revenue produces **41.44075%**. Consolidation eliminates that owned-subsidiary transaction. External group services of ₹152.6827cr divided by ₹532.2666cr consolidated revenue produce **28.68538%**. There is no unresolved numerical contradiction. [S1 BRSR, standalone note33, consolidated note33]

The economically important exposure remains. Related billed and unbilled receivables rose from **₹33.17cr to ₹77.64cr**, up **134.0%**, and equal **63.6% of total consolidated trade receivables including unbilled amounts**. Consolidated management fees increased **20.60%, from ₹29.6910cr to ₹35.8078cr**, while FY26 sales grew 0.67%. Audited arm's-length assertions and approved transactions are evidence of a framework, not a guarantee of favorable future economics for minority shareholders. These facts support collection and allocation risk. They do not prove diversion. [S1 notes9,26,33, MODEL]

The strongest counter-thesis is that implementation funding and lease disruption have peaked, existing integrations keep customers, and cash generation returns toward FY25's level. The revised normalization and joint sensitivities give that thesis meaningful credit. At current price, stronger listed-entity growth or recurring cash around ₹115–120cr would materially strengthen it. Current Q1 sales growth of 1.06%, payroll growth above sales and lower operating margin do not establish that recovery. [S1,S2, MODEL]

## 5. Disposition and reproducibility

**Conditional PASS survives 14× and genuinely unchanged normalized entry multiples.** Its rationale is the price paid for modest evidenced growth and concentrated group collection/allocation exposure. It cannot rest solely on a 12× exit or on repeating a weak working-capital year forever. Investors explicitly seeking an 8–9% income-oriented return could reasonably reach another portfolio decision. Portfolio suitability is not assessed here.

The useful reopening evidence is a listed-entity bridge of collections, contract funding, parent fees and recurring lease/software spending. Existing evidence can settle the economics if sufficient. No fixed two-quarter wait is required.
