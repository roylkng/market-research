## Operating hypothesis

Assume partial recovery in commercial CMS shipment timing after a weak Q2 while retaining input-cost and operating-cost pressure. The base is below the H1 average revenue and owner PAT. A single prior December decline is too little evidence for fixed seasonality in a project-driven CDMO. Conversely, an immediate return to the unusually strong Q1 is also unsupported.

EBIT is after depreciation and excludes other income. Owner PAT = (revenue × EBIT margin + other income − finance costs) × (1 − tax rate) − NCI. Associates and new exceptional items are zero assumptions. Scenarios are uncalibrated sensitivities with no assigned probabilities.

- **Revenue:** 530 cr, below H1 average 545.617 cr and between the two observed quarters. The recovery is attributed to established commercial molecules, not new clinical successes.
- **EBIT margin:** 22%, between Q2 approximately 15.30% and Q1 approximately 30.60%, below H1 blended 24.30%. More shipment throughput partly absorbs fixed costs while input/freight pressure persists.
- **Other Income:** 7.5 cr near Q2. No return from acquired Kakinada land assumed.
- **Finance Cost:** 8 cr versus Q2 7.3073 cr, allowing financing and commissioning costs.
- **Tax Rate:** 25.3%, close to current H1 effective rate.
- **NCI:** Zero. The signed statement identifies both included subsidiaries as wholly owned.

## Decisive unknowns

- Shipment timing, commercial-molecule customer concentration and product mix are not disclosed at the resolution needed for a December schedule.
- RFPs and early clinical projects do not equal near-term revenue. No specific approvals or new commercial launches are assumed.
- Geopolitical input/freight costs, new R&D staffing and depreciation may offset higher throughput.
- Management commentary uses standalone headlines. All model financial anchors are from the consolidated signed results.
