# MEDIASSIST | 20 November 2026 | Close the cash case, pass at the reference price

**Decision: PASS_CURRENT_PRICE.** Reopen on a sufficiently better offered price or a demonstrably stronger owner-cash path. **NSE EQ / INE456Z01021, ₹279.80 original 19 November close**, verified by the root researcher against the original NSE final bhavcopy. This is a reference price, not a 20 November executable quote. The five-year working scenario yields **3.49% annually**, with **−16.28% downside** and **17.25% upside** scenarios. No probability is assigned. Conditional working-case prices for 8%, 10%, 12% and 15% returns are **₹228.39, ₹209.38, ₹192.31 and ₹169.82**. These are sensitivities, not portfolio hurdle policy or standing orders.

The strongest counter-thesis is credible. A scaled claims administrator could convert its existing insurer relationships into a higher-margin technology platform, restore retention after Paramount integration and spend less on development than this model assumes. We have corroborated the Star Health platform relationship with **Star Health's own exchange disclosure**, not just Medi Assist's marketing. The counterweight is economic size. Platform/HBA was only **4.6% of Q2 revenue**, while the **91.0% India TPA business grew just 1.2%**. It is premature to value the whole group as high-growth software.

**Method:** trailing = FY26 + H1 FY27 − H1 FY26. The prior H1 residual here includes ₹9.020cr nonlease financing cash. This is why it differs from the prior note's ₹−49.467cr pre-interest diagnostic. It is an explicit scope addition, not a correction of that arithmetic. The ₹7.622cr H1 minority-stake acquisition remains an ownership purchase, not recurring software maintenance. [MA03, MA04]

Three decision consequences follow.

1. H1 alone understates the annual collection cycle. The business produced ₹84.198cr of trailing cash after the specified investment, lease and financing uses.
2. Trailing cash taxes were only ₹12.484cr. FY26 H2 included a **₹14.054cr net cash tax refund**, inferred from the annual and H1 cash statements. The source of each refund is not separately established. This low trailing cash tax must not be assumed permanent.
3. The ₹106.748cr trailing owner PAT contains the prior-year **₹32.868cr deferred-tax adjustment associated with the Paramount business transfer**. Mechanically removing just that adjustment leaves ₹73.880cr. That is **not fully normalized PAT**, because exceptional operating costs, other income, financing, minority effects and future tax treatment also need separate treatment. It explains why 19.58× reported trailing PAT is an incomplete valuation shorthand. [MA03 note 10, MA04 note 53]

Trailing net working-capital cash was approximately **−₹1.693cr**, despite large seasonal gross receivable movements. H1 receivable absorption of ₹73.731cr is therefore not proof that annual cash conversion has collapsed. Conversely, contract liabilities of ₹321.676cr represent unearned services and are not free surplus cash. Self-funded client schemes are intermediated, with restricted receivable/payable and bank balances netted where legally enforceable in the annual accounts. We do not count those client funds as owner treasury or mechanically treat all service liabilities as financial debt. [MA03, MA04 notes 12, 17, 18]

Cash taxes are 25% of EBITDA less lease cash and assumed deductible depreciation of 4% of revenue. This does not automatically give the acquired customer-relationship amortization a perpetual cash tax shield. Cash tax depreciation is uncertain, so 2% and 6% alternatives are retained. Employee and credit-loss expenses remain in EBITDA. There is no stock-compensation or bad-debt addback. A 0.5% group-cash minority allowance approximates residual international minority exposure. Mayfair ownership became **91.75% on 1 July 2026**, rather than 100%. [MA03 note 12]

The working 5.5% investment ratio is near actual trailing 5.83% and pays for both software development and other assets. No unverified growth-capex label is used to manufacture high distributable cash. FY26 direct software additions were ₹6.580cr and development additions ₹15.574cr, while total cash spending was broader. The auditor identified capitalization and impairment as judgment-heavy audit matters and reported no significant exceptions from its procedures. That supports the accounting process within its scope, not product returns or negligible maintenance needs. [MA04 notes 7 and audit report]

The working case already requires TPA growth to recover from Q2's 1.2%, and margins to approach management's **22–23% core ambition**. The upside combines stronger retention, platform scale, efficient reinvestment and a higher exit valuation. It is a coherent favorable combination, not an expected outcome. [MA01, MA05]

**Exit sensitivity matters.** A 14× multiple means a 7.14% terminal cash yield. For comparison, the issuer's own goodwill impairment model uses a 14.65% post-tax discount rate, 5% terminal growth and 13.22% budgeted EBITDA growth. Its terminal-rate pair implies about 10.88× next-period cash. That accounting test is not a market target, but it prevents treating 14× as obviously conservative. Our working 10×, 12×, 16× and 18× alternatives yield **−1.58%, 1.08%, 5.68% and 7.70%**. No verified peer premium is claimed. [MA04 note 6]

## Treasury, buyback and dilution count once

The model starts with **₹229.347cr** of visible September current investments, cash and other bank balances. From that it reserves the March restricted-deposit proxy ₹14.515cr, current tax ₹10.456cr, declared September dividend about ₹14.940cr, assumed completed buyback ₹49.266cr plus ₹1cr costs, operating cash ₹40cr and the prior annual unfunded defined-benefit obligation proxy ₹49.051cr. This leaves **₹50.119cr** of working-case initial surplus treasury. Carrying March restriction and gratuity amounts into September is explicitly an estimate. [MA03, MA04 notes 12, 20, 30]

The issuer deck's broader ₹268.3cr cash figure is not silently substituted for current surplus. Noncurrent liquidity components and the ₹89.831cr tax asset receive no base value without a dated realization bridge. Crucially, the ₹42.026cr unspent preferential proceeds are **not deducted again**, because the model pays for future development and other investment. The old minority acquisition is already reflected in September balances.

Full buyback completion is conditional. Cash and **1,159,199 retired shares** are adjusted together. Remaining options are approximated as March 1,266,877 less H1 92,415 exercised, giving **1,174,462**. Fully adding them without credit for exercise cash is a conservative dilution sensitivity, not a claim all are presently in the money. The modeled denominator is **74,717,105 shares** after buyback and that dilution. Future compensation stays an expense. [MA02, MA03, MA04 note 31]

Half the modeled annual owner cash plus treasury yield is distributed and half retained at a 3% after-tax treasury yield. The issuer's policy is **about half of cumulative adjusted FCF over rolling five years**, not a guaranteed annual dividend. We therefore test no annual payout and full payout. Their working returns are 3.46% and 3.51%, close to 3.49%. Retained cash is added once at exit, and treasury income is excluded from the operating exit multiple. There is no new shareholder tender entitlement. [MA01, MA05]

Caution is not what creates the weak result. Releasing all restriction, operating-cash and legacy-gratuity reserves raises the working return to **4.52%**. Crediting the whole tax asset as immediate cash as well raises it to **5.38%**. Removing option dilution gives **3.83%**. Assuming no buyback gives **3.66%**. These favorable sensitivities still require operating improvement to reach a stronger return.

## Reopening rule and what would falsify this pass

At the same ₹279.80 reference, proportionately scaling the working operating-cash path while retaining the same 14× exit and treasury/distribution assumptions requires approximately **₹120.55cr in year one and ₹176.19cr in year five for 10% annualized return**. For 12%, the corresponding cash path is ₹131.44cr to ₹192.11cr. These are reverse sensitivities, not forecasts or a claim that only one cash path can work.

A same-price reopening is justified if original results and commercial evidence show that recovering retention, profitable platform revenue and actual collections can support that larger cash generation after taxes, leases and development. For example, capex at 3.5% of sales rather than5.5% alone yields only7.72% at the working14× multiple. No single favorable headline currently closes the gap.

The highest-value next check is **the next meaningful same-scope collections, retention and development-spend disclosure**, including customer renewal evidence. There is no automatic requirement to wait for two future quarters if a material original disclosure resolves the uncertainty earlier. At roughly **₹210–230**, a stable working operating case warrants renewed review for the10–8% sensitivity band, with governance and funding evidence reassessed. A lower price accompanied by renewed churn or impaired customer relationships is not the same opportunity.

In contrast, persistent retention below90%, recurring claim losses, platform growth without cash conversion, or material adverse regulatory findings would weaken the investment case. The five-year downside is not a maximum drawdown or loss floor.
