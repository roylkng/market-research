# Epigral: an underwritable expansion case, but a modest cushion at the offered price

Research date: 19 November 2026. Reference: official NSE 18 November close **₹944.30**, EPIGRAL / INE071N01016 / EQ. All amounts are INR crore unless stated. Research judgment only. No position sizing or order is implied.

## Decision

**WATCH_PRICE, with a conditional research entry range of ₹800–875 and a route to reopen at the same price after profitable ramp evidence.** This is a completed price and business judgment. It is not a blanket demand for more documents.

The working funding scenario produces approximately **11.17–12.05% annualized return** at ₹944.30, depending on whether 75% or 100% of forecast retained net cash is ultimately valued for outside shareholders. Financing availability is an explicit scenario condition, detailed below. At ₹850 the same assumptions give **13.39–14.29%**. A holder comfortable with an approximately 10% target and meaningful chemical-cycle risk could reasonably accept the current price. I do not judge the present cushion compelling enough for a high-conviction concentrated purchase. This is a judgment about the risk and return combination, not an unstated universal 15% hurdle.

The strongest favorable case is substantial installed capacity entering production, relatively modest starting leverage and an integrated chlorine chain that can earn both upstream and downstream economic contributions. The strongest opposing case is simultaneous rival capacity, lower-margin new epoxy sales and cash being consumed before utilization is established. The model's allocated turnover is not a verified bridge to management's consolidated revenue ambition. Two factual corrections prevent the simple cheap-P/E narrative from becoming the investment thesis.

1. The correct mechanical trailing owner PAT is **₹284.88cr**, implying **14.30×**, rather than the screen's **12.36× doubled H1 EPS**. The ₹80.87cr deferred-tax release in the preceding year is in both FY26 and preceding H1, so it cancels in the proper trailing calculation. It must not be subtracted again.
2. H1 cash after the reported PPE, finance-charge and lease cash lines is **₹24.73cr**, falling to **₹19.42cr** after the separate renewable-SPV investment. The cash is positive but does not establish a large distributable yield. [S1, S2]

## What is already priced, and what could differ

At 4,31,41,338 shares, market capitalization is **₹4,073.84cr**. The exact count is from the audited FY26 report and June shareholding return, with unchanged rounded September share capital. A separate November capital audit has not been obtained. The 18 November session traded 12,154 shares, **₹1.1412cr** of turnover. One session is not an average liquidity estimate or executable fill. [S1, S2, S6, S13]

The model charges the remaining investment cash, tax, working capital, financing and leases before valuing equity. To earn approximately 12% at the offered price under the working sales trajectory, a 7× terminal post-lease EBITDA multiple and full retained-cash value, the existing business must sustain roughly **20.95% EBITDA margin**. The corresponding requirements at 10% and 15% are **19.23%** and **23.81%**. These are reverse calculations, not forecasts.

The business can be attractive without returning to its 2022–2023 peak margins. But an investment at this price still needs profitable use of the new assets. Paying for the expansion while revenue remains around ₹2,900cr produces only **3.88%** annualized in the separate no-incremental-revenue scenario, even retaining a 21% core margin and 7× exit multiple.

FY26 also paid ₹3.24cr of preference dividends, reducing that year's selected residual to **−₹16.42cr**. Those preference shares were redeemed in the preceding year. No continuing preference claim is invented. The mechanical trailing cash proxy is only **₹6.70cr**. It includes growth investment, so it is not a fair estimate of steady-state maintenance-only earnings.

H1 operating profit before working-capital changes was **₹321.37cr**. Working capital absorbed **₹137.18cr**, including other financial assets ₹24.23cr, other assets ₹62.71cr and reduction of trade payables ₹52.04cr. Receivables released ₹20.36cr. The immediate deterioration therefore cannot be attributed simply to customers failing to pay. Inventories absorbed another ₹16.39cr. However, neither supplier advances nor other assets should be assumed to reverse fully next period.

Current H1 tax expense was **₹57.33cr**, versus cash tax ₹43.35cr. Subtracting the current tax amount, cash finance charges, reported leases and an analyst half-year maintenance estimate of ₹22.5cr from pre-working-capital operating profit gives **₹213.40cr**. That figure is a diagnostic of the productive business, not distributable cash. It excludes the real working-capital consumption and remaining growth investment. The maintenance estimate uses management's 1.5% of gross block rule and approximately ₹3,000cr of gross block, not an audited maintenance split. [S1, S3]

September borrowings are **₹564.92cr**, cash equivalents **₹0.86cr**, and leases **₹5.26cr**. The valuation starts with cash-only net borrowing of **₹564.06cr**. It does not capitalize management's lower ₹514cr net-debt figure as spare cash. The March statements identify ₹50.10cr of longer-term deposits as margin money securing guarantees and short-term borrowing. September current/noncurrent reclassification is not fully disclosed, so these deposits receive no immediate distributable-cash credit. That is a conservative valuation treatment, not an allegation that the company's reconciliation is wrong. [S1, S2, S3]

The model separately charges **₹80.90cr** of first-period cash uses: a ₹25cr derivative settlement allowance, the full new ₹28.11cr tax demand, ₹13.98cr for the H1 current-tax/cash-tax timing gap and ₹13.81cr of remaining renewable-SPV OCD subscription. The derivative number is an assumption rather than the exact September fair value. The tax demand is contested by the company. It is charged in the scenario to avoid relying on a favorable legal outcome. The SPV amount is the ₹21.25cr commitment less ₹2.13cr previously subscribed and ₹5.31cr paid in H1. The issued ₹0.13cr equity subscription is not charged twice. [S1, S2, S5]

The published term-loan instalments imply approximately **₹115cr of scheduled principal in FY28**, before any optional prepayment. The March report discloses ₹600cr of sanctioned working-capital facilities. The October CRISIL bulletin lists individual facilities including ₹82cr of proposed term funding that is still unallocated. Rated amounts and proposed facilities are not guaranteed cash available to draw. In the working scenario net borrowing peaks at **₹741.67cr**, then decreases. The downside peaks at **₹1,153.50cr**, illustrating why credit quality today does not eliminate project risk. [S2, S7, S8]

**This is modeled financeability, not verification that every required project drawdown is committed.** The working scenario's first-period net borrowing increase is ₹177.61cr. Adding approximately ₹57.65cr of scheduled term principal implies **₹235.26cr of gross new or replacement borrowing in H2 FY27**, before any minimum cash buffer. In FY28, net debt declines ₹40.42cr but roughly ₹115.28cr of principal still matures, implying **₹74.86cr of replacement borrowing**. Principal is a financing flow and is not deducted twice as an operating cost. The net-debt recurrence assumes that these cash flows can be financed, working-capital facilities remain accessible and later excess cash can repay debt. It does not establish facility eligibility, committed unrestricted drawdowns, covenant headroom or prepayment terms. Those are financing conditions for a purchase assessment.

## Capex overlap is removed

The July 27 call announced ₹600cr for epoxy resin/formulations and a multipurpose downstream plant. In that same call management confirmed total capex of approximately **₹400cr in FY27 and ₹400cr in FY28**. The November presentation shows ₹88cr already spent in H1 and ₹312cr remaining in FY27. Those are whole-company budgets. The model uses **₹312cr plus ₹400cr**, not those amounts plus another ₹600cr. Remaining renewable-SPV subscriptions are treated separately because they are financial investments rather than PPE purchases. [S3, S4]

The working scenario assumes FY29 onward annual capital expenditure of ₹75/85/90/95cr. This allows maintenance and some continued plant spending above management's mechanical 1.5% rule. It does not assume another large unannounced acquisition or expansion is free. If management begins another major program, both its spending and resulting economics must enter the model. Adding ₹150cr of extra FY28 capex without extra sales is separately calculated in the supplied JSON.

## Revenue allocation and captive transfers

The November call says approximately half the expanded ECH output will ultimately be used internally. The July call describes chlorotoluene and MPP turnover on a combined basis. Neither supplies a complete product-level external-revenue and margin bridge for the forecasts used here. [S3, S4]

The model uses an **analyst economic-contribution convention**. Core turnover includes the market-equivalent value of upstream material transferred to new downstream plants. New downstream sales are external output, and their modeled 14% margin is **after a market-equivalent feedstock transfer charge**. Under that convention the upstream contribution remains economically earned through the final product. It is not automatically deleted merely because material is consumed internally. The downstream margin does not also claim the upstream feedstock margin.

Consequently, **₹3,550cr core plus ₹1,000cr downstream is a pre-elimination gross turnover proxy, not ₹4,550cr of verified consolidated external sales**. No numerical internal-sales elimination is asserted. The fact that this sum is below management's revenue ambition does not validate the allocation or prove the model conservative. Working capital is charged using the gross growth proxy without a captive-elimination credit. That can overstate funding needs, but it cannot validate the sales bridge either. The aggregate EBITDA and funding sensitivities below expose uncertainty in this allocation without mechanically subtracting upstream profit.

## Rival supply is a central fact

Management reported CPVC utilization of **60–65%**, ECH **75–80%**, hydrogen peroxide **95%**, and caustic around **75%** in Q2. Epigral is doubling CPVC from 75,000 to 150,000 TPA and ECH from 50,000 to 100,000 TPA. Its November call acknowledged that new industry supply may take several years to absorb. [S3]

Independent issuer filings establish actual competing commercial starts:

| Competitor | Product and annual capacity | Original commercial-start disclosure |
|---|---|---|
| Grasim / Lubrizol | CPVC, approximately 50,000 tonnes | 11 August 2026 |
| Grasim | ECH, 50,000 tonnes | 21 October 2026 |
| DCM Shriram | ECH, total 52,000 tonnes | Remaining 17,000 tonnes commissioned 1 April 2026 |

These are stronger evidence than a generic assertion that demand grows at double digits. Captive chlorine, customer qualification and an integrated site can improve Epigral's economics, but competitors also operate integrated chemical businesses. Imports are not the only supply it must displace. The model therefore applies **21%** margin to the existing/expanded core and only **14%** to new epoxy/MPP revenue. These numerical margins are analyst assumptions. Management explicitly says epoxy percentage margins will be lower than the current portfolio. It does not publish the 14% used here. [S4, S9, S10, S11]

## Scenarios through 31 March 2032

The valuation has an explicit remaining H2 FY27 period followed by FY28–FY32. The last date is **5.36 years after the 18 November price**. Starting balances are from 30 September. The H2 forecast includes October to November activity that is not separately reported. This is a dated fundamental model, not an executable return reconstruction. Dividends are modeled at fiscal period endpoints for simplicity, not claimed payment dates. No probabilities, transaction costs or personal tax assumptions are supplied.

The downside terminal price is about **48% below today's reference**, before considering an interim drawdown. It is a scenario, not a floor or assigned probability. The working terminal value includes **₹1,236.31cr** of modeled retained net cash. At only 75% recognition of that cash, the working 12% present value becomes **₹907.59** and the current-price IRR becomes **11.17%**.

The model distributes only the specified ₹5 annual dividend from FY28 onward. All other cash pays down borrowing or accumulates. The terminal value adds that retained cash once. It does not pay out FCF and also count it as debt repayment. Lease cash is charged as an operating-like cost, terminal EBITDA is after lease cash, and lease debt is not subtracted a second time. No treasury interest is earned on accumulated cash in the forecasts.

Two additional mapping stresses hold capex and the other working assumptions unchanged. **10% lower aggregate EBITDA before lease cash in every forecast period** reduces the current-price annual return to **9.21%** and the 12% present value to **₹825.49**. Reducing only the new downstream margin from **14% to 10%** reduces annual return to **11.03%** and the 12% present value to **₹901.38**. Tax, interest and retained debt/cash are recalculated. These are adverse scenarios, not findings that the original model definitely double-counted a particular profit amount.

**The principal valuation weakness is exit dependence.** Approximately 98.17% of working present value is in the terminal share value because modeled dividends are small. That terminal amount includes both the enterprise value and retained cash. At unchanged operating assumptions but a 6× exit, annual return falls to **9.47%**. At 5× it falls to **6.59%**. The 7× working choice is a valuation assumption, not a known historical floor.

## Reopening routes and prospective review

**Price route:** around **₹800–875**, with the same economics and modeled financing conditions intact. A falling price after damaged margins, unavailable funding or capex escalation requires rebuilding the scenarios. The range is not an order or permanent fair value.

**Evidence route at approximately today's price:** commercial commissioning followed by disclosed profitable volume growth, cash conversion and demonstrated funding access can reduce the downside range. Useful checkpoints are recurring consolidated EBITDA near ₹650–700cr annualized before full new-downstream commissioning, improving plant utilization, FY27/FY28 cash capex tracking the ₹400cr annual envelope and borrowing remaining near the modeled ₹750cr peak rather than the stressed ₹1,150cr path. The 21% core/14% downstream split is an analyst allocation, not separately reported segment guidance. These are decision markers that must be reconciled to the company's actual disclosures.

**Highest-value next task:** evaluate the first operating update after the expanded CPVC/ECH plants enter commercial service, using volumes, selling-price/feedstock spread, collections and cash capital payments together. A commissioning press release alone will not settle the investment case.

Record the original judgment now. Review on **19 December 2026**, **17 February 2027** and **18 May 2027**, or earlier on material original disclosures. Prospective price outcomes, dividend/corporate-action clearance and economic-return fields remain null until observed. No new automation is created by this note.

## Independent-review clarification, 19 November 2026

The independent reviewer accepted the model's cash, debt, dividend and lease mechanics but identified two presentation risks: the summed core/downstream turnover had no explicit captive-elimination convention, and net debt understated the visibility of gross refinancing needs. This revision makes the gross economic-contribution convention and financing conditions explicit, removes an unsupported comparison with management's consolidated-sales ambition and adds aggregate-EBITDA/downstream-margin stresses. The original source claims about captive ECH use, combined chlorotoluene/MPP turnover and unallocated proposed funding remain preserved. **The underlying working arithmetic and WATCH_PRICE verdict are unchanged.** Its 12.05% result must be read as a working funding scenario. The independent review is preserved byte-for-byte in `epigral_independent_review.md` and `epigral_independent_review.json`, alongside `epigral_peer_review_response.json`. A further reviewer sensitivity using the full ₹25.30cr September current tax liability instead of the ₹13.98cr timing allowance gives working present value ₹944.87 at 12% and approximately 12.01% annual return. It is a reserve sensitivity, not a claim that all the liability falls due immediately.
