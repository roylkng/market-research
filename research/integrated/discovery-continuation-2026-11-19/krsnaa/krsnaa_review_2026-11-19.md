## Decision and the material change from the previous work

**WATCH_EVIDENCE_AT_CURRENT_PRICE. The current cash and funding record does not yet support promotion to BUY_CANDIDATE_FOR_POLICY_REVIEW at ₹511.30.** This is a completed dated judgment, not a request to redo the already-resolved acquisition, warrant or accounting work.

**The old note's ₹350–360 reference depended on a 15% return preference. The user has not imposed that preference.** Under the same working EPS path, values are approximately ₹447 for a 10% preference, ₹409 for 12% and ₹359 for 15%, including an illustrative ₹2 annual dividend. At the observed ₹511.30, the working path yields 7.07%. A stronger operating path yields 12.44% at the same price, so better evidence can change this decision without a price decline.

The strongest counter-thesis is that the Rajasthan start-up has already absorbed much of its cost base and the infrastructure can support additional revenue without comparable new investment. If utilization rises, old dues are collected and cash PPP/retail mix improves, the lower current profit can be a trough. Management's November call supports this explanation as an expectation. It does not establish the cash outcome. [S3]

## 3. Receivables are an older issue as well as a new-project issue

The annual-report ageing table is newly examined in this continuation. At March 2026, considered-good receivables aged **one to two years were ₹38.334cr**, versus **₹0.282cr** a year earlier. Considered-good balances older than six months totaled **₹85.717cr**, approximately **29.0%** of net receivables. This table uses invoice date, not a statement that every balance is contractually overdue. The annual report classified the balances as recoverable. It also separately provided ₹7.340cr for credit-impaired balances. [S1 p219, printed231, visually inspected]

This is not proof of a new bad debt or misconduct. It does mean the entire September increase cannot safely be explained away as initial Rajasthan invoicing. The November call itself distinguishes new-project timing from delayed government payments in several states. [S3 printed pp8–9]

September net receivables reached ₹474.556cr, versus ₹295.506cr in March. The H1 statement shows ₹181.569cr receivable cash absorption, while trade payables contributed ₹149.492cr. Management says the payable balance combines ordinary trade, project and capital items. The detail needed to normalize supplier support has not been published in the call. [S2 pp7–8. S3 printed p13]

Management's stated visibility on approximately ₹200cr of Q3 collections remains an **attributed expectation**, not independently observed receipts. It was not clearly defined as total quarterly credit collections rather than a subset of delayed dues. A proper collections check needs opening receivables, new credit billing, actual cash received, write-offs and reclassifications.

For illustration only, suppose a quarter has ₹270cr revenue, 40% collected as cash-based sales and ₹200cr total collections from credit customers. New credit billing is ₹162cr, so ending receivables would be **₹436.556cr**, not ₹274.556cr. Bringing the balance to ₹350cr under those assumptions would require **₹286.556cr total credit collections**. If the issuer's ₹200cr refers only to incremental delayed receipts, ordinary collections would be additional. The announcement does not settle that distinction.

The next result should be evaluated against the complete bridge. Gross collection headlines alone are insufficient. Supplier settlement and capital creditors also need to be observed so an apparent collection improvement is not financed by a fresh payable build.

## 6. Reopening rule and what could prove us wrong

**Highest-value next task:** obtain the next actual receivable and cash-flow bridge, including old balances, supplier payments, maintenance/growth investment, restricted cash and gross debt repayments. Repeating the November call will not answer it.

The strongest same-price route is **utilization of already-financed assets, rather than a second debt-funded build-out**. The purchase case becomes materially stronger if older dues are collected, the net receivable position and ageing improve without new supplier stretching, and utilization raises recurring per-share profit while the remaining capital programme and debt service are funded. Management says much of Rajasthan's cost base has already entered the P&L and sees approximately₹250cr annual revenue potential next year. That revenue is not all incremental to today's base, and no separate₹250cr profit contribution is added. Actual margins and net investment must support the improvement. This route can justify reconsideration at₹511.30. The current-price pass is falsified by sustained funded cash improvement, not by a daily price rally alone. [S3 printed pp7–9]

A lower entry around **₹409–447** corresponds to the retained working path at 12–10% return preferences. It is a conditional valuation reference, not an unconditional buy band. Deteriorating recoverability, new dilution, executed leaseback obligations or slower utilization require a new model. The prior ₹350–360 reference remains the roughly15%-preference version, not the only valid threshold.

The adverse thesis strengthens if old receivables are written off, collections merely repay ever-growing creditors, debt service requires repeated unplanned equity, or new sites fail to improve after the ramp-up window. The favorable thesis strengthens if existing assets produce incremental cash at low additional capital cost and the customer cash mix changes in practice.

The 18November price was **3.59% above** the earlier9November reference₹493.60. This movement went against waiting. It is retained without revising the historical judgment. Neither reference is a verified post-publication fill, and corporate-action/dividend/cost clearance was not performed for an economic-return claim. This short interval cannot establish a five-year false negative or validate the stock-selection method.
