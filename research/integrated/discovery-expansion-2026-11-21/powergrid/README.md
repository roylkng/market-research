# POWERGRID: growth can be financed, but price still matters

**Decision date: 21 November 2026. Reference price: ₹269, official NSE EQ close on 20 November, ISIN INE752E01010. Current decision: CONDITIONAL. Concrete price condition: consider BUY_AT_PRICE at ₹220 or below if the operating and financing thesis remains intact. This is a research decision, not an order or a standing instruction to buy.** [P9]

The purpose is to determine what an outside shareholder can earn from operating assets and future commissioning, after financing and dividends. The previous negative cash-after-capex observation was insufficient. A growing transmission company should borrow against productive assets. The decisive questions are what those assets earn, how quickly they begin earning, and how much of that value is already in the share price.

**Conclusion:** the modeled growth program can be funded without an assumed equity issue. The base case peaks near 1.67 times gross debt/book equity. At ₹269, however, it produces about **6.2% annualized over five years and 7.3% over ten**, including modeled dividends. At ₹220 the same economics produce **10.9% and 9.9%**. The lower entry is justified by this company's bid-return, commissioning, funding and governance uncertainty. It is not a universal 10% hurdle.

These are **bounded funding scenarios**, not a forecast supported by signed financing and tariffs for every project. No probability weights are assigned.

## 2. What is already owned, and what is still being built

At 30 September the group had **₹151,207 crore gross borrowings**, **₹106,748.38 crore book equity**, and **₹55,419 crore CWIP including construction advances**. Management showed ₹2.20 lakh crore work in hand, of which 89% was TBCB. **That work figure already includes CWIP**. Adding CWIP again would overstate future assets and financing needs. [P1][P2]

The existing regulated base and new competitively bid assets require different economics:

| Asset class | Verified framework | Modeling treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Existing regulated transmission | CERC's 2024 rules preserve a 15.5% base post-tax return for qualifying existing assets | Existing earnings are anchored to recent group profitability. No separate valuation premium or artificial profit from regulatory debt repayment is added. |
| New cost-plus transmission with COD from 1 April 2024 | Current tariff block gives 15.0% base post-tax return on recognized equity. Normative debt/equity is 70:30, subject to the regulation's actual-equity conditions | 11% of new commissioning is modeled at 30% reference equity and 15% return. Continuing this rule beyond 2029 is an explicit assumption. |
| New TBCB transmission | Competitively quoted charges. Construction cost, actual funding and contract terms determine returns | 89% of new commissioning uses explicit accounting-return sensitivities. No guaranteed 15% or 15.5% return is attached to a TBCB award. |

The CERC order directly reproduces Regulations 18, 30 and 31. Its rules concern cost-plus assets and do not prove the profitability of a competitively won project. The base RoE is not the shareholder's return on today's market price. [P8]

### Why debt-funded capex is not automatically destructive

The AGM notice describes planned FY28 capex around ₹40,000–45,000 crore and roughly ₹35,000 crore debt mobilisation, with an intended 80:20 funding/internal-resource mix. This is evidence of a financing plan, not a loan commitment for each SPV. The notice also states a ₹2.20 lakh crore authorized borrowing limit. [P4, notice pp13–14]

The base model's first-year gross debt issuance is about ₹33,132 crore including reference repayments, and second-year issuance about ₹35,766 crore. These are broadly consistent with the scale of the issuer's financing plan. The modeled debt level crosses the current ₹2.20 lakh crore authorization in year four, requiring future approvals and actual credit access. The adverse case crosses in year three. **Financeable under assumptions does not mean already financed.**

Management capex and statement cash payments are preserved separately. FY26 accrual capex was ₹39,967 crore, the historical summary's cash-capex metric was ₹33,305 crore, and the consolidated cash-flow purchase line including advances was ₹37,279.14 crore. H1 management capex of ₹16,864 crore also differs from the ₹18,912.69 crore statement purchase line. Definitions, advances and timing matter. No exact reconciliation between these alternative metrics is asserted. [P1][P2][P4]

## 4. How the funded scenarios work

The portable [calculator](calculate.py) reads [inputs](inputs.json) and writes [outputs](outputs.json). It starts with September financial balances and the 20 November entry price. The unreported 51-day balance-sheet gap is not rolled forward. Each model year is a successive future twelve-month period, not a forecast of the remaining FY27 months.

1. **Legacy earnings:** base normalized PAT begins at ₹15,000 crore, close to twice H1 FY27 PAT of ₹14,929.16 crore. Reported TTM PAT is ₹16,195.87 crore, but it includes FY26 tax and regulatory remeasurement effects. A sensitivity restores the higher number.
2. **Construction:** all-in asset spending, including construction interest, is ₹40,000, ₹42,000, ₹42,000, ₹38,000 and ₹33,000 crore in years one to five. An assumed ₹3,000 crore annual sustaining component supports existing assets. The remainder adds to CWIP.
3. **Commissioning:** base assets entering operation are ₹30,000, ₹35,000, ₹40,000, ₹42,000 and ₹40,000 crore. New cohorts contribute for half a year initially. Opening CWIP is not spent again.
4. **New earnings:** TBCB first full-year post-tax accounting RoE on an assumed original 20% equity contribution is 10%, 13% or 16%, measured at a 7% reference borrowing rate. These are assumptions, **not actual bid IRRs or lifetime project returns**. New cost-plus assets use the separate rules above.
5. **Financing:** reference project loans amortize over an assumed 15 years. TBCB retains modeled interest savings as loans amortize. Gross borrowing then closes the actual annual cash equation. Interest on financing above the reference debt schedule reduces PAT, preventing free growth financing.
6. **Cash interest:** estimated capitalized construction interest is contained within all-in capex. It is separated from expensed interest and from construction cash payments so it is not deducted twice. The FY26 actual borrowing-cost capitalization of ₹2,021.97 crore confirms that this distinction is material. [P4, consolidated note49]
7. **Owners:** base dividends are 55% of PAT, subject to an illustrative 2.0 times gross debt/book ceiling. Book equity grows only by retained profit. Shares stay at the actual 9,300,603,819. No equity issue, disposal windfall or InvIT monetisation is required by the modeled scenarios.

**Debt in the main tables is gross borrowing, not net debt.** Reported cash of ₹2,520.91 crore is held constant. Separate output fields show net debt after all cash and after only unrestricted cash. Gross debt excludes reported lease liabilities. Principal schedules and the leverage ceiling are modeling assumptions, not actual loan covenants.

Legacy PAT embeds its own existing financing and tariff recovery. The interest-rate stress reprices new TBCB loans and excess financing, but does not fully reprice every legacy fixed-rate, floating-rate or foreign-currency instrument. Detailed tax, regulatory deferrals, actual tariff escalation, finance-lease accounting and commissioning-by-SPV remain outside this aggregate model.

## 5. Humnabad: turn an award headline into an economic question

The 11 November LoI states **₹243.34 crore quoted annual transmission charges** for a new 400/220kV, 3×500MVA Humnabad substation with associated 400kV/220kV lines and bays. It is a BOOT TBCB project. The letter does not disclose the all-in construction cost, actual debt terms, tariff escalation or concession duration. **₹243.34 crore is neither profit nor current cash collection.** The 1,500 figure is transformer MVA, not a verified ₹1,500 crore cost. [P6]

The following is a reverse sensitivity, not an estimate of the award. It assumes full annual collection after operation, operating cost 1.5% of project cost, depreciation 3.5%, 80% debt at 7%, 15-year straight-line debt amortization and 25% tax. No concession term or terminal value is invented.

| Hypothetical all-in cost, ₹ crore | First operating-year PAT | Accounting RoE on 20% equity | Cash after interest, tax and principal | Illustrative debt-service cover |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1,500 | 63.25 | 21.1% | 35.75 | 1.22× |
| 1,800 | 39.40 | 10.9% | 6.40 | 1.03× |
| 2,000 | 23.50 | 5.9% | −13.16 | 0.94× |
| 2,200 | 7.60 | 1.7% | −32.73 | 0.86× |
| 2,500 | −21.66 | −4.3% | −67.49 | 0.75× |

Under precisely those assumptions, first-year accounting RoE of 13% would require all-in cost near **₹1,730 crore or less**. This is a testable reverse requirement, not a claim that actual cost is ₹1,730 crore. Sculpted debt, different operating cost, escalation, incentives or concessional funding could change the result substantially. The next useful evidence is the acquired SPV's cost/financing and transmission-service terms. Another award count does not answer it.

## 7. Ten-lens coverage and decisive checks

| Lens | Coverage | Investment consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Market and opportunity cost | MIXED | Official equity price verified. No current full-market return/rate model is asserted. At ₹269 the base return is modest for construction and bid risk. |
| Customer need and runway | SUPPORTED | Large construction program and operating network support a real demand runway. Future awards beyond existing work remain assumptions. |
| Competition and value capture | MIXED | TBCB experience supports capability. Exact winning-bid return protection remains unknown. |
| Leadership and strategic judgment | MIXED | FY26 commissioning and bulk procurement are useful evidence. FY27 guidance widened and project return detail is absent. |
| Integrity and minority alignment | ADVERSE_EVIDENCE | Independent-director/committee composition gap is disclosed. Dividend payment is confirmed. No blanket clean-governance claim. |
| Team, culture and succession | MIXED | Large installed network and high availability imply institutional delivery. Internal accountability and succession quality are not independently verified. |
| Stakeholder reputation | MIXED | Operating availability, collections and funding plan are positive issuer evidence. No invented customer or employee endorsements. |
| Capital allocation and owner economics | MIXED | Growth can be financed under scenarios. Incremental TBCB returns, sustaining spend and future debt terms remain material uncertainties. |
| Expectations and valuation | MIXED | Price, shares and returns reconcile. ₹220 is a conditional valuation response, not a target-price guarantee. |
| Catalysts, timing and portfolio fit | MIXED | Commissioning, borrowing, receivables and governance updates can change the case. Portfolio holdings, taxes and concentration were not supplied. |

**Three facts most likely to change the verdict:**

1. **Commissioning and earnings conversion.** FY27 actual capitalization relative to the original ₹30,000 crore target, subsequent CWIP movement, and recurring PAT. A growing work book with stalled capitalization would invalidate the favorable construction-to-cash mechanism.
2. **Incremental TBCB economics and funding.** Actual cost/loan/contract evidence for Humnabad and other new SPVs, gross borrowing cost and maturity access. A demonstrated return below the adverse scenario, or funding that requires materially more dilution, lowers the acceptable price.
3. **Governance and shareholder claims.** Independent-director/committee appointments, audit qualifications, share issuance, dividends and restricted cash. A price decline caused by a new integrity or dilution problem is not a buy signal.

The base case already excludes a repeated working-capital release and monetization windfall. Reassessment does not wait for perfect certainty. It requires evidence sufficient to update the few assumptions driving owner returns.
