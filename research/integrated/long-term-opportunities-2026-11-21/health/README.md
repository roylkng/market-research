# Healthcare: regional diagnostic density can compound, but price already expects it

**As of 21 November 2026. Research only.** Official reference closes are 20 November NSE EQ prices supplied by the parent research run. This note adds one bounded financial and structural case, Vijaya Diagnostic Centre, and two comparison leads. It is not a blind replay or evidence that the research process has beaten a benchmark.

## Decision

**VIJAYA, ₹1,572.30: WATCH_PRICE_AND_COHORT_EVIDENCE.** The 5–10 year business thesis is credible. The current purchase case remains demanding. Approximately ₹16,194 crore of issued equity value represents **79.5 times TTM profit**. Gross outstanding-option sensitivity moves that to approximately 80.0 times. Funding is not the central weakness. The unresolved question is whether new geographic clusters can earn attractive cash returns after equipment, leases, integration and dilution for long enough to support this price.

Do not mistake low cash remaining after a year of hub construction for a broken business. Equally, do not replace capital payback with management's statement that a center has reached operating break-even. A strong medical need, a strong company and a favorable purchase price are separate propositions.

## 1. Structural demand and where the money goes

| Observed pattern | Evidence available by the cut-off | Economic implication, an inference rather than a reported forecast |
|---|---|---|
| Population ageing | UNFPA's 2023 report projects people aged 60+ rising from about 10.5% of the population in 2022 to 20.8% in 2050 [S10] | More chronic monitoring and diagnosis is plausible. The projection neither predicts Vijaya's revenue nor proves household ability to pay. |
| Large chronic disease burden | ICMR-INDIAB estimates quoted by the Health Ministry in September 2023 were 101 million people with diabetes and 136 million with prediabetes, based on the 2021 estimates [S11] | Repeated clinically necessary testing can be more durable than a one-off wellness sale. Disease prevalence is not equivalent to addressable paid demand. |
| Payment is becoming more organized | NHA 2023–24, released **15 November 2026**, puts out-of-pocket spending at 42.7% of total health expenditure versus 64.2% in 2013–14. Private insurance's share rose 3.4% to 10.2%, social security 6% to 11% [S12] | Access can expand, but pooled payers can bargain harder. Healthcare spending growth does not automatically become provider margin growth. These are expenditure shares, not population insurance coverage. |
| Local networks can process more work on existing capacity | Vijaya reports Q2 test growth 16.5%, footfall growth 14.5% and a 92% B2C mix [S2–S3] | Trust, availability, referrals and network density can improve utilization. Capacity becomes valuable when paid volumes arrive at sustainable prices. |

The valuable node is a reliable regional collection and testing network with sufficient local density to share laboratories, doctors, equipment and logistics. Routine commodity tests can attract discounting. Advanced radiology offers differentiation, but requires expensive equipment and specialist staff. Specialized pathology can improve mix, but needs clinical credibility and referrals. AI-assisted reporting is potentially an efficiency tool. None of these observations establishes an enduring pricing premium by itself.

The payer distinction matters to the existing discovery set. **Krsnaa's public-contract receivable conversion and debt service remain different from collecting retail diagnostic fees. Medi Assist earns an administration fee, so insurance-premium growth need not translate proportionally into its revenue or profit.** The earlier Medi Assist case recorded Q2 core TPA growth of only 1.2%. Those prior-case observations are imported from the existing 19–20 November research, not independently re-underwritten here. Vijaya's direct consumer mix reduces this specific working-capital exposure. It does not eliminate price, regional, clinical or capex risk.

## 3. Vijaya: what has actually been achieved

### Operating history

The FY24 acquisition cash outflow of about ₹147.5cr is additional to that year's capex. Revenue grew about 21% annually from FY23 to FY26. That period is a recovery and expansion interval, not a justified decade forecast.

**Historical disconfirmation:** FY22 to FY23 center count rose 27%, tests rose 8%, yet revenue slipped and PAT fell about 23%. Pandemic-related mix normalization defeated a simple volume/capacity thesis. Further, the issuer changed PPE depreciation from written-down value to straight line effective 1 January 2023. It states that the change increased FY23 PAT by ₹6.77cr and FY24 PAT by ₹29.08cr [S2]. Reported EPS growth across these years overstates the improvement in underlying cash economics.

**An acquisition caution:** FY26 PH Diagnostic's disclosed subsidiary turnover was ₹47.66cr, PBT ₹0.26cr and PAT negative ₹0.77cr. Management acknowledges Pune integration took longer than anticipated. The November call reports double-digit growth and margins above 35% for the PH/Pune cluster, but that subsequent operating improvement is not a measured return on all acquisition and follow-on capital [S1, S3]. The historical acquisition outlay, subsequent capex and intra-group funding cannot be ignored because current EBITDA has recovered.

### Latest growth is not all repeatable volume

H1 FY27 revenue was ₹486.37cr and PAT ₹112.57cr. Q2 geography was Hyderabad 67%, rest of AP/Telangana 20%, Pune 5%, West Bengal 5%, others 3%. Management attributes approximately **3 percentage points of revenue growth and 2 margin points** to the prior CGHS tariff reset. Its long-term revenue discussion is closer to **15%**, primarily volume-led, than the latest 26.7% quarter [S2–S3]. FY27 EBITDA guidance was approximately **42% and above**, not a permanent 44% margin promise.

CGHS-related economics within a predominantly B2C franchise are also a reminder that B2C is a reported channel description, not proof of zero reimbursement exposure.

**Exact source correction:** signed H1 cash flow on PDF page 13 records lease principal ₹9.6106cr and finance cash ₹21.3496cr. Their sum is **₹30.9602cr**, which the iXBRL groups under lease payments. The iXBRL's **₹20.5995cr other financing item is an earmarked dividend transfer**, paid 7 October, not additional interest. Subtracting ₹30.9602cr and then ₹21.3496cr would double-count lease interest. The signed image also confirms interest income is a negative operating reconciliation adjustment. Critical signed pages 11–13 were visually inspected.

FY26 cash funded ₹182.7cr gross capex, ₹52.1cr full lease payments and approximately ₹20.5cr dividends from ₹270.6cr CFO, before treasury receipts and option exercise proceeds. CFO covered FY26 recorded cash capex, current lease payments and dividends. Funding over the new hubs’ lives still depends on future rent commitments and cohort returns. September bank borrowings were zero, but lease liabilities were **₹508.75cr**. “Debt free” would hide a material fixed obligation.

At September, investments ₹304.01cr and cash equivalents ₹10.08cr are clearly identified. Other bank balances ₹34.59cr include the dividend earmarking. Other financial assets are not assumed to be freely distributable cash. Trade receivables were only ₹17.94cr, compared with TTM revenue ₹910.96cr. That is materially different from a long public-contract collection cycle, although a single balance-sheet observation is not a cohort collection curve [S4].

### What the next retained rupee must earn

The company described eight hubs and fourteen spokes through Q1 FY28 at ₹155–160cr, including some already spent in H1. It separately described about ₹20cr for the new central laboratory, ₹9cr Kadapa land already purchased, ₹18–20cr for Pune Market Yard and about ₹19cr for the Mayur Colony property. These amounts have different timing and overlaps with booked capex. They must not simply be added to historical capex as entirely unspent commitments [S3].

**Arya Wellness adds a concrete capital test.** September's agreement is ₹46.2cr cash, subject to closing working-capital adjustments. FY26 revenue was ₹26.95cr, versus ₹25.61cr FY25 and ₹22.99cr FY24. At the latest call conditions precedent remained outstanding. The deck targets operations under Vijaya from January 2027. It quotes 8–9 times adjusted Ind AS EBITDA. That implies an initial EBITDA yield of roughly 11–12.5%, before lease cash, replacement capex, taxes and integration. It is not a free-cash-flow yield or proven acquisition IRR [S2–S3, S13].

Management reports core Khammam/Nandyal hubs broke even in two quarters, West Bengal Krishnanagar/Barasat in three and Pune Ambegaon in one year [S1]. These are useful execution observations. **Cash return by opening-year cohort is not disclosed.** The decision-relevant schedule is initial equipment/property/pre-opening cash, annual cash after rent and maintenance, incremental working capital, and cumulative cash recovered. Average pretax ROCE around 25%, calculated after excluding surplus cash, does not replace it.

## 5. People, incentives and capital allocation

Founder and chairman Dr S. Surendranath Reddy held 32.23% at March 2026. MD/CEO S. Suprita Reddy held 8.72%, executive director K. Sunil Chandra 7.56%. The operating choices emphasize regional density instead of promising simultaneous pan-India expansion. In November management explicitly discouraged modeling ten new hubs every year [S1, S3].

Alignment is mixed rather than assumed. FY26 disclosed rent to directors, their relatives and connected businesses totals about ₹16.47cr. The company states arm's-length terms, but external rent comparables were not obtained. The annual AGM notice proposed increasing the option pool by two million options. Unused authorization is not the same as granted dilution, and the resolution's final approval is not independently established in this note [S1].

Finance succession needs monitoring. FY26 accounts show a CFO transition to Ankit Shah in February 2026. A June filing announced his resignation. The latest call identifies Narasimha Raju as CFO. This establishes turnover, not misconduct. The financial review was unmodified. Stable second-line financial control and operating accountability across new regions matter more than founder reputation alone [S1, S3–S4, S14].

## 6. Price expectations, explicitly not a funded forecast

TTM PAT is ₹203.6792cr. Approximately 10.2998cr issued shares give EPS ₹19.775 and a multiple of 79.51. Latest H1 diluted EPS of ₹10.91 implies a weighted diluted denominator near 10.3185cr, not a current fully diluted count. March options outstanding 446,941, plus H1 grants 269,500, less exercises 100,987, gives 615,454 before unknown lapses. Adding these gross options without exercise-cash credit produces about 10.3613cr shares and 79.98 times TTM earnings. No exact current fully diluted count is claimed [S1, S4].

The following is arithmetic, with **no assigned probabilities and no universal hurdle**. It assumes per-share earnings growth after dilution, includes no separate treasury add-back and excludes dividends, fees and tax. High terminal P/Es are assumptions, not entitlements. It does not demonstrate that the capex required for each EPS path has been funded.

| EPS growth for ten years | Year-ten EPS | Price CAGR at 30× exit | Price CAGR at 40× exit | Price CAGR at 50× exit |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10% | ₹51.29 | -0.2% | 2.7% | 5.0% |
| 15% | ₹80.00 | 4.3% | 7.4% | 9.8% |
| 20% | ₹122.44 | 8.9% | 12.0% | 14.6% |
| 25% | ₹184.17 | 13.4% | 16.7% | 19.3% |

For a **10% price CAGR**, the current quote requires ten-year EPS growth of approximately **21.3% at a 30× exit or 17.8% at a 40× exit**. The distinction matters. A good 15% business growth path can still produce modest stock returns after a multiple contracts. Conversely, reliable 20% per-share growth for a decade could justify an attractive result. This case has not established that stronger path.

The practical next research priority is actual returns from the FY25–27 hub cohorts, plus a complete current cash valuation of Dr Lal as a less equipment-intensive comparator. There is no automatic two-quarter waiting rule. A credible cash-cohort disclosure or a material price change can alter the decision immediately.
