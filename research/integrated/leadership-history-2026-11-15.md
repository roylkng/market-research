## Integrity counterexample

The prior study used the SEC's settled 2011 Satyam case as a warning against treating prestige and apparent financial success as proof of integrity [SAT11]. Preserve the distinction between allegations and adjudicated facts, including the settlement's no-admission/no-denial status. The enforcement release was not re-opened during this persistence update. Satyam is not an industry-matched control for Titan, Costco or TSMC.

## Evidence about operating practices, not stock returns

The prior study cites the published 2013 randomized Indian-textile management intervention and its 2020 follow-up [MGMT13, MGMT20]. These examine operating performance and persistence of practices, not listed-equity returns or a universal management-quality signal. Preserve published-study versions and do not mix estimates from different working-paper revisions. The original empirical papers were not newly reproduced or reverified here.

## Seven pillars retained

1. A valuable customer problem and willingness to change the proposition.
2. Integrity when an honest choice is costly.
3. A capable team and repeatable operating practices rather than one indispensable hero.
4. Stakeholder preference that rivals cannot immediately copy.
5. Capital allocation that improves value per outside share.
6. Learning, renewal and willingness to stop weak projects.
7. A long opportunity runway with financing and operating resilience.

These are interacting research lenses, not seven predictive coefficients. Reputation must be divided among customers, employees, commercial/financing partners and investors. A prestigious investor image can raise the purchase price faster than business quality improves.

## How to use the histories on a new company

Document the behaviour and the causal hypothesis separately. Find the early indicator, identify a similar firm that failed or lost the capability, and test industry/timing/financing alternatives. Then ask what duration and cash economics the current share price requires.

Example: evidence of repeat qualification may justify investigating durable customer preference. It does not immediately justify higher revenue, margins, an extended terminal growth period AND a higher exit multiple. Use one consistent economic bridge and explicitly test the no-quality-premium case.

The next historical research task is a matched comparison for a mechanism actually decisive to a current company case. Repeating famous success stories without a counterfactual does not count as additional investment evidence.
